2 · Must remember

🧩 Four abuses = S-N-P-A: Simony (selling) · Nepotism (family) · Pluralism (plural jobs) · Absenteeism (absent). Other causes: Renaissance + printing press; Church wealth (tithes, indulgences); priests with mistresses, undereducated.

Luther's road

1483 born, Saxony→Augustinian monk, Professor of Theology, Wittenberg→1517 Ninety-five Theses (v Tetzel)→John Eck debate→Burns papal bull → 1521 excommunicated→Diet of Worms → outlaw→Wartburg Castle: Bible into German→1546 dies→1555 Peace of Augsburg

Luther's beliefs: faith alone · indulgences don't save · Mass and Bible in the vernacular · Bible the only guide · clergy may marry · 2 sacraments (baptism, communion) · consubstantiation.

⚠️ Don't lose marks

  • Consubstantiation (“with”) = Luther; transubstantiation (“transforms”) = Catholic.
  • The Counter-Reformation stopped further spread of Protestantism but could not reverse it.

🏰 Ch 9 · The Plantations ⏱ 12 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
The PaleDublin and its surrounding areas: the only part of Ireland under English control in the 1500s.
Anglo-IrishDescendants of the Anglo-Normans, independent of the Crown, who adopted Gaelic laws and traditions.
Gaelic IrishChieftains who followed Brehon Law.
Act of SupremacyHenry VIII made himself head of the Church of England.
Surrender and regrantHenry VIII's first, peaceful policy to control Ireland.
PlantationConfiscating land and settling it with English (and later Scottish) planters.
PlantersSettlers brought in to farm confiscated land (undertakers, servitors and loyal Irish in Ulster).
AdventurersSent by Elizabeth I to claim land in Munster and spread English law, language and religion.
Protestant AscendancyThe wealthy Protestant landlord class that owned 85% of land by 1700.
Penal LawsLaws that discriminated against Catholics.
LondonderryName given to Derry by the London guilds who worked there.

2 · Must remember

Ireland in the 1500s, three groups: Pale (loyal) · Anglo-Irish (went Gaelic) · Gaelic Irish (Brehon Law).

Tudors: Henry VII first Tudor → Henry VIII breaks from Rome to divorce Catherine of Aragon (no male heir), marries Anne Boleyn. Children: Mary (Catherine), Elizabeth (Anne Boleyn), Edward (Jane Seymour).

🧩 Reasons = L-L-R-P-A-M: Land beyond the Pale · Laws, customs, culture · stop Rebellions · spread Protestantism · stop Alliances with Catholic countries · save Money.
PlantationGaelic familyMonarchResult
Laois-OffalyO'Moores & O'Connors (raided the Pale)Mary IFailure: too few planters, Gaelic planters given land, lands attacked → better planning
MunsterFitzgeralds of DesmondElizabeth IPartial success: ~4,000 planters (wanted 20,000), still rented to Gaelic Irish; new towns, trades, farming
Ulster ★O'Neills of Tyrone & O'Donnells of DonegalJames IMost successful: smaller estates, stricter rules, Derry for craft guilds; many English & Scottish settlers; rebellions persisted, tensions to this day

Identity: most people stayed Catholic, but by 1700 Protestants owned 85% of land · Penal Laws · Gaelic laws, language, culture, farming declined · anger, distrust, violence.

Towns: medieval Dublin (Norman centre, port, unsanitary) · plantation towns Killarney, Portlaoise (courthouse, gaol, Protestant church) · later market towns grew with canals and railways (Mullingar, Ballina).

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Henry VII was the first Tudor; Henry VIII broke from the Church.
  • Match monarch → plantation: Mary → Laois-Offaly, Elizabeth → Munster, James I → Ulster.
  • Only Ulster was “most successful”: Laois-Offaly failed, Munster only partial. Quote 4,000 v 20,000.

🗽 Ch 10 · The American Revolution ⏱ 12 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
ColonyTerritory ruled by another country; Britain had 13 in North America, each with a governor.
EnlightenmentMovement that valued reason/science over faith or traditional authority.
Navigation ActsForced the colonies to sell some products only to Britain, so Britain controlled the price.
Stamp ActRequired all legal documents to have a government stamp.
No Taxation Without RepresentationSlogan: colonists had no representatives in the London parliament that taxed them.
Sons of LibertyGroup that advocated for Americans to govern themselves.
Boston Massacre1770: British soldiers fired on a mob protesting the Townshend Acts, killing five.
Boston Tea Party1773: Sons of Liberty, dressed as Native Americans, dumped 342 crates of tea into Boston Harbour.
Intolerable ActsBritish punishment: Boston port closed and city put under military rule.
Declaration of IndependenceIssued 4 July 1776 to separate the colonies from British rule.
Valley ForgeWinter 1777–78 camp where Washington's men regrouped in terrible conditions.
Treaty of Paris1783 treaty between Britain and the former colonies, ending the war.
Federal republicForm of government set up by the US constitution (Constitutional Convention, chaired by Washington).

2 · Must remember

🧩 Causes = E-R-S-T-S: Enlightenment · Raw materials (Navigation Acts) · Seven Years' War costs · Taxes (Stamp, Quartering) · Sons of Liberty.

Road to war

Townshend Acts→1770 Boston Massacre (5 killed)→1773 Tea Act → Tea Party→Intolerable Acts→1774 First Continental Congress→1775 Lexington & Concord→Paine's pamphlet→1776 Second Congress: Washington C-in-C; Declaration 4 July

The war: Americans 20,000 part-time v British 80,000 professionals, but British thousands of miles from home; Americans on home turf.

Bunker Hill 1775 (lost)→New York (lost)→Trenton & Princeton (won)→1777 Howe takes Philadelphia→Saratoga: Gates wins → France, Spain, Netherlands join→Valley Forge 1777–78: 3000 die; Von Steuben drills→1781 Yorktown: Cornwallis surrenders→1783 Treaty of Paris
PersonRemember for
Paul RevereWarned the rebels/militiamen (1775)
Thomas PainePamphlet arguing for independence
Horatio GatesWon Saratoga
Baron Von SteubenDrilled the men at Valley Forge
CornwallisBritish general who surrendered at Yorktown

Results: independence · constitution → federal republic · inspired the French Revolution and Ireland's 1798 Rebellion · “all men are created equal” limited to white men (women, slaves, indigenous Americans left out).

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Boston Massacre 1770 (soldiers shot five) ≠ Boston Tea Party 1773 (tea dumped).
  • Saratoga was won by Gates, not Washington; Philadelphia was lost to Howe.
  • The Second Continental Congress (1776) chose Washington and declared independence, not the First (1774).

🏁 Final mixed quiz ⏱ 6 min

12 questions across all three chapters. Anything you miss: jump back to that chapter's key-terms table.

Giving Church positions to family and friends is called…

  1. Simony
  2. Pluralism
  3. Nepotism
  4. Absenteeism
Nepotism = family. Simony = buying/selling; pluralism = more than one; absenteeism = absent.

Who issued the Edict of Worms, making Luther an outlaw?

  1. Pope Leo X
  2. John Eck
  3. Charles V
  4. Prince Frederick of Saxony
Charles V. The Pope excommunicated Luther; Frederick hid him in Wartburg Castle.

Which belief about bread and wine did Luther hold?

  1. Transubstantiation
  2. Consubstantiation
  3. Predestination
  4. Simony
Consubstantiation (side by side). Transubstantiation is the Catholic belief.

Which art movement grew from lavishly decorated Catholic churches?

  1. Baroque
  2. Renaissance
  3. Gothic
  4. Norman
Protestants kept churches plain; Catholic decoration led to Baroque.

Henry VIII's first policy to control Ireland was…

  1. Plantation
  2. Penal Laws
  3. Surrender and regrant
  4. Act of Supremacy
He first tried peaceful means: surrender and regrant.

Which plantation was a failure?

  1. Laois-Offaly
  2. Munster
  3. Ulster
  4. Derry
Laois-Offaly (Mary I): too few planters and lands still attacked.

Which monarch organised the Munster plantation?

  1. Mary I
  2. Elizabeth I
  3. James I
  4. Henry VII
Elizabeth I, against the Fitzgeralds of Desmond.

By 1700, Protestants owned what share of the land?

  1. 20%
  2. 85%
  3. 50%
  4. 40%
85%: this landlord class was the Protestant Ascendancy.

In what year did the Boston Massacre happen?

  1. 1770
  2. 1773
  3. 1774
  4. 1776
1770. Tea Party 1773, First Continental Congress 1774, Declaration 1776.

Who wrote a pamphlet arguing for American independence?

  1. Paul Revere
  2. Thomas Paine
  3. Horatio Gates
  4. Baron Von Steuben
Thomas Paine. Revere warned the rebels; Gates won Saratoga; Von Steuben drilled troops.

Which victory brought France, Spain and the Netherlands into the war?

  1. Valley Forge
  2. Saratoga
  3. Bunker Hill
  4. Concord
General Horatio Gates won at Saratoga; French help was key to victory.

Which Irish event was inspired by the American Revolution?

  1. The 1798 Rebellion
  2. The Penal Laws
  3. The Plantation of Ulster
  4. The Peace of Augsburg
Ireland's dream of independence led to the 1798 Rebellion.
