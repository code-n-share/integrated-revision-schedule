🔥 1. Causes of the American Revolution

From the 1600s Britain had established 13 colonies in North America, each with a governor representing the Crown. By 1750 the population had grown to 2 million and become prosperous through trade. Various factors made the colonies unhappy with British rule:

  • The Enlightenment (movement valuing reason/science over faith or traditional authority) caused people to challenge the idea that rulers had a divine right to rule in any way they saw fit.
  • Britain wanted a cheap supply of raw materials for its industries but did not want American competition – they wanted the colonies as a market to sell finished products. The Navigation Acts forced the colonies to sell some products only to Britain, so Britain could control the price, leading to smuggling and clashes with the British Navy.
  • Many British soldiers fought in the Seven Years’ War. Britain wanted the colonies to contribute to the cost of the army. Many Americans also gained military experience.
  • The Stamp Act required all legal documents to have a government stamp. The Quartering Act imposed taxes to cover the cost of soldiers. Colonists resented taxes being forced upon them by a London parliament where they had no representatives. Opposition groups used the slogan ‘No Taxation Without Representation’. ‘Stamp men’ were beaten, ‘tarred and feathered’ and the Stamp Act was withdrawn.
  • Groups like the Sons of Liberty advocated for Americans to govern themselves.
🧩 Remember it: Causes: E-R-S-T-S – Enlightenment, Raw materials (Navigation Acts), Seven Years’ War costs, Taxes (Stamp/Quartering), Sons of Liberty.
🛣️ 2. The Road to War
  • Britain imposed the Townshend Acts (taxes on imported goods). In 1770 a Boston mob protesting confronted British soldiers, who opened fire, killing five people in the Boston Massacre. Britain withdrew the Acts but kept a tea tax.
  • 1773: the Tea Act exempted the East India Company from paying the tea tax. Some of the Sons of Liberty, dressed as Native Americans, dumped 342 crates of tea into Boston Harbour: the Boston Tea Party.
  • Britain imposed the ‘intolerable acts’ – Boston port was closed and the city put under military rule, which worried the other colonies.
  • 1774: representatives met at the First Continental Congress. They opposed all British taxes, urged a boycott of British goods and demanded the removal of British soldiers. Secret groups stockpiled weapons and militias trained/armed themselves, even though most weren’t willing to go to war yet.
  • 1775: British General Gage sent troops to seize weapons at Concord. Paul Revere organised militiamen to warn the rebels. On the way to Concord, a small American force engaged the British at Lexington: 10 Americans were killed, 8 captured. At Concord the weapons were gone; on the way back, a larger battle left 273 British dead.
  • The violence convinced many that war was inevitable. Thomas Paine wrote a pamphlet arguing that the American people should be independent of Britain. The Second Continental Congress met in 1776 and chose George Washington as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army. On 4 July the Declaration of Independence was issued to separate the colonies from British rule (also in 1776).
⚠️ Common mistake: The Boston Massacre (1770, soldiers shot five) and the Boston Tea Party (1773, tea dumped) are different events three years apart.
⚔️ 3. The American War of Independence
  • Washington faced a daunting situation. The American Army was poorly trained and equipped part-time soldiers with problems of indiscipline and disease and numbered only 20,000 to the British Army’s 80,000. The British were professional full-time soldiers, well trained and equipped, with a large navy, and treated all Americans as enemies and punished civilians for helping rebels, increasing support for them.
  • However, the British were thousands of miles from home, fighting in unfamiliar territory; the Americans were on home turf and had local knowledge.
  • Fighting broke out in 1775. Washington lost his first battle at Bunker Hill despite heavy casualties. He was defeated at New York but victorious at Princeton and Trenton. In 1777, British General Howe captured Philadelphia, a huge blow. Many began to doubt Washington’s leadership.
  • A month later, American General Horatio Gates defeated the British at Saratoga. This reassured the American people, and some of Britain’s European rivals became interested. France, Spain and the Netherlands joined the war. French help was particularly key to the colonies’ victory.
  • Washington and his men regrouped at Valley Forge during the winter of 1777–1778, one of the harshest of the century: miserable, hunger and disease rife, 3000 men died and hundreds more deserted. Washington won loyalty by refusing to abandon them. Prussian soldier Baron Von Steuben drilled the men so they were much better trained/disciplined.
  • Over the next few years the balance turned in favour of the Americans. In the battle at Yorktown in 1781, the British under General Cornwallis were surrounded by French and Americans. Cornwallis surrendered to Washington, marking the end of the War of Independence. The Treaty of Paris was signed between Britain and the former colonies in 1783.
🧩 Remember it: Washington’s war: Bunker Hill (lost) → New York (lost) → Trenton/Princeton (won) → Saratoga (Gates won; France joins) → Valley Forge (train) → Yorktown 1781 (win).
⚠️ Common mistake: Saratoga was won by General Gates, not Washington; Philadelphia was lost to General Howe in 1777.
🏁 4. Results
  • The most significant was the independence of the colonies. Conflicts arose and a Constitutional Convention, chaired by George Washington, was called to prevent this from happening again. It wrote a constitution for the United States of America, which established a federal republic. Over the next 200 years, the USA grew to 50 states. By 1900 it was one of the most powerful countries on earth and millions of immigrants made it their home.
  • News of the American War of Independence spread quickly. In France, the rule of King Louis XVI was beginning to collapse. The French looked to America for inspiration, paving the way for the French Revolution.
  • Ireland had been under British rule since the 1100s. Like America, they also dreamed of an independent Ireland, leading to the 1798 Rebellion.
  • The Declaration of Independence states that ‘all men are created equal’ and promotes ‘life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness’ but these ideals were limited to white men when the USA was founded. Women were treated as second-class citizens, black people were kept as slaves and indigenous Americans were almost wiped out. However, the ideals inspired all these groups and their supporters to keep fighting for their equal rights (slavery was abolished after the Civil War [though racism continued] and women campaigned for the right to vote and greater equality after WW2).

✏️ Practice Questions

Try answering in full sentences first, then click a question to see the model answer.

Describe two causes of the American Revolution.
1) Taxation without representation – the Stamp Act and Quartering Act imposed taxes by a London parliament where colonists had no representatives. 2) Trade restrictions – Navigation Acts forced colonies to sell some products only to Britain. (Also: Enlightenment ideas, cost of the Seven Years’ War, the Sons of Liberty.)
Explain how events from 1770 to 1775 led towards war.
Townshend Acts → Boston Massacre (1770, five killed) → Tea Act and Boston Tea Party (1773) → Intolerable Acts (Boston port closed, military rule) → First Continental Congress (1774) and militias arming → British troops sent to seize weapons at Concord; fighting at Lexington and Concord (1775).
Why was Washington’s task so difficult at the start of the war?
The American army was poorly trained and equipped part-time soldiers with problems of indiscipline and disease, only 20,000 against 80,000 British professional full-time soldiers with a large navy. He lost at Bunker Hill and New York, and Philadelphia was captured in 1777.
Explain two reasons why the Americans won the war.
French (and Spanish/Dutch) help after the American victory at Saratoga – French help was particularly key; Washington’s leadership and the training by Von Steuben at Valley Forge; the British were thousands of miles from home while the Americans were on home turf with local knowledge. Cornwallis was surrounded by French and Americans at Yorktown (1781).
Describe two results of the American Revolution.
Independence of the colonies and a constitution establishing a federal republic (USA grew to 50 states and was a major power by 1900). It inspired the French Revolution and Ireland’s 1798 Rebellion, and its ideals later inspired women, black people and indigenous Americans to fight for equal rights.
