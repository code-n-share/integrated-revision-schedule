🔥 1. Causes of the Reformation
Influence of the Renaissance: People learned to read and the printing press made books more available.
Wealth of the Church: The Church grew rich through tithes (a 10% tax) and the sale of indulgences (prayers to reduce time in Purgatory).

Corruption in the Church:

  • Simony – buying/selling Church positions
  • Nepotism – giving positions to family/friends
  • Pluralism – holding more than one Church position
  • Absenteeism – being absent from Church duties
  • Many priests had mistresses, fathered illegitimate children and/or were undereducated
🧩 Remember it: S-N-P-A = Simony (selling), Nepotism (nephews/family), Pluralism (plural jobs), Absenteeism (absent).
⚠️ Common mistake: Mixing up the four abuses – match each to its key word: buying/selling, family, more than one, absent.
👤 2. Martin Luther
  • Born in Saxony, Germany in 1483. Studied law, but became an Augustinian monk and then a Professor of Theology at the University of Wittenberg.
  • The Catholic Church taught that faith in God and good works were needed to get into heaven; Luther believed in “Justification by faith alone”.
  • 1517: in response to John Tetzel selling indulgences in Wittenberg, Luther wrote ninety-five theses and allegedly nailed them to the door of a Wittenberg church. They spread all over Germany thanks to the printing press.
  • Pope Leo X sent the theologian John Eck to debate Luther in public. Luther claimed the Pope had no more power to interpret the Bible than anyone else.
  • Leo sent a papal bull threatening Luther with excommunication. Luther burned it and was excommunicated in 1521.
  • That year Charles V, ruler of Germany, called the Diet of Worms to discuss Luther’s beliefs. Luther refused to change his mind, so Charles issued the Edict of Worms, making Luther an outlaw.
  • Luther’s followers protested and became known as “Protestants”.
  • Prince Frederick of Saxony hid Luther in Wartburg Castle for a year. There he translated the Bible into German.
  • Luther married a former nun, Katharina von Bora; they had six children. He died in 1546.
  • War broke out after his death until the Peace of Augsburg (1555): each ruler could choose the religion for his own state.
🧩 Remember it: Luther’s road: 1517 Theses → 1521 Excommunicated + Worms → hidden in Wartburg → dies 1546 → Augsburg 1555.
⚠️ Common mistake: The Pope and Charles V did different things: the Pope excommunicated Luther; Charles V (the ruler) made him an outlaw at the Diet of Worms.
📜 3. Luther’s Key Beliefs
  • Justification by faith alone – sins cannot be forgiven by the Pope or through good works
  • Indulgences do not save people from Purgatory
  • Mass and the Bible should be in the vernacular
  • The Bible is the only source of guidance for Christians – not the Pope or the Church
  • Clergy should be allowed to marry
  • Only two sacraments, not seven: baptism and communion
  • Consubstantiation (bread and wine and body and blood of Christ exist side by side), not transubstantiation (bread and wine become body and blood of Christ)
🧩 Remember it: Luther said “Con = with”: Con-substantiation = the bread is with Christ’s body. Trans-substantiation = bread transforms into it.
⚠️ Common mistake: Consubstantiation is Luther’s (Protestant) belief; transubstantiation is the Catholic one.
🌍 4. The Spread of the Reformation

The Reformation spread quickly throughout Europe.

  • 1531: Ulrich Zwingli launched the Reformation in Switzerland.
  • Ten years later John Calvin was invited to Geneva, Switzerland to set up his own version of Protestantism – Presbyterianism (different to Lutheranism; Calvinists believed in predestination).
  • 1534: King Henry VIII set up the Church of England with himself as the head.
  • 1572: John Knox introduced Presbyterianism to Scotland.
⚔️ 5. The Catholic Counter-Reformation

The Catholic Church’s response to Protestantism.

  • Courts of Inquisition tried, arrested and punished anyone seen as a threat to Church teachings. Punishments: imprisonment, torture, fines, confiscation of property and burning at the stake (auto-da-fé). Heretics also had to wear a yellow cloak (sanbenito) in public.
  • New religious orders: the Ursuline nuns and Capuchin monks.
  • The Jesuits, founded by Ignatius Loyola in 1534, were like an army. They travelled the world as missionaries and set up schools to teach the sons of wealthy, noble families.
  • The Council of Trent imposed discipline by banning simony, nepotism, pluralism, absenteeism and indulgences; punishing priests who were not celibate; and setting up seminaries to train priests.
  • A single rulebook for Catholicism: the Catechism.
Outcome: The Counter-Reformation was successful in preventing the further spread of Protestantism but could not reverse it in countries where it had already spread.
🏁 6. The Results of the Reformation
  • By 1650 Europe was divided into a largely Protestant north and Catholic south. Minority groups in these countries faced persecution and discrimination: Protestants were tried by the Inquisition in Catholic countries, and the same happened to Catholics in Protestant countries.
  • Religious wars broke out, including the Thirty Years’ War (1618–1648) between Catholic and Protestant states, ended by the Peace of Westphalia, which reinforced the earlier Peace of Augsburg.
  • The Church’s power was much reduced: Church property in Protestant countries was seized by the rulers, and even Catholic countries needed the ruler’s support to stay Catholic – the Pope had very little control.
  • Education became very important as both sides believed it was necessary to keep faith strong, leading to an overall increase in education for the people of Europe from the 1500s onwards.
  • Protestants kept their churches simple and plain; Catholics continued to lavishly decorate theirs. This led to the Baroque art movement.

Describe two causes of the Reformation.
1) Wealth of the Church – it raised money through tithes (10% tax) and selling indulgences (prayers to reduce time in Purgatory). 2) Corruption – simony, nepotism, pluralism and absenteeism; many priests had mistresses and were undereducated. (Also: the Renaissance and the printing press helped people read for themselves.)
Explain what happened to Martin Luther between 1517 and 1521.
In 1517 he wrote the ninety-five theses in response to Tetzel selling indulgences; they spread thanks to the printing press. Pope Leo X sent John Eck to debate him, then a papal bull threatening excommunication. Luther burned it and was excommunicated in 1521. Charles V called the Diet of Worms; Luther refused to change his mind so the Edict of Worms made him an outlaw.
List four of Luther’s key beliefs.

How did the Catholic Church respond to the Reformation? (Counter-Reformation)
