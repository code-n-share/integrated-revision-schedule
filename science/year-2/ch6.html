🔄 The five stages of human nutrition
The digestive system allows the body to access nutrients from food, which are needed for energy and growth. There are five stages of human nutrition.
💡 Think of food as a parcel: take it in, unwrap it, deliver it, use it, bin the packaging.
1 Ingestion→2 Digestion→3 Absorption→4 Assimilation→5 Egestion
StageWhat happens
1. IngestionFood is taken into the mouth
2. DigestionFood is broken down
3. AbsorptionDigested food is passed into the blood and carried to cells
4. AssimilationDigested food is used by the body for energy and to build new cells
5. EgestionUndigested food is released as waste
🧩 Remember it: In, Down, Across, Add, Exit – Ingestion, Digestion, Absorption, Assimilation, Egestion (in the order food travels).
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up absorption (digested food goes into the blood) and assimilation (cells use it). Also: egestion is undigested food leaving – not waste made by cells.
Two types of digestion:
  • Physical digestion: food is broken down into smaller pieces by the teeth, tongue and churning action of the muscles in the stomach.
  • Chemical digestion: food is broken down further by digestive enzymes and other digestive juices in the stomach.
💡 Physical = chopping the vegetables. Chemical = the cooking that changes what they are made of.
Enzyme: a protein that speeds up the rate of chemical reactions in cells and is not used up in the process. In the digestive system, enzymes break down the chemicals in food into simpler forms so they are small enough to be used by the body.
💡 An enzyme is like a pair of scissors: it cuts lots of strings and is still a pair of scissors at the end.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying an enzyme is “used up”. It speeds up the reaction and is not used up.
👄 The mouth
  • Food is ingested through the mouth.
  • The teeth physically digest food; the enzymes in saliva chemically digest it.
  • Food is then swallowed.
Type of toothDescriptionFunction
IncisorsNarrow, sharp teeth at the front of the mouth, similar to chiselsBiting, cutting and slicing food
CaninesPointed teeth, often called ‘eye teeth’Grasping and tearing food
PremolarsLarge, rounded teethCrushing and grinding food
MolarsLargest teeth, found at the back of the mouthCrushing and grinding food
🧩 Remember it: front to back = Incisors (cut) → Canines (tear) → Premolars + Molars (crush and grind). Premolars and molars share the same job.
Saliva and amylase: salivary glands secrete saliva onto the food. Saliva softens the food. The enzyme amylase in the saliva chemically digests starch by breaking it down into the sugar maltose.
💡 Chew a piece of bread for a while: the starch is already starting to be broken down into sugar in your mouth.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying the mouth only does physical digestion. Saliva’s amylase means chemical digestion starts in the mouth.
⬇️ Oesophagus and stomach
Oesophagus (food pipe): swallowed food is pushed along by a wave-like movement of muscles called peristalsis. This continues the physical digestion of food.
💡 Squeezing toothpaste from the bottom of the tube: a squeeze moving along pushes the paste forward.
🎯 Concentrate!
Fibre in the diet is important for peristalsis and good digestive health.
Stomach: an expandable muscular bag that physically churns and temporarily stores food.
  • It releases juices that continue the chemical digestion of food.
  • Hydrochloric acid in the stomach chemically digests food and kills bacteria in food.
💡 The stomach is a stretchy, muscly blender with a built-in cleaning acid.
⚠️ Common mistake: forgetting the stomach does both kinds of digestion: churning (physical) and acid + juices (chemical).
🌀 Small intestine
  • Food from the stomach enters the small intestine.
  • The enzyme maltase breaks down maltose into a smaller sugar called glucose. This completes the breakdown of food into a form that can be absorbed into the bloodstream and cells.
  • The small intestine is densely covered in villi. The villi provide an increased surface area to allow the nutrients from fully digested food to be absorbed into the bloodstream.
Starch—amylase (mouth)→Maltose—maltase (small intestine)→Glucose
💡 Villi are like the loops of a towel: lots of tiny folds mean far more surface to soak things up.
⚠️ Common mistake: writing “villi absorb food” without the reason. The mark is for increased surface area.
🧪 Pancreas, liver and large intestine
OrganWhat it does
PancreasWhere the digestive enzymes that pass into the small intestine are produced.
LiverLargest internal organ in the body. Breaks down harmful substances such as alcohol and keeps body temperature at 37 °C. Produces bile, which passes into the small intestine where it helps digest fats.
Large intestineA long, tube-like organ. Its major function is to reabsorb water from fully digested food into the blood. It also prepares undigested food and waste matter for egestion as faeces, which are temporarily stored in the rectum before being passed out through the anus.
💡 Food never travels through the pancreas or liver. They are helpers: they make the chemicals (enzymes, bile) that are sent to the small intestine.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying food passes through the liver or pancreas, or that the large intestine digests food. It reabsorbs water.
🔬 Experiment 6.1: Does amylase break down starch?
🔬 Experimental Investigation 6.1

Equipment: water bath, thermometer, beakers, starch solution, pH 7 buffer solution, amylase solution, water, 2 test tubes, test tube rack, 3 droppers, white dropping plate, iodine solution. Safety: be careful when using the heated water bath.

  1. Set up a 37 °C water bath. Warm beakers of starch, pH 7 buffer, amylase and water in it for 5 minutes.
  2. Label two test tubes “Water” and “Amylase”.
  3. Add 2 cm³ starch solution and 1 cm³ pH 7 buffer to both tubes.
  4. Add 2 cm³ water to the “Water” tube and 2 cm³ amylase to the “Amylase” tube.
  5. Swirl, then stand both tubes in the 37 °C water bath for 15 minutes.
  6. With separate droppers, put a drop from each tube on a labelled white dropping plate and add one drop of iodine to each. Record observations.
💡 The “Water” tube is the comparison (control): it is the same in every way except there is no amylase. 37 °C is human body temperature.
⚠️ Common mistake: forgetting why there are two tubes. One has water instead of amylase so you can compare and show it is the amylase that makes the difference.

❓ Quick Quiz

Digested food passes into the blood and is carried to cells. Which stage of nutrition is this?

  1. Ingestion
  2. Assimilation
  3. Absorption
  4. Egestion
Absorption is digested food passing into the blood. Assimilation is the next stage – the cells using the food for energy and to build new cells.

Churning of food by the muscles of the stomach is an example of…

  1. physical digestion
  2. chemical digestion
  3. absorption
  4. egestion
Breaking food into smaller pieces by teeth, tongue and the stomach muscles is physical digestion. Chemical digestion needs enzymes and juices.

What does the enzyme amylase in saliva break starch down into?

  1. Glucose
  2. Bile
  3. Amylose
  4. Maltose
Amylase breaks starch into maltose. It is maltase, later in the small intestine, that turns maltose into glucose.

Which enzyme completes the breakdown of food in the small intestine by making glucose?

  1. Amylase
  2. Maltase
  3. Hydrochloric acid
  4. Bile
Maltase breaks maltose down into glucose. Amylase acts earlier (in the mouth) on starch.

What is the job of the villi in the small intestine?

  1. Make bile to digest fats
  2. Squeeze food along by peristalsis
  3. Increase the surface area for absorption of nutrients
  4. Kill bacteria in food
Villi increase the surface area so nutrients from fully digested food can be absorbed into the bloodstream.

Why is hydrochloric acid found in the stomach?

  1. It chemically digests food and kills bacteria in food
  2. It reabsorbs water into the blood
  3. It pushes food along the oesophagus
  4. It makes bile for fat digestion
Hydrochloric acid does two jobs: chemical digestion and killing bacteria.

What is peristalsis?

  1. The breakdown of starch by saliva
  2. The absorption of water in the large intestine
  3. The churning of food in the stomach
  4. A wave-like movement of muscles that pushes food along the oesophagus
Peristalsis is the wave-like muscle movement along the oesophagus. Churning belongs to the stomach.

Which type of tooth is used for grasping and tearing food?

  1. Incisor
  2. Canine
  3. Premolar
  4. Molar
Canines are pointed ‘eye teeth’ for grasping and tearing. Incisors cut and slice; premolars and molars crush and grind.

What is the major function of the large intestine?

  1. To produce digestive enzymes
  2. To complete the breakdown of maltose
  3. To reabsorb water from fully digested food into the blood
  4. To store food temporarily and churn it
The large intestine reabsorbs water and prepares waste for egestion as faeces. The pancreas makes enzymes; the small intestine finishes digestion; the stomach stores and churns.

Which statement about bile is correct?

  1. It is made by the pancreas and digests starch
  2. It is made by the liver and helps digest fats in the small intestine
  3. It is made by the stomach and kills bacteria
  4. It is made by the small intestine and absorbs water
The liver produces bile, which passes into the small intestine to help digest fats. The pancreas makes digestive enzymes.

Which statement about an enzyme is correct?

  1. It is a protein that speeds up reactions and is not used up
  2. It is a sugar that is used up in the reaction
  3. It is a protein that is used up to make glucose
  4. It is an acid that kills bacteria
An enzyme is a protein that speeds up the rate of a reaction and is not used up in the process.

1. Explain the difference between physical and chemical digestion. (Summary Q3)
  • Physical digestion: food is broken down into smaller pieces by the teeth, tongue and churning action of the muscles in the stomach.
  • Chemical digestion: food is broken down further by digestive enzymes and other digestive juices (e.g. amylase in saliva, acid in the stomach).

4. Distinguish between the roles of the small intestine and the large intestine. (Summary Q7) [4 marks]

Three liver functions: (1) breaks down harmful substances such as alcohol; (2) keeps body temperature at 37 °C; (3) produces bile.
