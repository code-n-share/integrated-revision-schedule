🍽️ Ch 6 · The Digestive System ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
IngestionFood is taken into the mouth.
DigestionBreaking food down into smaller pieces / simpler chemicals (physical and chemical).
AbsorptionDigested food is passed into the blood and carried to cells.
AssimilationDigested food is used by the body for energy and to build new cells.
EgestionUndigested food is released as waste.
EnzymeA protein that speeds up the rate of chemical reactions and is not used up.
AmylaseEnzyme in saliva: starch → maltose.
MaltaseEnzyme in the small intestine: maltose → glucose.
PeristalsisWave-like movement of muscles that pushes food along the oesophagus.
VilliTiny projections in the small intestine that give a large surface area for absorption.
BileMade by the liver; helps digest fats.

2 · Must remember

Ingestion→Digestion→Absorption→Assimilation→Egestion
  • Physical digestion: teeth, tongue, stomach churning. Chemical: enzymes and juices. The stomach does both.
  • Stomach acid (hydrochloric) digests food and kills bacteria.
  • Pancreas makes enzymes; liver makes bile, breaks down harmful substances, keeps 37 °C.
  • Large intestine reabsorbs water; faeces stored in the rectum, leave via the anus.
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • An enzyme is not used up.
  • Absorption = into the blood; assimilation = cells use it.
  • Villi: say “increased surface area”. Food doesn't pass through the pancreas or liver.

3 · Quick check answer before you flip

Absorption v assimilation?
Absorption: into the blood. Assimilation: body uses it for energy / new cells.
Define an enzyme
A protein that speeds up chemical reactions and is not used up
What does amylase do, and where?
In saliva: breaks starch into maltose
How are villi suited to absorption?
They give a large surface area
What is peristalsis?
Wave-like muscle movement pushing food along the oesophagus
❤️ Ch 7 · The Circulatory System ⏱ 8 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
PlasmaLight yellow liquid, mostly water, carrying dissolved substances, waste and heat.
Red blood cellsTransport oxygen to cells so respiration can take place.
HaemoglobinRed pigment in red blood cells; contains iron and picks up oxygen.
White blood cellsFight harmful micro-organisms or make antibodies.
PlateletsFragments of larger cells that help blood clot.
ArteriesCarry blood away from the heart; thick muscle walls.
VeinsCarry blood to the heart; thinner walls and valves.
CapillariesLink arteries and veins; walls one cell thick.
Atria / ventriclesAtria = upper chambers; ventricles = lower chambers.
SeptumWall separating the left and right sides of the heart.
PulseA wave of vibration through an artery when blood is forced out of the heart.

2 · Must remember

Vena cava→R atrium→R ventricle→Pulmonary artery→Lungs→Pulmonary vein→L atrium→L ventricle→Aorta→Body

Blue = deoxygenated (low O₂, high CO₂) · red = oxygenated.

  • 3 functions of blood: transport, protect, regulate temperature.
  • Plasma = 55% of blood. Resting pulse 60–100 bpm (count 30 s, ×2).
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood; pulmonary vein oxygenated: the exceptions.
  • Platelets are fragments, not cells.
  • Arteries: thick walls because of high pressure; veins have valves because pressure is lower.

3 · Quick check answer before you flip

Three functions of blood?
Transport, protect, regulate temperature
What is haemoglobin?
Iron-containing red pigment that picks up oxygen
Artery v vein: direction and wall?
Artery: away from heart, thick walls. Vein: to heart, thinner walls + valves
Why are capillary walls one cell thick?
So oxygen and carbon dioxide can be exchanged easily
Which vessel takes oxygenated blood to the body?
The aorta (from the left ventricle)
🫁 Ch 8 · The Respiratory System ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
Inhalation / exhalationBreathing in / breathing out.
EpiglottisFlap of tissue that stops food going into the trachea.
TracheaThe windpipe; c-shaped rings of cartilage keep it open.
Bronchus (bronchi)The two tubes the trachea divides into, one for each lung.
BronchiolesIncreasingly narrow tubes that each bronchus divides into.
Alveolus (alveoli)Tiny air sac where gases are exchanged between air and blood.
DiffusionMovement of a gas from high concentration to low concentration.
DiaphragmSheet of muscle below the lungs; contracts and flattens to bring air in.
Intercostal musclesMuscles between the ribs that move the ribs.

2 · Must remember

Nose→Pharynx→Trachea→2 Bronchi→Bronchioles→Alveoli
GasInhaledExhaled
Oxygen21%16%
Carbon dioxide0.04%4%
Nitrogen78%78%
Water vapourlessmore
  • Nose warms, moistens and cleans air (hairs + mucus).
  • Alveoli: ~300 million per lung, thin walls (one cell), large surface area, surrounded by capillaries.
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Respiration ≠ breathing.
  • In: diaphragm contracts and flattens. Out: relaxes to a dome. Don't reverse them.
  • Diffusion answers: say which gas, from where to where.

3 · Quick check answer before you flip

Job of the epiglottis?
Stops food going down into the trachea
Define diffusion
Movement of a gas from high to low concentration
Two ways alveoli suit gas exchange?
Large surface area; thin walls (one cell), surrounded by capillaries
Diaphragm when you inhale?
Contracts and flattens
% CO₂ inhaled v exhaled?
0.04% in, 4% out
🏃 Ch 27 · Motion ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
SpeedHow fast an object travels: distance ÷ time (m/s).
VelocityThe speed of an object and the direction it travels in.
AccelerationThe rate at which velocity changes: change in velocity ÷ time (m/s²).
Linear motionMovement in a straight line.
Distance-time graphDistance (y) against time (x); slope = speed.
Velocity-time graphVelocity (y) against time (x); slope = acceleration.

2 · Must remember

speed = distance ÷ time   (m/s)
distance = speed × time  ·  time = distance ÷ speed
acceleration = (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time   (m/s²)
Line on graphDistance-timeVelocity-time
Straight, sloping upConstant speed (steeper = faster)Constant acceleration
HorizontalStoppedConstant velocity
Sloping down–Slowing down
Worked example
Car goes from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s → a = (25 − 5) ÷ 4 = 5 m/s²
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Convert km → m and minutes/hours → s first.
  • Velocity always needs a direction (20 m/s east).
  • Slowing down = negative acceleration. Check the y-axis label before reading a graph.

3 · Quick check answer before you flip

Speed v velocity?
Velocity is speed with a direction
Formula for acceleration (with unit)
(final − initial velocity) ÷ time, m/s²
Slope of a distance-time graph gives…
Speed
Horizontal line on a velocity-time graph?
Constant velocity
360 m in 30 s: speed?
360 ÷ 30 = 12 m/s
🌍 Ch 36 · The Earth-Sun-Moon System ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
ShadowDark area formed when an opaque object blocks rays of light.
AxisImaginary line through the North and South poles; Earth spins on it; tilted at 23.5°.
SeasonA period of the year when the weather and hours of daylight change.
Lunar phasesThe different shapes of the Moon we see from Earth as it orbits.
Waxing / waningGetting bigger / getting smaller.
Solar eclipseMoon moves between the Sun and Earth; Moon's shadow falls on Earth.
Lunar eclipseEarth moves between the Sun and Moon; Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
UmbraDarkest part of a shadow: light completely blocked.
PenumbraLighter part of a shadow: light only partially blocked.

2 · Must remember

Key numberValue
Earth spins once (day + night)24 hours
Earth orbits the Sun365.25 days
Moon orbits Earth (and rotates once)27 days
Lunar cycle29.5 days
Axis tilt23.5°
New→Waxing crescent→First quarter→Waxing gibbous→Full→Waning gibbous→Third quarter→Waning crescent
  • Seasons = 365.25-day orbit + tilt staying the same. Summer: tilted towards the Sun, higher angle, longer days, smaller area heated → warmer.
  • Shadows: light travels in straight lines and can't pass through an opaque object.
  • Moon reflects sunlight; same side always faces us (rotation = orbit time).
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Seasons are not caused by being closer to the Sun.
  • Solar = Sun – Moon – Earth; lunar = Sun – Earth – Moon.
  • Never look directly at the Sun (UV burns the retina): eclipse glasses or pinhole camera.

3 · Quick check answer before you flip

What causes the seasons?
Earth's tilted axis (23.5°) as it orbits the Sun
Order in a solar eclipse?
Sun – Moon – Earth
Umbra v penumbra?
Umbra: light fully blocked (darkest). Penumbra: partly blocked
Waxing means…
Getting bigger
Why do we always see the same side of the Moon?
It rotates once in the same time it orbits Earth (27 days)
🏁 Final mixed quiz ⏱ 6 min

12 questions across all five chapters. Anything you miss: jump back to that chapter's key-terms table.

Digested food being used by cells for energy is…

  1. Absorption
  2. Assimilation
  3. Egestion
  4. Ingestion
Assimilation = cells use it; absorption = into the blood.

Amylase breaks starch down into…

  1. Glucose
  2. Maltose
  3. Fat
  4. Bile
Amylase: starch → maltose. Maltase then makes glucose.

Which organ makes bile?

  1. Pancreas
  2. Stomach
  3. Liver
  4. Small intestine
The liver makes bile; the pancreas makes enzymes.

Which blood component helps blood clot?

  1. Plasma
  2. Red blood cells
  3. White blood cells
  4. Platelets
Platelets are fragments that help clotting.

Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs?

  1. Pulmonary vein
  2. Aorta
  3. Pulmonary artery
  4. Vena cava
The pulmonary artery is the artery that carries deoxygenated blood.

The upper chambers of the heart are the…

  1. Ventricles
  2. Atria
  3. Septum
  4. Valves
Atria = upper; ventricles = lower.

Gas exchange in the lungs happens at the…

  1. Trachea
  2. Bronchi
  3. Alveoli
  4. Pharynx
Alveoli: large surface area, thin walls, capillaries.

Exhaled air contains about…

  1. 21% oxygen
  2. 4% carbon dioxide
  3. 0.04% carbon dioxide
  4. 90% nitrogen
CO₂ rises from 0.04% to 4%; oxygen falls 21% → 16%.

A bus travels 1200 m in 60 s. Its speed is…

  1. 20 m/s
  2. 72 000 m/s
  3. 1260 m/s
  4. 0.05 m/s
1200 ÷ 60 = 20 m/s.

A horizontal line on a distance-time graph means…

  1. Constant speed
  2. Accelerating
  3. Stopped
  4. Slowing down
Distance isn't changing, so the object is stopped.

Ireland has summer when…

  1. Earth is closest to the Sun
  2. The northern hemisphere tilts towards the Sun
  3. The Moon is full
  4. Earth spins faster
Tilt towards the Sun: higher angle, longer days, warmer.

In a lunar eclipse…

  1. The Moon is between Sun and Earth
  2. Earth is between Sun and Moon
  3. The Sun is between Earth and Moon
  4. The Moon is new
Sun – Earth – Moon: Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
