🍽️ Ch 6 · Digestive System
Key termMeaning
⭐ DigestionBreaking food into smaller pieces / simpler chemicals (physical + chemical)
IngestionFood taken into the mouth
⭐ AbsorptionDigested food passes into the blood
⭐ AssimilationBody uses digested food for energy and new cells
EgestionUndigested food released as waste
Physical digestionTeeth, tongue and stomach churning make pieces smaller
Chemical digestionEnzymes and juices break food down further
⭐ EnzymeA protein that speeds up chemical reactions and is not used up
⭐ AmylaseEnzyme in saliva: starch → maltose
⭐ MaltaseEnzyme in small intestine: maltose → glucose
⭐ PeristalsisWave-like muscle movement pushing food along the oesophagus
⭐ VilliTiny projections in small intestine: large surface area for absorption
⭐ BileMade by liver; helps digest fats
PancreasMakes digestive enzymes
Large intestineReabsorbs water; faeces stored in rectum, leave via anus
Ingestion→Digestion→Absorption→Assimilation→Egestion
⚠️ Common mistakes: an enzyme is not used up. Absorption = into the blood; assimilation = cells use it. The large intestine does not digest food.
❤️ Ch 7 · Circulatory System
Key termMeaning
⭐ PlasmaLight yellow liquid (mostly water) carrying dissolved substances, waste and heat
⭐ Red blood cellsCarry oxygen to cells for respiration
⭐ HaemoglobinRed, iron-containing pigment that picks up oxygen
⭐ White blood cellsFight micro-organisms or make antibodies
⭐ PlateletsFragments of larger cells that help blood clot
⭐ ArteriesCarry blood away from heart; thick muscle walls
⭐ VeinsCarry blood to heart; thinner walls and valves
⭐ CapillariesLink arteries and veins; walls one cell thick
ValvesStop backflow of blood
Oxygenated bloodHigh oxygen, low carbon dioxide
Deoxygenated bloodLow oxygen, high carbon dioxide
SeptumWall separating left and right sides of the heart
⭐ Atria / VentriclesAtria = upper chambers; ventricles = lower chambers
Vena cavaLargest vein; deoxygenated blood to right atrium
⭐ Pulmonary arteryDeoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs
⭐ Pulmonary veinOxygenated blood from lungs to left atrium
⭐ AortaMajor artery: oxygenated blood from left ventricle to body
PulseWave of vibration through an artery as blood leaves the heart
Vena cava→Right atrium→Right ventricle→Pulmonary artery→Lungs→Pulmonary vein→Left atrium→Left ventricle→Aorta→Body

Blue = deoxygenated, red = oxygenated. Resting pulse: 60–100 bpm.

⚠️ Common mistake: the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary vein oxygenated blood, the exception to the usual artery/vein rule.
🫁 Ch 8 · Respiratory System
Key termMeaning
⭐ Inhalation / ExhalationBreathing in / breathing out
⭐ EpiglottisFlap that stops food entering the trachea
⭐ TracheaWindpipe; c-shaped cartilage rings keep it open
Bronchus (bronchi)Two tubes the trachea splits into, one per lung
BronchiolesIncreasingly narrow tubes branching from each bronchus
⭐ Alveolus (alveoli)Tiny air sacs where gases are exchanged between air and blood
⭐ DiffusionGas moves from high concentration to low concentration
⭐ DiaphragmMuscle below lungs: contracts and flattens to bring air in
Intercostal musclesMuscles between ribs that move the ribs
PharynxThroat: opening for both digestive and respiratory systems
MucusSticky substance in the nose that traps dust and micro-organisms
Nose→Pharynx→Trachea→2 Bronchi→Bronchioles→Alveoli
GasInhaledExhaled
Oxygen21%16%
Carbon dioxide0.04%4%
Nitrogen78%78%

Exhaled air also has more water vapour. Inhaling: diaphragm contracts and flattens; exhaling: it relaxes to a dome. Respiration ≠ breathing.

🏃 Ch 27 · Motion
Key termMeaning
MotionAn object is moving
Linear motionMovement in a straight line
⭐ SpeedHow fast something travels: distance ÷ time (m/s)
⭐ VelocitySpeed in a given direction (e.g. 20 m/s east)
⭐ AccelerationRate of change of velocity: (final − initial velocity) ÷ time (m/s²)
⭐ Distance-time graphDistance on y-axis, time on x-axis; slope = speed
⭐ Velocity-time graphVelocity on y-axis, time on x-axis; slope = acceleration
Formulae (m/s, m/s², m, s):
speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed × time  ·  time = distance ÷ speed
acceleration = (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time
  • Distance-time graph: slope = speed; steeper = faster; horizontal = stopped.
  • Velocity-time graph: slope = acceleration; horizontal = constant velocity; downward = slowing.
⚠️ Common mistakes: convert km/minutes to m/s first; velocity needs a direction; slowing down gives negative acceleration.
🌍 Ch 36 · Earth-Sun-Moon System
Key termMeaning
ShadowDark area formed when an opaque object blocks light
Opaque objectAn object that blocks light
⭐ AxisImaginary line through the poles; Earth spins on it; tilted at 23.5°
⭐ SeasonPeriod of the year with a change in weather and hours of daylight
⭐ Lunar phasesDifferent shapes of the Moon seen from Earth as it orbits
⭐ Waxing / WaningWaxing = getting bigger; waning = getting smaller
EclipseThe Sun's light is blocked by the Moon or the Earth
⭐ Solar eclipseMoon between Sun and Earth (Sun – Moon – Earth)
⭐ Lunar eclipseEarth between Sun and Moon (Sun – Earth – Moon)
⭐ UmbraDarkest part of a shadow: light completely blocked
⭐ PenumbraLighter part of a shadow: light partly blocked
Key numberValue
Earth spins once24 hours
Earth orbits Sun365.25 days
Moon orbits Earth27 days
Lunar cycle29.5 days
Earth's axis tilt23.5°
New→Waxing crescent→First quarter→Waxing gibbous→Full→Waning gibbous→Third quarter→Waning crescent
⚠️ Common mistake: seasons are caused by the tilt and orbit, not by Earth being closer to the Sun. The Moon reflects sunlight. Never look directly at the Sun.

❓ Rapid Quiz

Digested food passing into the blood is called…

  1. Ingestion
  2. Absorption
  3. Assimilation
  4. Egestion
Absorption = into the blood. Assimilation is cells using it.

Which makes bile?

  1. Pancreas
  2. Stomach
  3. Liver
  4. Large intestine
The liver makes bile; the pancreas makes enzymes.

Why do villi help absorption?

  1. They churn food
  2. They make enzymes
  3. They give a large surface area
  4. They store bile
Villi greatly increase surface area.

Which vessel has walls one cell thick?

  1. Artery
  2. Vein
  3. Capillary
  4. Aorta
Capillaries are one cell thick so substances can exchange easily.

Which carries oxygenated blood to the left atrium?

  1. Vena cava
  2. Pulmonary artery
  3. Pulmonary vein
  4. Aorta
The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood from the lungs.

What do platelets do?

  1. Carry oxygen
  2. Make antibodies
  3. Help blood clot
  4. Carry heat only
Platelets are fragments that help blood to clot.

What stops food entering the trachea?

  1. Diaphragm
  2. Epiglottis
  3. Mucus
  4. Bronchiole
The epiglottis is the flap that covers the trachea.

When you inhale, the diaphragm…

  1. Relaxes and domes
  2. Contracts and flattens
  3. Stays still
  4. Fills with air
Contracts and flattens to bring air in.

A car travels 300 m in 20 s. Its speed is…

  1. 6000 m/s
  2. 15 m/s
  3. 320 m/s
  4. 0.07 m/s
300 ÷ 20 = 15 m/s.

A cyclist speeds from 2 m/s to 12 m/s in 5 s. Acceleration?

  1. 2 m/s²
  2. 10 m/s²
  3. 50 m/s²
  4. 14 m/s²
(12 − 2) ÷ 5 = 2 m/s².

A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means…

  1. Stopped
  2. Constant velocity
  3. Accelerating
  4. Slowing
Horizontal on a v-t graph = constant velocity (stopped is for distance-time).

Seasons are caused by…

  1. Distance from the Sun
  2. Earth's tilted axis and orbit
  3. The Moon's orbit
  4. Earth's spin
The 23.5° tilt plus the 365.25-day orbit.

In a lunar eclipse the order is…

  1. Sun-Moon-Earth
  2. Sun-Earth-Moon
  3. Earth-Sun-Moon
  4. Moon-Sun-Earth
Earth is between the Sun and the Moon.
✏️ Practice Questions
Distinguish between absorption and assimilation. (2 marks)
Absorption: digested food passes into the blood. Assimilation: the body uses the digested food for energy and to build new cells.
Name the vessel types and give one feature of each. (3 marks)
Arteries: thick muscle walls, carry blood away from heart. Veins: valves/thinner walls, carry blood to heart. Capillaries: walls one cell thick, link arteries and veins.
Explain how the alveoli are suited to gas exchange. (3 marks)
Tiny air sacs with a large surface area; thin walls (one cell thick); surrounded by capillaries; gases move by diffusion from high to low concentration.
A runner covers 400 m in 80 s then accelerates from 5 m/s to 9 m/s in 2 s. Find the speed and the acceleration.
Speed = 400 ÷ 80 = 5 m/s. Acceleration = (9 − 5) ÷ 2 = 2 m/s².
Explain why we have seasons. (4 marks)
Earth's axis is tilted at 23.5° and stays the same as Earth orbits the Sun in 365.25 days. Summer: tilted towards the Sun, higher angle of light, longer days, smaller area heated, warmer. Winter is the opposite.
Describe a solar eclipse. (2 marks)
The Moon moves between the Sun and the Earth (Sun – Moon – Earth), casting the Moon's shadow on Earth; umbra is the darkest part, penumbra the lighter part.
