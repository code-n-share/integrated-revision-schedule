🎯 Segmentation, Target & Niche Markets
Market segmentation: dividing the overall market into several sections or parts with the aim of promoting slightly different products to different groups of consumers.
Target market: the group of people whom a business aims to sell its products or services to. It can be based on any type of criteria, e.g. age, gender, location, income level, product usage, lifestyle.
Niche market: a small, specialised segment of a larger market for a particular product or service.
💡 e.g. folding bicycles: lots of people like/need/want bikes, but only a small part of that market wants to actually use a folding bike.
Marketing mix: a combination of four elements that help a business market its products to its target market and maximise profits – the 4 Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up target market (the group a business aims at) and niche market (a small specialised segment of a bigger market).
📍 Place
Place: refers to where customers will buy the product and the channel of distribution used to get the product to this location. Channels of distribution = the way in which the product gets from the manufacturer to the customer.
1 Manufacturer→Wholesaler→Retailer→Consumer
2 Manufacturer→Retailer→Consumer
3 Manufacturer→Wholesaler→Consumer
4 Manufacturer→Consumer

The channel of distribution used depends on: the product itself, legal requirement and product mass (as written in your notes).

💡 Fewer middlemen = shorter chain (channel 4 is direct, e.g. selling straight to the customer).

🏷️ Price
Price: the amount of money a seller charges a customer for a product or service.
Factors to think about when setting a price
  • Price charged for comparative (similar) products
  • Cost of making the product
  • Target market
  • Amount of profit the business wants to make
  • Where the product is in its life cycle
  • Also consider supply and demand
Pricing strategies
StrategyWhat it meansExample
1. Cost-plusCalculate how much it costs to produce a product, then add a % mark-up to make a profit.Cost €40 + 25% mark-up = €50
2. CompetitiveSet a price similar to rival products.Ryanair, Aer Lingus
3. PremiumCharge a permanently high price to convey an image of exclusivity or quality.Luxury cars and clothes
4. PenetrationCharge a low price when a product is new to the market to get consumers interested. The price may rise later.New product launch
5. Peak loadCharge higher prices during periods of high demand and lower the price when demand falls.Flights and holidays
6. PsychologicalSet a price slightly below a round number, in the belief consumers will see it as cheaper; it's in the mind of the consumer.€99 v €100
🧩 Remember it (memory aid): "Cats Chase Pink Pigs Playing Pianos" → Cost-plus · Competitive · Premium · Penetration · Peak load · Psychological.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up premium (permanently high, to suggest quality) with peak load (high only at busy times) and penetration (low at first, may rise later).
📦 Product
Product: an actual item (either a physical good or a service) provided to meet consumers' needs. The product element (product mix) includes design, product life cycle, branding and unique selling point (USP).
💡 A well-designed product is useful, attractive, long-lasting, easy to use, and should be sustainable.
Product life cycle

The different stages a product goes through from the time it was launched to being taken off the market.

StageWhat happens to sales
1 IntroductionNew product is launched; sales are slow until consumers are familiar with it.
2 GrowthSales rapidly increase as more people learn about it.
3 MaturitySales increase at a slower pace and may start to level off, often when competitors enter the market.
4 SaturationSales reach their peak, because most consumers already have the product or because of competitors in the market.
5 DeclineSales fall due to a change in taste or fashion or the introduction of a new product. Eventually the product is withdrawn from the market.
🧩 Remember it (from your notes): "I Get M & S Donuts" → Introduction · Growth · Maturity · Saturation · Decline.
Branding
Brand: a name, symbol, design or other feature that makes a product easy to recognise and distinguishes it from competing products.
  • Advantages: consumers know and trust the brand; products are easy to recognise; encourages customers to buy upgrades or new products (brand loyalty); higher prices can be charged as consumers associate them with better quality.
  • Own-label brands: usually developed by a retailer, such as Tesco Finest or Dunnes Stores Simply Better.
Unique selling point (USP)
USP: something that makes a product different from its competitors.
💡 Examples from your notes: McDonald's = fast food & low prices · Head & Shoulders = dandruff protection · TikTok = user-generated content.
Packaging

Packaging isn't only a container for the product but can also help to develop product identity. Functions: protection · presentation · providing information · supporting brand identity.

🧩 Remember it (memory aid): packaging does PPPI: Protect, Present, Provide information, support brand Identity.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying sales peak at maturity. Sales level off at maturity and peak at saturation.
📢 Promotion
Promotion: making customers aware of the product/brand in order to increase sales and create brand loyalty.

Businesses use promotion to: launch a new product or service · increase sales of existing products or services · improve the image of a business.

Promotion mix: a combination of promotional activities that a business uses to communicate with existing and potential customers.
  • Advertising · Sales promotion · Public relations · Sponsorship · Product placement
  • Personal selling · Social media · Merchandising · Celebrity endorsement / influencers
Advertising and the AIDA model
LetterStage
AAttract attention
ICapture the interest of the consumer
DStimulate desire for the product
ALead to action (purchase of the product)
Types of advertising
TypeWhat it does
1. InformativeProvides information about a product or service to the general public.
2. PersuasiveConvinces/persuades people that they need a product or service.
3. CompetitiveUsed to convince people that one product is better than another.
4. ReminderReminds customers that a product is still available and value for money.
5. GenericWhen firms in an industry work together to persuade people to buy a particular type of product rather than a specific brand.
Advertising Standards Authority (ASA): ensures that all marketing communications are legal, decent, honest and truthful.
⚠️ Common mistake: confusing competitive advertising (one brand v another) with generic advertising (the whole industry promotes a type of product, not a brand).

✏️ Practice Questions
Define market segmentation, target market and niche market.
Market segmentation: dividing the overall market into several sections to promote slightly different products to different groups of consumers. Target market: the group of people a business aims to sell its products or services to (based on e.g. age, gender, location, income, lifestyle). Niche market: a small, specialised segment of a larger market (e.g. folding bicycles).
Name the four elements of the marketing mix and explain what the marketing mix is for.
Product, Price, Place and Promotion (the 4 Ps). It is a combination of four elements that help a business market its products to its target market and maximise profits.
Describe the four channels of distribution and name three things that affect the channel chosen.
Manufacturer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer; manufacturer → retailer → consumer; manufacturer → wholesaler → consumer; manufacturer → consumer. The channel used depends on the product itself, legal requirement and product mass (as in your notes).
A firm's product costs €60 to make and it adds a 30% mark-up. (a) Name the pricing strategy. (b) Calculate the selling price. (c) Name and explain two other pricing strategies.
(a) Cost-plus pricing. (b) €60 × 1.30 = €78. (c) Any two: competitive (price similar to rivals); premium (permanently high to convey quality); penetration (low at launch, may rise); peak load (higher at busy times); psychological (just below a round number).
Describe the five stages of the product life cycle and what happens to sales in each.
Introduction: new product launched, slow sales until consumers are familiar. Growth: sales rapidly increase. Maturity: sales increase more slowly and may level off, often as competitors enter. Saturation: sales reach their peak (most consumers have it or competitors in the market). Decline: sales fall due to changing taste/fashion or a new product; eventually withdrawn from the market.
Explain what a brand is and outline three advantages of branding to a business. Define USP.
A brand is a name, symbol, design or other feature that makes a product easy to recognise and distinguishes it from competing products. Advantages: consumers know and trust the brand; products are easy to recognise; encourages brand loyalty (customers buy upgrades/new products); higher prices can be charged as consumers associate the brand with better quality. A USP is something that makes a product different from its competitors.
Outline the functions of packaging.
Protection, presentation, providing information and supporting brand identity.
Explain the AIDA model and describe three types of advertising.
AIDA: attract Attention, capture Interest, stimulate Desire, lead to Action (purchase). Types: informative (gives information to the public); persuasive (convinces people they need it); competitive (convinces people one product is better than another); reminder (reminds customers it is still available and value for money); generic (firms in an industry work together to promote a type of product rather than a brand). The ASA ensures advertising is legal, decent, honest and truthful.
