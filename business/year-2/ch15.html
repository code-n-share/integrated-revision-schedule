💡 Enterprise
Enterprise: any attempt to start or do something new. It is about turning ideas into actions, using creativity and a willingness to try new things.
💡 Enterprise isn't only business – it's "having a go" with a new idea.
WhereExamples of enterprise
At home / personal lifeLearning a new skill (e.g. a musical instrument); DIY projects; selling unwanted items.
In schoolStudent enterprise or mini-company; organising events or trips.
In the communitySetting up sports or youth clubs; meals on wheels for the elderly or Neighbourhood Watch groups.
In public serviceOnline services and portals such as Revenue Online; initiatives by local councils to help set up small businesses.
🧑‍💼 The Entrepreneur
Entrepreneur: a person who takes the initiative as well as the personal and financial risk involved in setting up an organisation. They try to make a profit as a reward for their efforts and put ideas into practice and action. Example: Ben Dunne – Dunnes Stores.
💡 The one who says "I'll do it" – and puts their own money and name on the line.
Characteristics (what they are like)Skills (what they can do)
• Realistic risk-takers
• Innovative
• Proactive
• Flexible
• Self-confident
• Decisive
• Determined / resilient		 • Ability to identify opportunities
• Planning and goal setting
• Time management
• Ability to manage money
• Human relations skills
• Ability to assess and manage risks
Intrapreneur: someone who is enterprising within an existing business. Example: a regional manager at McDonald's came up with the Happy Meal gimmick to promote a new meal for kids.
💡 Entrepreneur = starts their own business; intrapreneur = innovates inside someone else's.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up characteristics and skills. Characteristics are personality traits (innovative, determined); skills are things you can learn to do (planning, time management).
⚖️ Risks and Rewards
Rewards of being an entrepreneur / self-employedRisks
• You're your own boss and make all the decisions
• You can keep all the profit
• You can work flexible hours and make flexible opening hours for customers
• Great sense of satisfaction if your business succeeds		 • You may lose all the money you invested – your own money or personal assets
• You'll need to work long hours and oversee all aspects of the business
• Difficult to take time away
• Income isn't guaranteed
• Reputation and credit rating may suffer if the business fails
⚠️ Common mistake: "flexible hours" is a reward, but "long hours" is a risk. Say which one you're discussing and give the reason.

🔎 Sources of New Product Ideas
Sources of new product ideas for existing businesses: internal (from inside the business) and external (from outside).
InternalExternal
Brainstorming: people coming together and thinking of new ideas.

Sales personnel: feedback from salespeople who are in touch with customers is a valuable source of information about customer needs and wants.

Intrapreneurship: employers may encourage employees to think of new ideas, rewarding good ones with a bonus.

R&D department: dedicated research and development staff may discover a new product or improve an existing one.		 Competitors: see what competitors are doing, while being mindful of copyright or patent law (you cannot copy an idea that is protected). Copies are known as "me too" products.

Import substitution: an opportunity to create a business by replacing imports with goods and services produced in Ireland.

Customer complaints or feedback: could result in changes to, or further developments of, existing products or services.

Market research: identify gaps in the market and find market trends.
⚠️ Common mistake: putting sales personnel under external. They are employees, so they are an internal source (even though they hear from customers).
🪜 Product Development Process
The product development process – 7 steps:
1 Idea generation→2 Product screening→3 Concept development→4 Feasibility study→5 Prototype development→6 Test marketing→7 Product launch
#StepMeaning
1Idea generationComing up with ideas
2Product screeningGet rid of the bad ideas (filter)
3Concept developmentDevelop the idea you choose
4Feasibility studyWill you be able to afford the idea?
5Prototype developmentMaking a sample of the idea
6Test marketingTesting it in the market
7Product launchLaunching the idea to people

✏️ Practice Questions
Define enterprise, entrepreneur and intrapreneur. Give an example of each.
Enterprise: any attempt to start or do something new (e.g. a student mini-company). Entrepreneur: a person who takes the initiative and the personal and financial risk of setting up an organisation (e.g. Ben Dunne – Dunnes Stores). Intrapreneur: someone who is enterprising within an existing business (e.g. a McDonald's regional manager who created the Happy Meal).
Outline three characteristics and three skills of a successful entrepreneur.
Characteristics (any 3): realistic risk-taker, innovative, proactive, flexible, self-confident, decisive, determined/resilient. Skills (any 3): identify opportunities, planning and goal setting, time management, manage money, human relations, assess and manage risks.
Discuss the rewards and risks of being self-employed.
Rewards: own boss and make all the decisions; keep all the profit; flexible hours; satisfaction if the business succeeds. Risks: may lose all the money invested (including personal assets); long hours; difficult to take time away; income isn't guaranteed; reputation and credit rating may suffer if the business fails.
Explain the role of the entrepreneur in the economy and in society.
Economy: makes use of resources, provides goods and services, creates jobs and wealth, provides training and develops skills, raises the standard of living, contributes to national income through taxation and through foreign trade (exports). Society: provides services such as clubs and charities, develops infrastructure and amenities (e.g. transport, childcare), raises the standard of living, creates a culture of enterprise and self-help, and sponsors community organisations.
Describe two internal and two external sources of new product ideas.
Internal: brainstorming (people thinking of ideas together); sales personnel (feedback about customer needs); intrapreneurship (employees rewarded with a bonus for ideas); R&D department. External: competitors (without breaching copyright/patent law); import substitution; customer complaints or feedback; market research (gaps and trends). Choose any two of each and explain them.
List and explain the steps in the product development process.
1 Idea generation – coming up with ideas; 2 Product screening – filter out bad ideas; 3 Concept development – develop the chosen idea; 4 Feasibility study – can you afford it?; 5 Prototype development – make a sample; 6 Test marketing – test it in the market; 7 Product launch – launch it to people.
