🛡️ Ch 9 · Insurance ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
InsuranceFinancial protection against possible loss; tries to put the insured back in the same financial position.
Insured / insurerInsured = the person who takes out and pays for insurance. Insurer = the company that pays out.
PremiumThe amount the insured pays an insurer in return for cover for a particular risk.
Insurable interestYou must benefit from the item's existence and suffer financially from its loss.
Utmost good faithTell the truth and disclose all material facts; if you don't, no compensation is paid.
IndemnityYou cannot profit from insurance: compensation only for the value of the loss.
SubrogationOnce the insurer pays compensation, ownership of the item passes to the insurer.
ContributionSame risk insured with more than one insurer: they divide the cost of the claim.
LoadingAn extra charge added to the basic premium, e.g. novice driver charge, fully comprehensive cover.
Policy excessThe amount the insured must pay towards any loss; the insurer pays the rest.
Average clauseApplies to underinsurance and partial loss: compensation is reduced in proportion to the under-insurance.

2 · Must remember

🧩 5 principles: “I Usually Insure Sensible Cars” = Insurable interest, Utmost good faith, Indemnity, Subrogation, Contribution. Key phrases: indemnity = “cannot profit”, subrogation = “ownership passes”.
  • Home: two parts, buildings and contents. Discount for security features (e.g. burglar alarm).
  • Motor: required by law. Third party (and third party fire & theft) or comprehensive. No claims bonus = discount next year if you had no accidents.
Premium = (Value insured ÷ unit) × rate  → buildings + contents
Discounts: add the %s, then × (1 − total)  (10% + 5% → × 0.85)
Car quote: basic + loadings = total, then less discount
Compensation = Loss − Excess
Average clause: Compensation = (Sum insured ÷ Market value) × Loss
📘 Worked example: Healy family
Buildings €2.50 per €1,000 (house €320,000); contents €3 per €1,000 (€35,000). 10% security discount + 5% for applying online.
Buildings 320 × €2.50 = €800 · Contents 35 × €3 = €105 · Total €905 · 15% off → €905 × 0.85
= €769.25
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Insured = customer (pays the premium); insurer = company (pays the compensation). Don't swap them.
  • Divide by the unit (€1,000 / €5,000 / €10,000) first, and add the discounts before multiplying.
  • Average clause: sum insured on top, market value on the bottom, so the answer is never more than the loss.

3 · Quick check say the answer, then tap the card to check

Name the 5 principles of insurance
Insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, subrogation, contribution
The insurer pays out for a written-off car. Who owns the wreck, and which principle is this?
The insurer: subrogation
Buildings €5 per €1,000, house insured for €200,000. Premium?
200 × €5 = €1,000
Loss €6,000, policy excess €300. How much does the insurer pay?
€6,000 − €300 = €5,700
House worth €300,000 insured for €150,000; €40,000 damage. Compensation?
Average clause: (150,000 ÷ 300,000) × €40,000 = €20,000
🛒 Ch 12 · Protecting the Consumer ⏱ 7 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
ConsumerA person who buys goods or services for their own use.
Merchantable qualityGoods must be of a standard fit to be sold (not faulty or damaged).
RedressA solution to a consumer issue: repair, replacement or refund.
GuaranteeManufacturer's promise to sort out problems within a fixed period; usually free; doesn't take away legal rights.
Extended warrantyLike an insurance policy: you pay a premium to extend the guarantee for longer.
Misleading practiceFalse or untrue information used to deceive the consumer.
Aggressive practiceHarassment, physical force or influence used to force someone into buying a product.
Cooling-off period14 days to return an item bought at a distance because you changed your mind.
CCPCCompetition and Consumer Protection Commission: informs, educates, researches and enforces consumer protection law.
Small claims procedureQuick, cheap way to resolve a complaint without a solicitor: max claim €2,000, fee €25.
OmbudsmanIndependent, impartial and free; investigates complaints about public bodies (government departments, local authorities, HSE).

2 · Must remember

LawWhat it says
Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980Goods: merchantable quality · fit for purpose · as described · match the sample.
Services: skills & qualifications · care & attention · merchantable materials.
Consumer Protection Act 2007Misleading claims illegal: goods (performance, ingredients, weight), services (time, place, effect), prices (RRP, previous, actual). Misleading, aggressive and 32 prohibited practices.
Consumer Rights Act 2022EU Consumer Rights Directive: buying online, by phone or mail order from EU businesses. 14-day cooling-off, refund if not delivered, return faulty goods.
Unit price = Price ÷ Quantity  → lowest wins
9 cans €5.00 → 56c each · 16 cans €8.00 → 50c each → the 16-pack is better value
🧩 Responsibilities, W-I-S-E-R: behave Wisely · avoid Impulse buying & false economies · Shop around · Ethical & environmental impact · know your Rights.
Letter of complaint: Issue → Law broken → Redress wanted.
ProblemGo to
Faulty good / poor service from a trader, up to €2,000Small claims procedure
Unfair treatment by a public bodyOffice of the Ombudsman
Supplier in another EU member stateEuropean Consumer Centre (Ireland)
Information, research, enforcing consumer lawCCPC

Online shopping: ✅ convenient 24/7, greater variety, no sales pressure · ❌ fraud risk, can't try goods on, harder returns, environmental impact.

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Compare unit prices, not total prices.
  • A complaint about a shop doesn't go to the Ombudsman (public bodies only): use the small claims procedure.
  • A guarantee never removes your legal rights and is usually free; an extended warranty is paid for.

3 · Quick check say the answer, then tap the card to check

Four things goods must be under the 1980 Act
Merchantable quality, fit for purpose, as described, match the sample

Repair, replacement, refund

🧑‍💼 Ch 15 · Skills, Characteristics & Role of the Entrepreneur ⏱ 6 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
EnterpriseAny attempt to start or do something new: turning ideas into actions.
EntrepreneurTakes the initiative and the personal and financial risk of setting up an organisation (Ben Dunne, Dunnes Stores).
IntrapreneurSomeone enterprising within an existing business (McDonald's regional manager who created the Happy Meal).
BrainstormingPeople coming together and thinking of new ideas.
Import substitutionReplacing imports with goods and services produced in Ireland.
“Me too” productA copy of a competitor's product; you can't copy ideas protected by copyright or patent.
Feasibility studyChecking whether you will be able to afford the idea.
PrototypeA sample of the idea.
Test marketingTesting the product in the market before full launch.

2 · Must remember

Characteristics (what they are like)

Realistic risk-taker · innovative · proactive · flexible · self-confident · decisive · determined / resilient

Skills (what they can do)

Identify opportunities · planning & goal setting · time management · manage money · human relations · assess & manage risks

✅ Rewards

  • Own boss, make all the decisions
  • Keep all the profit
  • Flexible hours
  • Satisfaction if it succeeds

❌ Risks

  • May lose all the money invested
  • Long hours; hard to take time away
  • Income isn't guaranteed
  • Reputation and credit rating may suffer
Role in…The entrepreneur…
OrganisationsGenerates ideas · takes risks · vision & motivation · leadership · long-term plans · raises finance
The economyUses resources · goods & services · jobs & wealth · training · living standards · taxation · foreign trade
SocietyServices (clubs, charities) · infrastructure & amenities · living standards · enterprise culture · sponsorship
Internal sources of ideasExternal sources of ideas
Brainstorming · sales personnel · intrapreneurship (bonus for ideas) · R&D departmentCompetitors · import substitution · customer complaints/feedback · market research (gaps, trends)

Product development process (7 steps)

1 Idea generation→2 Product screening→3 Concept development→4 Feasibility study→5 Prototype→6 Test marketing→7 Product launch
🧩 Remember it: “I guess people should come down for some pizza dinner, tomorrow morning please leave” = Idea · Product screening · Concept · Feasibility · Prototype · Test marketing · Launch.
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Characteristics are personality traits (innovative); skills are learned (time management). Don't mix the lists.
  • Sales personnel are employees, so they're an internal source.
  • Flexible hours = reward; long hours = risk. Say which side you're on.

📊 Ch 21 · Market Research ⏱ 5 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
MarketingThe process of identifying and satisfying customer wants and needs while making a profit.
Market researchGathering, recording and analysing information about consumer preferences to make informed decisions about a potential market.
Field (primary) researchGoing out to the marketplace to gather information first-hand for a specific purpose.
Desk (secondary) researchAnalysing information already gathered for another purpose or from another source; internal or external.
SurveyQuestions asked to customers; they should be clear and not leading.
Closed questionA yes/no question.
Multiple choice questionGives a list of answers to choose from.
Open-ended questionLets the customer say what they like rather than being confined to a specific answer.
Central Statistics Office (CSO)Desk source: population of towns and cities, including age, gender, marital status.

2 · Must remember

Market research→Field = primary: surveys · focus groups · observation|Desk = secondary: internal · external
🧩 Remember it: Field = First-hand (Primary). Desk = Done before (Secondary).

Benefits of market research: customer needs and wants · information on competitors · reactions to changes to products · the best prices to charge · reactions to promotions.

Desk sourceTells you
Business sales reports (internal)Most and least successful products
Newspapers & magazinesCompetitors or consumers
The internetCompetitor products and prices; market size and trends
CSOPopulation: age, gender, marital status

✅ Desk research: advantages

  • Easy to obtain (already available)
  • Quick to gather
  • Cheaper: no trained interviewers

❌ Desk research: disadvantages

  • Not gathered for your needs, so may be less relevant
  • May be outdated
  • Lots to read through

The notes name focus groups and observation but don't define them, and don't give field research pros and cons: check your textbook.

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Market research needs all three verbs, gathering, recording and analysing, plus “to make informed decisions”.
  • A business's own sales reports are internal desk research.
  • Survey questions must be clear and not leading.

📢 Ch 22 · The Marketing Mix ⏱ 8 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
Market segmentationDividing the market into sections to promote slightly different products to different groups of consumers.
Target marketThe group of people a business aims to sell its products or services to.
Niche marketA small, specialised segment of a larger market, e.g. folding bicycles.
Marketing mixFour elements (the 4 Ps) that help a business market to its target market and maximise profits.
Channel of distributionThe way the product gets from the manufacturer to the customer.
Product life cycleThe stages a product goes through from launch to being taken off the market.
BrandA name, symbol, design or feature making a product easy to recognise and distinct from competitors.
USPUnique selling point: something that makes a product different from its competitors.
PromotionMaking customers aware of the product/brand to increase sales and create brand loyalty.
Sales promotionIncentives offered to customers to get them to buy; often short-term tactics.
Public relations (PR)Activities that help create a positive image of a business.

2 · Must remember

4 Ps: Product · Price · Place · Promotion

Place, 4 channels: manufacturer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer · manufacturer → retailer → consumer · manufacturer → wholesaler → consumer · manufacturer → consumer (direct).

Pricing strategyMeaningExample
Cost-plusProduction cost + % mark-up€40 + 25% = €50
CompetitiveSimilar to rivals' pricesRyanair, Aer Lingus
PremiumPermanently high, conveys qualityLuxury cars, clothes
PenetrationLow at launch, may rise laterNew product
Peak loadHigher when demand is highFlights, holidays
PsychologicalJust below a round number€99 v €100
🧩 Pricing: “Cats Chase Pink Pigs Playing Pianos” = Cost-plus, Competitive, Premium, Penetration, Peak load, Psychological.
Life cycle: “I Get M & S Donuts”. Packaging, PPPI: Protect, Present, Provide information, brand Identity.
Introduction: slow sales→Growth: rapid increase→Maturity: slower, levels off→Saturation: sales peak→Decline: sales fall, withdrawn

Branding: trust and recognition · brand loyalty · higher prices. Own-label = retailer's brand (Tesco Finest). USP e.g. Head & Shoulders = dandruff protection.

Advertising: AIDA = Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Five types: informative · persuasive · competitive (one brand v another) · reminder · generic (whole industry, no brand). ASA: legal, decent, honest and truthful.

Promotion mixExample
Sales promotionFree samples, BOGOF, loyalty cards, vouchers
Public relationsCharity events, press releases
SponsorshipSports team, arena, stadium
Personal sellingCars, jewellery, cosmetics
Social mediaCheap, quick, global audience
Celebrity endorsementFree products to influencers
Product placementPaid appearance in films and TV
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Sales level off at maturity and peak at saturation.
  • Premium = permanently high · peak load = high only at busy times · penetration = low at first.
  • Competitive advertising names a brand; generic advertising promotes a type of product. PR builds image; sales promotion is a short-term incentive.

🏁 Final mixed quiz ⏱ 5 min

12 questions across all six chapters. Anything you miss: jump back to that chapter's key-terms table.

A customer hides a material fact on the proposal form. Which principle is broken, and what happens?

  1. Utmost good faith: no compensation is paid
  2. Indemnity: compensation is doubled
  3. Contribution: the claim is shared
  4. Subrogation: the premium is returned
Utmost good faith means disclosing all material facts. If you don't, no compensation is paid.

A premium is €1,000. The customer gets 10% off for security plus a further 5% for applying online. Final premium?

  1. €950
  2. €900
  3. €850
  4. €855
Add the discounts: 15%, so €1,000 × 0.85 = €850.

A faulty washing machine cost €1,200. Which route lets you claim without a solicitor for a €25 fee?

  1. Office of the Ombudsman
  2. Small claims procedure
  3. European Consumer Centre
  4. Advertising Standards Authority
Small claims: max €2,000, €25 fee, no solicitor. The Ombudsman only handles public bodies.

A caller says you've won a prize but must pay a fee to claim it. This is…

  1. A guarantee
  2. An extended warranty
  3. Redress
  4. A prohibited (banned) practice
One of the 32 banned practices under the Consumer Protection Act 2007.

Which is a SKILL (not a characteristic) of an entrepreneur?

  1. Innovative
  2. Resilient
  3. Time management
  4. Proactive
Time management is learned. The other three are personality characteristics.

Which product development step checks whether the business can afford the idea?

  1. Product screening
  2. Feasibility study
  3. Prototype development
  4. Test marketing
Feasibility study: “Will you be able to afford the idea?” Screening only filters out bad ideas.

An Irish business wants help to start selling abroad. Which agency?

  1. Enterprise Ireland
  2. Local Enterprise Office
  3. Clann Credo
  4. Arts Council
Enterprise Ireland helps businesses expand abroad. LEOs help people set up a business.

Which organisation gives loans to community organisations, charities and social enterprises?

  1. Cultural Ireland
  2. Enterprise Ireland
  3. Social Entrepreneurs Ireland
  4. Clann Credo
Clann Credo is a social investment fund. SEI gives financial and practical support to social entrepreneurs.

Using Central Statistics Office population data is…

  1. Field research
  2. Primary research
  3. Desk research
  4. Observation
The CSO already gathered it for another purpose, so it's desk (secondary) research.

Which survey question has a yes/no answer?

  1. Open-ended
  2. Closed
  3. Leading
  4. Multiple choice
Closed = yes/no. Open-ended lets the customer say what they like.

A jumper is priced at €49.99 instead of €50. This is…

  1. Penetration pricing
  2. Psychological pricing
  3. Premium pricing
  4. Cost-plus pricing
Just below a round number so it seems cheaper: psychological pricing.

The dairy industry runs an advert to get people drinking more milk, with no brand named. This is…

  1. Generic advertising
  2. Competitive advertising
  3. Reminder advertising
  4. Public relations
Generic: firms in an industry together promote a type of product rather than a brand.
