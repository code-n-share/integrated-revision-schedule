🛒 The Wise Consumer
Consumer: a person who buys goods or services for their own use.
💡 If you buy it to use yourself (not to resell), you're the consumer.
The wise consumer asks…
  • Do I really need it? · Can I afford it? · Is it safe?
  • Is this the best price? · Is it a false economy?
  • Where does it come from? · How was the good produced? · Are there any hidden charges?
Unit pricing – which is better value?
Unit price = Price ÷ Quantity
📘 Worked example (from your notes)
(a) 9-pack of Brand A cans costs €5.00 → €5.00 ÷ 9 = 56c per can
(b) 16-pack of Brand B cans costs €8.00 → €8.00 ÷ 16 = 50c per can
16-pack of Brand B is better value for money
⚠️ Common mistake: picking the cheaper total price. Always divide to get the price per unit and choose the lowest.
✅ Consumer Responsibilities
Consumer responsibilities (the five main ones):
  1. Behave wisely when buying goods or services.
  2. Avoid impulse buying and false economies.
  3. Shop around for the best value for money.
  4. Consider the environmental and ethical impact of the products you consume.
  5. Know your legal rights.

Also listed in your notes (marked "not important"): use products carefully and in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions; dispose of packaging responsibly, recycling when possible; keep receipts as proof of purchase.

🧩 Remember it (memory aid): Wise · Impulse-free · Shop around · Ethical · Rights = W-I-S-E-R.
📜 Sale of Goods Act, Redress & Guarantees
Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act (1980) – what the law says you must get:
💡 Think "does it work, is it what they said, and was the job done properly?"
Goods must…Services must be…
• Be of merchantable quality
• Be fit for the purpose intended
• Be as described
• Match the sample shown		 • Provided by someone with the necessary skills and qualifications
• Provided with necessary care and attention
• Carried out with goods or materials of merchantable quality
Redress – solutions to issues
RedressWhat it means
1. RepairThe item may be fixed free of charge. The repair must be permanent.
2. ReplacementYou can have the same or an alternative product that is free from damage or faults.
3. RefundYou receive the cost of the item back, in cash or as money paid back to your debit or credit card.
Guarantees and warranties
Guarantee: a promise by the manufacturer or company that it will sort out any problems with a product or service within a specific, fixed period of time. It is usually free and doesn't take away any of your legal rights as a consumer.
Extended warranty: like an insurance policy, for which you must pay a premium. For an extra fee, the manufacturer or retailer will extend the guarantee for a longer period.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying a guarantee removes your legal rights – it doesn't. And a guarantee is usually free, an extended warranty is paid for.
⚖️ Consumer Protection Act 2007
Consumer Protection Act 2007: protects consumers in relation to misleading claims about goods, services and prices. It does this by making it illegal for retailers to act in certain ways.
💡 In plain English: businesses must tell the truth about what they sell.
Misleading claims
About…Must be truthful
GoodsPerformance of a product, the ingredients it contains and the weight of the item.
ServicesThe time, place and effect of a service.
PricesRecommended retail prices (RRP), previous prices and actual prices.
Misleading practices

The Act protects consumers against unfair practices whether they buy locally or from a business within the EU.

  • Misleading: false or untrue information is used to deceive the consumer (making false claims).
  • Aggressive: harassment, physical force or influence are used to force someone into buying a product.
  • Prohibited / banned: a list of 32 banned practices, e.g. telling a customer they have won a prize then demanding payment to redeem it; claiming a business is closing down when it's not.
💻 Consumer Rights Act 2022 & Online Shopping
Consumer Rights Act 2022 – distance (online) selling: when you buy from a retailer located anywhere in the EU you are protected by the EU Consumer Rights Directive. It protects you when buying online, over the phone or by mail order, but only for purchases from businesses located in the EU.
When you buy goods and services online you have the right to…
  • Clear and accurate information before you buy.
  • A refund if your goods are not delivered.
  • Return faulty goods.
  • Return the item because you have changed your mind (14-day cooling-off period).
  • Cancel a service.
  • Cancel digital purchases.

🏛️ Consumer Agencies
AgencyRole
CCPC
(Competition and Consumer Protection Commission)		Statutory organisation that provides information and education to consumers about their personal finance and consumer rights; conducts research into consumer matters; represents consumers' interests by advising government on possible changes to consumer law; enforces consumer protection law in Ireland.
Small claims procedureA quick, cheap and easy way to resolve a complaint about a faulty good or poor work by a service provider without hiring a solicitor. Maximum claim €2,000; application fee €25.
Office of the OmbudsmanInvestigates complaints from people who feel they have been unfairly treated by a public body (government departments, local authorities, HSE, etc.). Independent, impartial and free.
European Consumer Centre (Ireland)Supports consumers who have a problem with a supplier of goods or services in another EU member state. Free information, advice and assistance on consumer rights in the EU.
⚠️ Common mistake: sending a complaint about a shop to the Ombudsman. The Ombudsman deals with public bodies; a faulty good from a trader goes to the small claims procedure (up to €2,000) or CCPC for information.
✉️ Letter of Complaint
Writing a letter of complaint – 3 steps:
  1. Describe the details of the issue.
  2. Give the details of the complaint and show how the situation breaches consumer law.
  3. Explain what redress you want.
📘 Model letter (from your notes: Patricia Cooney, misleading claim)
73 Ballyjamesduff Road, Virginia, Co. Cavan
12 January 2025

Ciara O'Riordan, Snow Holidays Ltd, 44 Farnham Street, Co. Cavan

Re: Misleading Claim – Holiday Brochure

Dear Ms O'Riordan,

I booked a ski holiday to Italy, for me and my husband, costing €1,400, from 2nd to 9th January. The holiday brochure on your website said the ski slopes were a 5-minute walk from my chalet. When I arrived I discovered the nearest ski slope was a 30-minute bus journey away. This is a breach of the Consumer Protection Act 2007, as this was a misleading claim. Therefore, I would like a partial refund for the inconvenience caused.

Yours sincerely,
Patricia Cooney

❓ Quick Quiz

Which is better value: a 9-pack of cans for €5.00 or a 16-pack for €8.00?

  1. The 9-pack (56c per can)
  2. The 16-pack (50c per can)
  3. They are the same
  4. The 9-pack because it costs less in total
€8.00 ÷ 16 = 50c per can, cheaper than €5.00 ÷ 9 = 56c. Always compare unit prices, not totals.

Under the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980, goods must be…

  1. Cheap and attractive
  2. Of merchantable quality, fit for purpose, as described, and match the sample
  3. Made in Ireland
  4. Covered by a guarantee
These are the four requirements for goods. A guarantee is optional and is not part of the Act.

Which of these is NOT a form of redress?

  1. Repair
  2. Replacement
  3. Refund
  4. Rebranding
Redress = repair, replacement or refund.

What is the difference between a guarantee and an extended warranty?

  1. A guarantee is usually free; an extended warranty is paid for with a premium
  2. A guarantee removes your legal rights; a warranty does not
  3. There is no difference
  4. A warranty is free; a guarantee costs money
A guarantee is usually free and doesn't take away legal rights. An extended warranty is like insurance and you pay a premium.

✏️ Practice Questions
Which is better value for money: a 6-pack of yoghurts for €3.00 or a 10-pack for €4.50? Show your workings.
6-pack: €3.00 ÷ 6 = 50c each. 10-pack: €4.50 ÷ 10 = 45c each. The 10-pack is better value (5c cheaper per yoghurt).
Outline three responsibilities of a consumer.
Any three of: behave wisely when buying; avoid impulse buying and false economies; shop around for the best value for money; consider the environmental and ethical impact of products; know your legal rights.
Explain the difference between repair, replacement and refund.
Repair: the item is fixed free of charge and the repair must be permanent. Replacement: you get the same or an alternative product that is free from damage or faults. Refund: you get the cost of the item back, in cash or back to your debit/credit card.
Explain what the Consumer Protection Act 2007 protects consumers from and give two examples of prohibited practices.
It protects consumers from misleading claims about goods, services and prices (e.g. performance, ingredients, weight; time/place/effect of a service; RRP and previous prices) and from misleading and aggressive practices, whether buying locally or from an EU business. There are 32 banned practices, e.g. telling a customer they've won a prize and demanding payment to redeem it, or claiming a business is closing down when it isn't.
Discuss the benefits and challenges of online shopping for the consumer.
Benefits: convenient (24 hours, 365 days), greater variety, no crowds or sales pressure. Challenges: increased risk of fraud, can't touch or try on goods, returning goods can be more difficult, environmental impact. Rights online: clear information, refund if not delivered, return faulty goods, 14-day cooling-off.
Write a letter of complaint: you bought a pair of headphones advertised as "waterproof" from an Irish retailer but they stopped working in the rain. Outline the structure and key content.
Use your address and date, the retailer's name and address, a "Re:" line, "Dear Sir/Madam". Paragraph 1: describe the issue (what you bought, when, price, what happened). Paragraph 2: show it breaches consumer law – goods not as described / misleading claim (Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980; Consumer Protection Act 2007). Paragraph 3: state the redress you want (refund or replacement). Close with "Yours faithfully" (or "sincerely" if you named the person) and your name.
Name and describe two agencies that help consumers.
CCPC: statutory body that informs/educates consumers, researches, advises government and enforces consumer protection law. Small claims procedure: quick, cheap way to resolve a complaint without a solicitor (max €2,000, €25 fee). Also: Ombudsman (public bodies, free, impartial) and European Consumer Centre (Ireland) (cross-border EU problems).
