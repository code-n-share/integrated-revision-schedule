📘 What is Insurance?
Insurance: financial protection against possible loss. It tries to put the insured person back in the same financial position they were in before the loss.
💡 Like a safety net: you pay a little regularly so a big unexpected bill doesn't flatten you.
TermMeaning
Insurance policySets out the details of the types of losses covered and the amount of compensation paid.
InsuredThe person who takes out and pays for insurance.
InsurerThe company that pays money to the insured when there is an insured loss.
CompensationA financial payment made to an insured person if they suffer an insured loss.
PremiumThe amount paid by the insured person to an insurer in return for providing insurance cover for a particular risk.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up insured and insurer. The insured is the customer; the insurer is the company. The customer pays the premium; the company pays the compensation.
⚖️ Principles of Insurance
The 5 principles of insurance: insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, subrogation, contribution.
🧩 Remember it (memory aid): "I Usually Insure Sensible Cars" → Insurable interest, Utmost good faith, Indemnity, Subrogation, Contribution.
PrincipleWhat it meansEveryday picture 💡
Insurable interestTo insure something you must benefit by its existence and suffer (financially) from its loss.You can insure your own car, not a stranger's.
Utmost good faithYou must tell the truth and disclose all material facts to the insurance company. If you fail to, no compensation will be paid.Hiding a fact on the form can cost you the whole claim.
IndemnityYou cannot profit from insurance. You only get compensation for the value of the loss or damage suffered.A €300 phone destroyed pays out €300, not €500.
SubrogationOnce an insurance company has paid compensation, the right of ownership of that item passes to the insurance company.Insurer pays for your written-off car, so it now owns the wreck.
ContributionWhere the same risk is insured with more than one insurer, they divide the cost of the claim between them.Two friends share the bill instead of you being paid twice.
⚠️ Common mistake: confusing indemnity (you can't profit) with subrogation (ownership passes to the insurer). Learn one key phrase for each: "cannot profit" / "ownership passes".
🏠 Types of Insurance
🏠 Home insurance
  • Two parts: buildings and contents.
  • Cost depends on the value of your house and the value of your contents.
  • Discount for homes with greater protection (e.g. security features such as a burglar alarm).
🚗 Motor insurance
  • Everyone who drives a car must have motor insurance – it's the law.
  • Cost depends on your age, type of licence, driving record, engine size, etc.
  • Two types: third party (and third party fire and theft) and comprehensive.
  • A no claims bonus is a discount on the price of next year's insurance if you didn't have any accidents during the year.

🧮 Calculating Premiums
Calculating a premium: work out how many "units" of value you are insuring, multiply by the rate, then add buildings + contents.
💡 Like buying by the kilo: the insurer charges a price per €1,000 (or €5,000, €10,000) of value.
Premium = (Value insured ÷ unit value) × rate
Total premium = Buildings premium + Contents premium
📘 Worked example (from your notes)
Buildings = €12 per €10,000 value, insured for €180,000. Contents = €20 per €5,000 value, insured for €90,000.
  1. Buildings: 180,000 ÷ 10,000 = 18 → 18 × €12 = €216
  2. Contents: 90,000 ÷ 5,000 = 18 → 18 × €20 = €360
  3. Total premium: €216 + €360
= €576
📘 Worked example – comparing 3 quotes (Naughton family)
Insure house for €285,000 and contents for €20,000. Rates per €1,000: A: buildings €2, contents €6.50 · B: €2.50, €5 · C: €3, €4.

💡 Tip: dividing by €1,000 is the same as "how many thousands": €285,000 → 285.
Discounts – multiply by (1 − total discount)
📘 Worked example – home discounts (Healy family)
Circle Insurances: buildings €2.50 per €1,000, contents €3 per €1,000. 10% discount for security features plus a further 5% for applying online (they qualify for both). House €320,000, contents €35,000.
  1. Buildings: 320 × €2.50 = €800
  2. Contents: 35 × €3 = €105
  3. Total before discount = €905
  4. Discounts: 10% + 5% = 15% → €905 × 0.85
= €769.25
📘 Worked example – Hanrahan family
Northern Insurances: buildings €3 per €1,000, contents €2 per €1,000. 8% discount for security features plus a further 10% for applying online (both apply). House €450,000, contents €50,000.
  1. Buildings: 450 × €3 = €1,350; Contents: 50 × €2 = €100 → total €1,450
  2. Discounts: 8% + 10% = 18% → €1,450 × 0.82
= €1,189
📘 Worked example – saving from a burglar alarm (A1 Insurance)
Buildings €2.50 per €1,000; burglar alarm = 9% discount. House €320,000 with no alarm.
  1. Cost without alarm: 320 × €2.50 = €800
  2. Cost with alarm: €800 × 0.91 = €728
  3. Saving: €800 − €728
= €72

💶 Policy Excess
Policy excess: the amount the insured person must pay for any loss or damage to the insured item. The insurance company pays the rest.
💡 Like the "first bit is on you" rule: you cover the first few euro of every claim.
Compensation paid = Loss − Excess
📘 Worked example (Tracy)
Tracy is awarded €5,000 compensation for damage to her car following an accident. Her policy carries an excess of €250. She must pay for the first €250 of any claim, so the insurer actually pays:
€5,000 − €250 = €4,750
➗ The Average Clause
Average clause: applies in the case of underinsurance and partial loss.
💡 If you only insured half the house's value, the insurer only pays half of any (partial) loss.
Compensation = (Sum insured ÷ Market value (actual value)) × Loss
📘 Worked example (from your notes)
House worth €200,000, insured for €100,000, damage €25,000.
  1. Fraction insured: 100,000 ÷ 200,000 = ½
  2. Compensation = ½ × €25,000
= €12,500
⚠️ Common mistake: putting the fraction upside-down. It is sum insured on top and market value on the bottom, so the answer is never more than the loss.

❓ Quick Quiz

Who is the "insured"?

  1. The company that pays compensation
  2. The person who takes out and pays for insurance
  3. The person who sells the policy
  4. The person who is awarded damages in court
The insured is the customer who takes out and pays for insurance. The company paying out is the insurer.

Which principle says you cannot make a profit from an insurance claim?

  1. Contribution
  2. Subrogation
  3. Indemnity
  4. Insurable interest
Indemnity: you only get compensation for the value of the loss. Subrogation is about ownership passing to the insurer.

A customer hides a material fact on the proposal form. Which principle is broken and what is the likely result?

  1. Utmost good faith – no compensation is paid
  2. Indemnity – compensation is doubled
  3. Contribution – the claim is shared
  4. Subrogation – the insurer returns the premium
Utmost good faith means disclosing all material facts. If you fail to, no compensation will be paid.

A car is written off and the insurer pays out. Who now has the right to the wreck?

  1. The insured
  2. The insurer
  3. The Garda
  4. The car's previous owner
Subrogation: once compensation is paid, ownership of the item passes to the insurance company.

The same house is insured with two insurers. When a claim is made, the insurers split the cost. Which principle is this?

  1. Contribution
  2. Indemnity
  3. Insurable interest
  4. Utmost good faith
Contribution: where the same risk is insured with more than one insurer, they divide the cost of the claim.

Buildings are insured at €5 per €1,000. The house is insured for €200,000. What is the buildings premium?

  1. €40
  2. €200
  3. €1,000
  4. €10,000
200,000 ÷ 1,000 = 200 units × €5 = €1,000. Forgetting to divide by the unit is the commonest slip.

A premium is €1,000 before discounts. The customer gets a 10% security discount plus a further 5% for applying online. What is the final premium?

  1. €850
  2. €900
  3. €950
  4. €765
The discounts add: 10% + 5% = 15%, so €1,000 × 0.85 = €850.

Maeve is awarded €8,000 compensation. Her policy excess is €500. How much does the insurer pay?

  1. €500
  2. €7,500
  3. €8,000
  4. €8,500
Excess is deducted: €8,000 − €500 = €7,500.

A house worth €300,000 is insured for €150,000 and suffers €40,000 damage. Under the average clause, how much is paid?

  1. €40,000
  2. €150,000
  3. €20,000
  4. €10,000
Compensation = (150,000 ÷ 300,000) × €40,000 = ½ × €40,000 = €20,000.

What is a "no claims bonus"?

  1. A discount on next year's premium if you made no claims
  2. Extra compensation for honest customers
  3. A refund of the excess
  4. A charge added for new drivers
A no claims bonus is a discount on next year's insurance if you didn't have any accidents during the year. The novice driver charge is a loading.
✏️ Practice Questions
(a) Define insurance and premium. (b) Distinguish between the insured and the insurer.
(a) Insurance is financial protection against possible loss and tries to put the insured person back in the same financial position. A premium is the amount paid by the insured person to an insurer in return for providing insurance cover for a particular risk. (b) The insured is the person who takes out and pays for insurance; the insurer is the company that pays compensation if there is an insured loss.
Explain any three principles of insurance.
Insurable interest: you must benefit by the item's existence and suffer financially from its loss. Utmost good faith: you must tell the truth and disclose all material facts, otherwise no compensation will be paid. Indemnity: you cannot profit from insurance; you only get compensation for the value of the loss. (Also: subrogation – ownership passes to the insurer after paying; contribution – insurers share the cost if the same risk is insured with more than one.)
Calculate the annual home premium: buildings €4 per €1,000 insured for €250,000; contents €6 per €1,000 insured for €30,000. The customer gets a 10% discount for security features and a further 5% for applying online.
Buildings: 250 × €4 = €1,000. Contents: 30 × €6 = €180. Total = €1,180. Discount: 10% + 5% = 15% → €1,180 × 0.85 = €1,003.
Complete a car insurance quote: basic premium €800; novice driver loading €250; fully comprehensive loading €350; 10% discount (of basic premium plus loadings) for a car alarm.
Basic €800 + €250 + €350 = €1,400. Less 10%: €1,400 × 0.9 = €1,260 yearly premium.
Ciara's car is damaged and the loss is €6,000. Her policy has an excess of €300. (a) How much does the insurer pay? (b) Define policy excess.
(a) €6,000 − €300 = €5,700. (b) The policy excess is the amount the insured person must pay for any loss or damage to the insured item; the insurance company pays the rest.
A house worth €400,000 is insured for €300,000. A fire causes €60,000 of damage. Calculate the compensation and name the rule used.
Average clause (underinsurance and partial loss): (300,000 ÷ 400,000) × €60,000 = ¾ × €60,000 = €45,000.
