💡 What is Enterprise?
Enterprise
Enterprise is any attempt to start or do something new. It is about turning ideas into actions, using creativity and a willingness to try new things.

Enterprise is not just about business. Enterprise can happen in many different areas of people's personal, working and social lives.

🏠
Enterprise in the Home
Learning new skills, DIY projects, managing household budgets, or selling unwanted items for extra cash.
🏫
Enterprise in School
Starting a student mini-company, setting up a club, organising events, or introducing new subjects like LCVP.
🤝
Enterprise in the Community
Setting up a sports club, organising a Neighbourhood Watch scheme, or starting a Meals on Wheels service.
🏛️
Enterprise in Public Service
Government initiatives like the National Broadband Plan, Coillte, Revenue's online ROS system, and Local Enterprise Offices (LEOs).
🏢 Enterprise in Business
Entrepreneur
A person who takes the initiative as well as the personal and financial risk to set up a business or organisation.
Social Entrepreneur
Uses their skills to create organisations that benefit the wider community. Less concerned about making a profit than business entrepreneurs.

Enterprise is about turning an idea into action. Entrepreneurs are people who put ideas into practice — they show initiative by making decisions and taking a chance to set up a business.

🚀 Enterprise in Action
Limerick brothers Patrick and John Collison — Stripe
Patrick and John Collison started computer programming as teenagers and developed a web and mobile payments company called Stripe. With dual headquarters in Dublin and San Francisco, the business is valued at $50 billion and is the main payments platform for online businesses. It has a presence in more than 20 countries and has linked up with FreeNow, Amazon and AirBnB, with over 7,000 employees worldwide.
🚀 Enterprise in Action
Grasper — Student Enterprise Programme
Grasper, designed by students from Drumshanbo Vocational School, Co. Leitrim, won the junior category at the 2023 national final of the Student Enterprise Programme. The tool safely opens and closes the lid of a slatted tank, addressing a real safety issue for farmers.
🧩 Characteristics of Entrepreneurs

A characteristic is a personality trait that you are born with. It is part of your personality.

🎲
Realistic Risk-Taker
Willing to risk money and time, but research carefully and only take a chance if there's a reasonable chance of success.
💡
Innovative / Creative
Good at coming up with new ideas or creative ways of doing things. E.g. Steve Jobs' iPhone in 2007 — the first multi-functional smartphone.
⚡
Proactive
Prepared to take action and make things happen, rather than waiting for something to happen or for someone else to do it.
🔄
Flexible
Accepts change as natural. Learns from mistakes and failures, and can adapt plans and actions to new situations.
💪
Self-Confident
Believes in themselves and their business idea. Believes they have what it takes to be successful and can cope with setbacks.
⚖️
Decisive
Able to make timely decisions and take responsibility for the decisions they make.
🏆
Determined / Resilient
Does not give up easily when faced with obstacles and failures. Sticks with a task until it is completed and recovers from setbacks.
💬 Did You Know?
Thomas Edison famously said: "I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work." James Dyson developed 5,127 prototype designs before he successfully invented the bagless vacuum cleaner!
🚀 Enterprise in Action — Risky Business
Brian Lee, Co-founder of Chopped
"You have to be fearless but you have to be calculated too. I usually have a ratio whenever I'm looking at a new business and if I think there's a 70% chance it's going to do well, according to the business plan, the research and the market analysis, then I go with it. I'm never going to be 100% certain."
🛠️ Skills of Entrepreneurs

A skill is an ability or expertise that people develop through practice, experience, learning or training.

Skill What it means
Planning & Goal-Setting Set strategic goals and use plans to achieve them. Write a business plan when establishing a new/start-up business.
Time Management Use time carefully to achieve goals by prioritising daily tasks.
Communication Skills Use various methods to exchange information — letters, business plans, staff meetings, advertisements, etc. Both oral and written.
Ability to Manage Money Good financial skills to prepare budgets and manage cash flow.
Human Relations Skills Good communication and listening skills to get on with employees, suppliers, investors, customers and state agencies.
Problem-Solving Skills Spot when consumers have a problem and use creativity to come up with an innovative solution — e.g. developing prototypes, sourcing finance.
Ability to Assess & Manage Risk Measure risk in a potential course of action and take steps to minimise these risks.
Ability to Identify Opportunities Spot a need or gap in the market not currently being met, by listening carefully to customers' needs and being aware of future business opportunities.
📌
Characteristics vs Skills — Key Difference A characteristic is a personality trait you are born with. A skill is an ability you develop through practice, experience, learning or training.
Should Entrepreneurial Types Become Entrepreneurs?
Intrapreneur
An enterprising employee in an existing business. Uses entrepreneurial skills to benefit or improve their employer's business.

Not everyone with entrepreneurial characteristics wants to start their own business. Some may be happy working for someone else. Examples of intrapreneur innovations include: Sony PlayStation, Post-it notes and Gmail. Google allows employees to spend 20% of their working week on a personal project.

⚖️ Rewards & Risks of Self-Employment
✅ Rewards of Being an Entrepreneur
  • You are your own boss and make all decisions.
  • You keep some or all of the profit the business makes.
  • Possibility of more flexible working hours.
  • Great sense of satisfaction if the business is a success.
❌ Risks of Starting Your Own Business
  • You may lose the money you invested — a major risk as a sole trader (unlimited liability).
  • You will need to work long hours to get the business running.
  • Difficult to take time away from running the business.
  • Sales may vary each week — income is not guaranteed.
  • You may not have all the necessary skills to run a business.
  • Your personal reputation and credit rating may suffer if the business fails.
🏗️ The Role of the Entrepreneur in an Organisation
  • Generates ideas: Uses innovation and creativity to come up with new ideas, or new uses for existing products.
  • Takes risks: Takes a risk to put their ideas into practice and try to develop successful organisations.
  • Provides vision and motivation: Generates ideas and sets out the organisation's future direction and goals — important for guiding and motivating all staff.
  • Provides leadership and management: In start-up organisations, the entrepreneur is usually the person who provides leadership for others.
  • Creates long-term plans: Turns their vision into business plans to help raise finance and guide future growth and development.
  • Raises finance: If the entrepreneur doesn't have all the money they need, they will attract investment and funding.
🌍 The Role of the Entrepreneur in the Economy & Society
Role in the Economy
  • Provide goods and services: Organisations started by entrepreneurs provide consumers and communities with a range of goods and services.
  • Make use of resources: Entrepreneurs use their creative ability to make the best use of available resources.
  • Create jobs and wealth: By starting new businesses, entrepreneurs help create direct and indirect employment.
  • Provide training and develop skills: As a result of employment, workers become more skilled and better able to contribute.
  • Raise standards of living: Through productive use of resources and increased employment, they help improve the standard of living.
  • Taxation: Entrepreneurs and their employees pay taxes to the government which can be reinvested into the local or national economy.
  • Foreign trade: Irish entrepreneurs sell goods and services abroad (exports). They also help reduce imports by producing goods and services in Ireland.
Role in Society
  • Provide services: Social entrepreneurs provide services and supports — e.g. clubs and charities.
  • Develop infrastructure and amenities: Help develop transport, recreation or childcare facilities.
  • Support and sustain communities: Create jobs and improve standard of living, especially in rural areas.
  • Enterprise culture: Inspire other entrepreneurs through positive example.
  • Sponsorship: Many entrepreneurs support and sponsor community-based organisations, including clubs and charities.
🔍 Sources of Business Ideas for Entrepreneurs

To start in business, entrepreneurs obviously need a good idea. Ideas can come from many sources:

🧠
Brainstorming
A way of working together as a group to generate ideas. E.g. the founders of Innocent drinks brainstormed until they came up with the idea of smoothies.
📈
Market Trend
A growing trend can create an opportunity. E.g. the major move towards organically produced foods creates opportunities in organic farming and food products.
👨‍👩‍👧
Family & Friends
An idea could come from hearing a family member complain about not being able to buy a product or service they need.
✈️
Ideas from Abroad
An entrepreneur may spot something working well in another country and bring the idea back to their own country.
Brainstorming
A way of working together as a group to generate ideas.
🏭 Sources of New Product Ideas for Existing Businesses

Existing businesses also need new ideas to replace or improve existing products. Ideas can come from inside the business (internal) or from outside (external).

Internal Sources
  • Brainstorming sessions: People come together to think of new ideas.
  • Sales personnel: Feedback from salespeople in touch with customers is a vital source of information about customer needs and wants.
  • Ideas from employees (intrapreneurship): Employees may be encouraged to think of new ideas by rewarding good ideas with a bonus.
  • R&D department: Research and Development staff may discover a new product or improve an existing one. In 2022 Amazon spent almost €67 billion on R&D.
  • Market research: Gathering, recording and analysing information about consumer preferences to identify gaps and trends.
External Sources
  • Competitors: A business sees what competitors are doing and might use some of their ideas — known as "me-too" products (e.g. tablets similar to the iPad).
  • Import substitution: Some entrepreneurs create a business by replacing imports with goods and services produced in Ireland.
  • Customer complaints or feedback: Could lead to changing or developing existing products or services.
🚀 Enterprise in Action
The Wriggler — Pluxty Ltd.
Inspired by personal experience with their own children, Dublin couple James and Aileen McCauley created The Wriggler, a portable anti-roll changing mat that resembles an adorable bear character. They appeared on Dragons' Den (UK) seeking £50k for a 25% stake — and accepted Sara Davies's offer of £50k for a 35% stake in the business.
🚀 Enterprise in Action
Stira Folding Attic Stairs
Michael Burke founded Stira Folding Attic Stairs Ltd in 1982. When asked to repair an imported attic stairs, he immediately saw the problem and an opportunity — inferior material was used on the arms. He built a prototype and took six months to get the design sorted out, resulting in a strong, safe product.
🚀 Enterprise in Action
Roll It Hand Made All Butter Pastry — Mairéad Finnegan
Mairéad Finnegan, a property surveyor, couldn't find all-butter puff pastry made in Ireland — this was her gap in the market. She produced samples, displayed them at a food festival in Kells in 2012, and sold them all within hours. She approached SuperValu, took business courses with Leader, and received grants through Co. Meath LEO. She now provides advice to new food start-ups in her local area.
🔬 The Product Development Process

This process helps entrepreneurs and businesses develop ideas that have genuine market potential, ensuring the finished product has the best possible chance of success. Product development is expensive and risky.

Market Research
Gathering, recording and analysing information about consumer preferences for a good or service in order to make informed decisions about a potential market.
Prototype
A realistic working model of a product.
Step What happens Example (Scented Candle Mini-Company)
1. Idea Generation Think up as many new ideas as possible via brainstorming. No idea should be ruled out at this point. Students decide to set up a mini-company to produce scented candles, cupcakes or T-shirts or wooden ornaments.
2. Product Screening The best ideas are considered further and any unworkable ones are rejected. Students realise they don't have the resources to make T-shirts, cupcakes or wooden ornaments — scented candles have the best chance.
3. Concept Development The chosen idea is developed into a product that will appeal to consumers. Identify a Unique Selling Point (USP). Students consider candle ingredients, shapes and scents that will distinguish their candles from rivals — their USP.
4. Feasibility Study A feasibility study is carried out — is it actually possible to make, and can it be produced and sold profitably? Students research costs and speak to potential customers. They decide the scented candle idea is realistic and has potential to be profitable.
5. Prototype Development A sample or model of the product is made so the developer can see how it will be manufactured. It can then be tested and altered. Students make several attempts; their fourth prototype is the most successful and sets the standard for all future production.
6. Test Marketing In test marketing, the product is tested on a small sample of the market before going into full production. Students produce a batch of five candles and sell them to neighbours and teachers — receiving very positive feedback.
7. Product Launch The product goes into full-scale production and is introduced to the market. A marketing strategy is needed to persuade consumers to buy. Students invest in raw materials for 50 candles, advertise in school, get free publicity in a local paper, and take a stall at a local Christmas market.
Feasibility Study
A study carried out to see if an idea is commercially viable — i.e. is it actually possible to make, and can it be produced and sold profitably?
USP (Unique Selling Point)
The feature of the product that will make it different from other products on the market.
🃏 Key Term Flashcards
What is enterprise?
Enterprise
Enterprise is any attempt to start or do something new. It is about turning ideas into actions, using creativity and a willingness to try new things.
What is an entrepreneur?
Key Term
A person who takes the initiative as well as the personal and financial risk to set up a business or organisation.
What is an intrapreneur?
Key Term

Name 4 characteristics of an entrepreneur and explain each briefly.
Characteristics
Realistic risk-taker — takes calculated risks after careful research.
Innovative/creative — comes up with new ideas or ways of doing things.
Proactive — takes action rather than waiting for things to happen.
Self-confident — believes in themselves and their business idea.
Determined/resilient — doesn't give up when faced with obstacles.
Name 4 skills of an entrepreneur.
Skills
Planning and goal-setting · Time management · Communication skills · Ability to manage money · Human relations skills · Problem-solving skills · Ability to assess and manage risk · Ability to identify opportunities.
Name 3 rewards and 3 risks of self-employment.
Rewards & Risks
Rewards: You are your own boss · Keep some/all profit · Flexible hours · Sense of satisfaction.
Risks: May lose investment/personal assets · Long working hours · Income not guaranteed · May lack all necessary skills.
What are the 7 steps of the product development process?
Product Dev
1. Idea Generation · 2. Product Screening · 3. Concept Development · 4. Feasibility Study · 5. Prototype Development · 6. Test Marketing · 7. Product Launch
What is a feasibility study?
Key Term
A study carried out to see if an idea is commercially viable — (a) Is it actually possible to make this product? (b) Can it be produced and sold profitably in the current market?
What is test marketing?
Key Term
When the product is tested on a small sample of the market before going into full production. The aim is to identify possible faults and test customer reaction.
What is market research?
Key Term
Gathering, recording and analysing information about consumer preferences for a good or service in order to make informed decisions about a potential market.
Name 3 roles entrepreneurs play in the economy.
Economy
Any 3 of: Provide goods and services · Make use of resources · Create jobs and wealth · Provide training and develop skills · Raise standards of living · Taxation · Foreign trade (exports/reduce imports).
