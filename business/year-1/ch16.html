🏢 Types of Enterprise

In Chapter 15 we saw that entrepreneurs take action to set up many different organisations. What entrepreneurs do gives value for others, and the value can be economic, social or cultural.

Enterprises can be local, national or global:

📍
Local Enterprises
Operate in a specific local area only. Examples: community groups, local newspapers and local shops.
🇮🇪
National Enterprises
Operate or have branches all over Ireland. Examples: the Football Association of Ireland (FAI) and Dunnes Stores.
🌍
Global Enterprises
Very large organisations with operations all over the world. Examples: McDonald's, Coca-Cola and the Red Cross.
The Three Main Types
💰 Financial (Commercial) Enterprise
Aims to make a profit by selling a product or a service.
Examples: Banks, shops, airlines, Apple, Google, sole traders.
🤝 Social Enterprise
Puts people and community ahead of personal or commercial gain. Any surplus is reinvested.
Examples: GAA, FoodCloud, Credit unions, CoderDojo.
🎭 Cultural Enterprise
Provides access to arts, theatre, film, music, radio, literature, folklore, food, sport and cultural events.
Examples: Abbey Theatre, Electric Picnic, GAA, National Gallery shop.
💰 Financial Enterprises
Financial (or Commercial) Enterprise
Aims to make a profit by selling a product or a service. Most businesses — including banks, shops, airlines, etc. — are financial enterprises.

Some financial enterprises are set up and owned by one person (a sole trader). Others, like Apple and Google, are very large companies that operate all over the world and have thousands of owners (called shareholders) and millions of customers.

📊 The Role of Financial Enterprises in Society
  • Goods and services: Most financial enterprises provide goods and services for consumers, governments or other organisations.
  • Employment: Financial enterprises provide direct and indirect jobs.
  • Taxation: Financial enterprises pay tax to the government. This money can be used to provide public services.
  • Finance: Some financial enterprises, including banks, credit unions and An Post, provide finance for organisations and individuals.
  • Economic growth: By creating employment, paying taxes and supporting other enterprises, financial enterprises help contribute to economic growth.
Economic Growth
Occurs when there is an increase in the amount of goods and services produced in an economy from one year to the next.
🏛️ Support for Financial Enterprises
🏢 Local Enterprise Offices (LEOs)

Thirty-one Local Enterprise Offices across the country offer a range of support and services to people interested in setting up a new business. These include:

  • Financial advice to entrepreneurs and direct financial support for micro-businesses with fewer than 10 employees.
  • A mentoring service that enables entrepreneurs to seek advice from experienced business professionals.
  • A business information, advisory and training service that helps entrepreneurs to upskill in some areas of business management.
🍀 Enterprise Ireland

Enterprise Ireland is a government organisation that helps Irish start-up enterprises that have potential to create employment by exporting their goods and services to overseas markets. Services include:

  • Financial support for start-up and expanding enterprises.
  • Help with research and development (R&D) to help Irish enterprises become more innovative and competitive in international markets.
  • Help with exporting, including introductions to overseas customers and information about selling in overseas markets.
🤲 Non-Commercial Enterprises
Non-Commercial (Not-for-Profit) Enterprise
One which has not been set up for the sole purpose of making a profit. These enterprises include many charities and voluntary organisations that provide a range of services and benefits to communities.

Examples include the GAA and the Arts Council. Many not-for-profit enterprises have social and cultural goals.

🤝 Social Enterprises
Social Enterprise
One that puts people and community ahead of personal or commercial gain. Social entrepreneurs try to create jobs and tackle social, economic or environmental issues that affect the community.

Social enterprises are often owned by the community or by members. They can have both financial and social goals, but any profits (or surplus) they make are reinvested into the enterprise or the community.

The social enterprise sector in Ireland employs between 25,000 and 35,000 people in over 1,400 social enterprises. These include organisations involved in community radio, rural transport schemes, tourism, heritage products and local accommodation.

Examples of Social Enterprises
  • Young Social Innovators (YSI): Ireland's social awareness, active citizenship and education programme for 15- to 18-year-olds. Its main goal is to get young people involved in action that helps to improve the lives of others in their community.
  • Credit unions: Organisations with both financial and social goals. They exist to serve members' needs, not necessarily to maximise profits. Surplus income is returned to members as a dividend or used to improve or add services for members.
  • CoderDojo: Started in Cork in 2011, it has expanded throughout the world. Volunteers provide young people with a free introduction to programming/coding, website development and game development.
  • Macra na Feirme: A voluntary rural youth organisation providing a social outlet for members in sport, travel, public speaking, performing arts, community involvement and agriculture.
  • Focus Ireland: A non-profit organisation based in Dublin that provides services for people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness.
🚀 Enterprise in Action
FoodCloud — Aoibheann O'Brien and Iseult Ward
Social entrepreneurs Aoibheann O'Brien and Iseult Ward used their initiative and creativity to address food waste. They established FoodCloud, a social enterprise that connects businesses with surplus food to charities that can use the food. In 2012 they researched ways of matching businesses and charities and quickly realised that technology was needed to do this efficiently and on a large scale. They developed an app and website and launched FoodCloud in October 2013 with some cafés and one Tesco store. FoodCloud has since redistributed 1,585,570 kilograms of food (the equivalent of 3,488,253 meals) to charities around Ireland, with over 1,100 charities and 500 businesses on board.
💬 Did You Know?
According to the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), approximately one million tonnes of food goes to waste in Ireland each year. The amount of food wasted by businesses in Ireland each year is equivalent to over 426 Statues of Liberty!
🌱 The Role of Social Enterprises in Society
  • Tackle social problems: Raise awareness of and try to tackle social issues and problems such as homelessness, unemployment and poverty.
  • Provide services: Provide services and support for their communities by meeting community needs that are not served by the state or by commercial enterprises, e.g. Meals on Wheels, FoodCloud.
  • Volunteerism: Social enterprises offer a platform for people to volunteer and develop new skills.
  • Employment: While social enterprises often rely on volunteers, some help create employment in their local communities.
  • Finance: Some social enterprises, such as SEI and Clann Credo, provide finance for other social enterprises.
  • Enterprise culture: Help create a culture of entrepreneurship and self-help, particularly in disadvantaged communities, which encourages others to follow their example.
🏛️ Support for Social Enterprises
🌐 Social Entrepreneurs Ireland (SEI)

SEI is a not-for-profit organisation that helps social entrepreneurs by providing financial and practical support. Since it was set up in 2004, SEI funding of over €7 million has supported 264 social entrepreneurs. These projects have directly impacted 1.7 million people all over Ireland and created almost 1,500 job opportunities.

🏦 Clann Credo

Established in 1997, Clann Credo is a community development and social finance lending organisation with a national remit. A registered charity, its purpose is to enable communities deliver high social impact projects and services in their local community. In 2024, Clann Credo's loan book was €50.2m and growing.

Social Finance
A proven finance solution for community-led projects and services. A category of lending that uniquely places an equal focus on the social impact potential of a proposal along with the organisation's capacity to repay the loan.
🎭 Cultural Enterprises
Cultural Enterprise
Provides access to visual arts, theatre, film, music, radio, literature, arts and crafts, folklore, language, food, traditional sports festivals or events. Cultural organisations can be either commercial or non-commercial.

Cultural organisations are all around us. Your local community may have a library, theatre or arts centre, while GAA clubs and Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann support Irish culture by promoting Irish sport, music, singing, dancing and language.

Examples of Cultural Enterprises
  • GAA: The Gaelic Athletic Association is an Irish amateur sporting and cultural organisation that focuses on promoting Irish games and pastimes including hurling, camogie, Gaelic football, Gaelic handball and rounders. Since GAA clubs are so widespread in Ireland and the organisation is community based, it could also be seen as a social enterprise.
  • Abbey Theatre: Also known as Ireland's national theatre, the Abbey Theatre was established in 1904. It aims to promote Irish writers and artists and to produce a programme of interesting and exciting Irish and international theatre each year.
  • Electric Picnic: This annual music and arts festival takes place in Stradbally, Co. Laois. It attracts over 50,000 people each year.
  • National Gallery of Ireland shop: The National Gallery does not charge an entrance fee, but its shop sells a range of goods such as books, greetings cards, umbrellas and stationery inspired by the gallery's collections.
  • Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann: Fleadh cheoil means 'feast of music'. This traditional Irish music festival is an annual event run by Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann, also featuring the national finals for a range of traditional music and singing competitions.
  • Local cultural enterprise events: Events such as festivals take place throughout Ireland and promote the local economy. Examples include the Kilkenny Cat Laughs Comedy Festival, the Cork Jazz Festival, the Galway Arts Festival, the Rose of Tralee Festival and the Taste of Donegal Food Festival.
🌟 The Role of Cultural Enterprises in Society
  • Promote arts and culture: Cultural enterprises support and promote Irish culture, especially in music, sport and literature. They also help to make sure that people can get to experience cultural events.
  • Create employment: Many cultural enterprises employ full-time staff to support their day-to-day operations.
  • Support local economy: Cultural enterprises can have a very positive impact on a local economy through direct employment, promoting tourism and staging large events such as festivals.
  • Entertainment: Cultural enterprises are not all about money and making a profit. Very often they exist to provide entertainment for people, and this can have a positive impact on people's lives and wellbeing.
🏛️ Support for Cultural Enterprises
🌐 Culture Ireland

Culture Ireland is a division of the Department of Tourism, Culture, Arts, Gaeltacht, Sport and Media. It is responsible for promoting Irish arts worldwide. It creates and supports opportunities for Irish artists and companies to present their work at international showcases and venues. It supports a range of artistic and cultural activities including visual art, theatre, literature, music, dance, opera and film.

🎨 Arts Council of Ireland

The Arts Council of Ireland is a government organisation with responsibility for developing the arts. It works in partnership with artists, art organisations and the government to promote and support the arts in Irish life.
