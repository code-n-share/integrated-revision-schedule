📖 Key Terms at a Glance
TermMeaning (plain English)
Contour lineA line on a map joining places of the same height. The numbers (10, 20, 30…) are heights.
Even slopeA slope that is the same steepness all the way up; the contour lines are evenly spaced out.
Concave slopeContour lines spaced out at the base and closer together as they near the peak. Gentle at first, then steep.
Convex slopeContour lines closer together at the base and more spread out as they near the peak. Steep at first, then gentle near the top.
Compound slopeContour lines are sometimes spaced out and sometimes close together: steep in places and not steep in others. Also called a stepped slope.
Aerial photographA photograph taken from the air (the notes show it taken from a helicopter).
Vertical aerial photoTaken from directly above, looking straight down at the ground. Location is described with the compass points (NW, North, NE…).
Oblique aerial photoTaken from the air at an angle (looking slantways). Location is described with left / centre / right and foreground / middle ground / background.
ForegroundThe part of an oblique photo nearest the camera (bottom of the photo).
BackgroundThe part of an oblique photo furthest from the camera (top of the photo).
⛰️ Types of Slopes
A slope's shape is shown on a map by its contour lines. Close together = steep. Spread out = gentle. There are four types: even, concave, compound and convex.
💡 Think of contours as a staircase seen from above: tightly packed stairs = a steep climb; wide-spaced stairs = an easy walk.
SlopeContour linesSteepness
EvenEvenly spaced outThe same all the way up
ConcaveSpaced out at the base, closer together near the peakGentle at first, then steep
ConvexCloser together at the base, more spread out near the peakSteep at first, then gentle near the top
Compound (stepped)Sometimes spaced out, sometimes close togetherSteep in places, not steep in others
🧩 Remember it: CON-CAVE is like a cave / bowl: the ground dips in, so you start gentle and it gets steep at the top. CON-VEX is like a bulge / dome: steep at the start, rounding off gently at the top. Compound slopes come in steps.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up concave and convex. Always check where the lines are close together: near the peak = concave; near the base = convex.
🚁 Types of Aerial Photographs
There are two types of aerial photograph: vertical (the camera points straight down) and oblique (the camera points down at an angle).
💡 Vertical = a bird looking straight down at a map. Oblique = the view from a plane window, looking out and down at the scene in front of you.
🧭 Describing Location on an Aerial Photo
Imagine the photo split into a 3 × 3 grid (nine boxes). Name the box where the feature is, using the words for that type of photo.
  • Vertical photo: compass points: NW, North, NE / West, Centre, East / SW, South, SE.
  • Oblique photo: Left / Centre / Right + background, middle ground or foreground.
📌 Worked example

A church is in the top-right corner of a vertical photo → it is in the NE.

A lake is in the bottom-left corner of an oblique photo → it is in the left foreground.

⚠️ Common mistake: using compass directions (NE, SW) on an oblique photo, or "foreground" on a vertical one. Match the words to the photo type. Also: the foreground is at the bottom and the background at the top.

❓ Quick Quiz

Contour lines on a map are very close together. What does this show?

  1. A gentle slope
  2. A steep slope
  3. Flat land
  4. The sea
Close together = a short horizontal distance for each height gain = steep.

Contour lines are spaced out at the base and get closer together near the peak. Which slope is this?

  1. Convex
  2. Even
  3. Concave
  4. Compound
Concave: gentle first, then steep. Lines are close together near the peak.

A slope is steep at first and then gentle near the top. Which type is it?

  1. Convex
  2. Concave
  3. Even
  4. Stepped
Convex: contours are close together at the base and spread out near the peak.

Which slope is also called a “stepped slope”?

  1. Even
  2. Convex
  3. Concave
  4. Compound
A compound slope has steep and gentle sections, like steps.

Evenly spaced contour lines show which type of slope?

  1. Convex
  2. Even
  3. Concave
  4. Compound
Same spacing = same steepness all the way up = even slope.

Which aerial photo is taken looking straight down from above?

  1. Oblique
  2. Horizontal
  3. Vertical
  4. Diagonal
Vertical photos are taken from directly above. Oblique photos are taken at an angle.

A farm is in the top-left corner of a vertical aerial photo. Where is it?

  1. NW
  2. Left background
  3. North
  4. West
Vertical photos use compass points. Top-left = NW (the top-middle box would be North).

A road is in the bottom-right of an oblique aerial photo. Where is it?

  1. SE
  2. Right background
  3. Right middle ground
  4. Right foreground
Oblique photos use left/centre/right + foreground, middle ground, background. Bottom = foreground.

In an oblique photo, which part is furthest from the camera?

  1. Foreground
  2. Background
  3. Middle ground
  4. Centre
The background is at the top of the photo, furthest from the camera.
✏️ Practice Questions

Written from your class notes. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

1. Name the four types of slope.

Even, concave, convex and compound (also called a stepped slope).

2. Describe how the contour lines on a concave slope look, and what the slope is like.

The contour lines are spaced out at the base and get closer together as they near the peak. The slope is gentle at first and then gets steep.

3. Describe a convex slope and say how it differs from a concave slope.

A convex slope has contour lines closer together at the base and more spread out near the peak: steep at first, then gentle.

A concave slope is the opposite: gentle at first, then steep, with lines close together near the peak.

4. Draw a quick sketch of a compound slope with contour lines. Explain its shape.

Sketch: a profile that goes up in steps (see the diagram above), with contour lines close together on the steep parts and spread out on the gentle parts.

The slope is steep in places and not steep in others, so it is also called a stepped slope.

5. What is the difference between a vertical and an oblique aerial photograph?

A vertical photo is taken from directly above, looking straight down. An oblique photo is taken from the air looking down at an angle.

6. Describe two ways to give the location of a feature on an aerial photo. Give an example of each.

Vertical photo: use compass points, e.g. “the church is in the NE” (or North, East, Centre…).

Oblique photo: use left / centre / right plus foreground / middle ground / background, e.g. “the lake is in the left foreground”.
