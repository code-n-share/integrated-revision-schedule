⛰️ Ch 1 · Slopes & Aerial Photographs ⏱ 8 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
Contour lineA line on a map joining places of the same height.
Even slopeSame steepness all the way up; contour lines evenly spaced out.
Concave slopeContours spaced out at the base, closer together near the peak: gentle, then steep.
Convex slopeContours close together at the base, spread out near the peak: steep, then gentle.
Compound (stepped) slopeContours sometimes spaced out, sometimes close together: steep in places, not in others.
Vertical aerial photoTaken from directly above, looking straight down. Location given with compass points.
Oblique aerial photoTaken from the air at an angle. Location: left/centre/right + foreground/middle ground/background.
Foreground / backgroundForeground = bottom of an oblique photo, nearest the camera. Background = top, furthest away.

🏭 Ch 2 · Secondary Economic Activities ⏱ 10 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
Primary economic activitiesThe extraction of raw materials.
Secondary economic activitiesActivities that take raw materials and manufacture a product.
Skilled labourWork that requires third-level qualifications.
Semi-skilled labourWorkers who are trained on the job.
LinkagesA company relies on another company's products, so they locate near each other (bakery near flour mill).
IDAIndustrial Development Authority: works to attract industry to Ireland.
Heavy industryRaw materials and products are bulky and heavy, e.g. iron and steel making.
Light industryMakes small, lightweight products, e.g. electronics and healthcare products.
Footloose industryNot tied to one place; usually light industries that locate close to cities.
Industrial estateA site at the edge of an urban area set aside for industries, e.g. Sandyford Business Park.
Industrial inertiaAn industry doesn't relocate even though it might make sense to, due to historical ties.

2 · Must remember

Every factory: Inputs → Processes → Outputs (crisp factory)

Inputs: potatoes, flavouring, labour→Processes: cutting, cooking→Outputs: crisps, waste

9 location factors (name it, then say why)

FactorWhy it matters
Raw materialsBulky/heavy → locate near them to cut transport costs
TransportAir, sea, road, rail to receive materials and ship products
ServicesWater, electricity, WiFi, broadband
LabourTowns/cities give a workforce; universities give skilled workers
MarketLocate near where the product is sold

🧩 Remember it: “Really Tall Sheep Like Mixing Lovely Cold Green Peas” = Raw materials, Transport, Services, Labour, Market, Linkages, Capital, Government, Personal.
  • Why locate on an industrial estate? Cheaper land and room to expand · services (water, electricity, fast broadband) · motorway access · beside suppliers of semi-finished products.
  • Footloose: factories once had to be near coal for energy; now they are no longer tied down.
  • Inertia example: Sheffield steel: coal mines closed, but the steel factories stayed, used new energy sources and made new products (e.g. surgical instruments).
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Naming a location factor isn't enough: explain why it matters.
  • Labour is an input; waste is an output.
  • Inertia means the industry stays where it is, not that it closes down. Industrial estates are at the edge of towns, not the centre.

🏞️ Ch 4 · Rivers ⏱ 8 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
Course of a riverThe journey of a river from its source to its mouth.
Youthful stageThe upper course.
Mature stageThe middle course.
Elderly stageThe lower course.
Hydraulic actionThe force of water hitting against the surface.
AbrasionScraping or wearing away by the river load.
AttritionRocks in the load hit each other and break each other down.
SolutionMinerals are dissolved in the water and carried along.
WaterfallWhere a river flows over a vertical slope; a feature of erosion (Powerscourt, Co. Wicklow).
Plunge poolPool at a waterfall's base, formed by hydraulic action and the swirling load (abrasion).
UndercutRock above the plunge pool is worn away underneath until it collapses.

2 · Must remember

StageCourseFeatures
YouthfulUpperV-shaped valley · interlocking spurs · waterfalls
MatureMiddleMeanders · wide valleys · ox-bow lakes · flood plains
ElderlyLowerOx-bow lakes · flood plains · deltas · levees
🧩 Remember it: Y-M-E = Youthful, Mature, Elderly = Upper, Middle, Lower. Erosion = H-A-A-S: Hydraulic action, Abrasion, Attrition, Solution. Abrasion = load Attacks the bed and banks; aTtrition = load hiTs itself.

How a waterfall forms (H-S-P-U-C)

Hard rock (granite) over soft rock (limestone)→Soft rock erodes faster: vertical drop→Plunge pool (hydraulic action + abrasion)→Rock above is undercut→Collapses into the pool→Waterfall retreats upstream

Diagram labels: hard rock, soft rock, plunge pool, an arrow showing the retreat. Example: Powerscourt, Co. Wicklow.

The notes name the other features (V-shaped valley, meanders, deltas…) but don't yet explain how they form: learn which stage each belongs to.

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Abrasion = load scrapes the bed and banks; attrition = rocks hit each other.
  • A waterfall moves upstream (backwards), and always starts with hard rock over soft rock.
  • Give both names for a stage: youthful = upper, mature = middle, elderly = lower.

3 · Quick check say the answer, then tap the card to check

What is the course of a river?
Its journey from source to mouth

Youthful (upper), mature (middle), elderly (lower)
Name the four processes of river erosion
Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution

  2. Even

  1. SE
  2. Right background
  3. Right middle ground
  4. Right foreground
Oblique photos use left/centre/right + foreground/middle ground/background. Bottom = foreground. SE is for vertical photos.

Which aerial photo is taken looking straight down from above?

  1. Oblique
  2. Vertical
  3. Horizontal
  4. Compound
Vertical = directly above. Oblique = at an angle.

A bakery locating near a flour mill is an example of which factor?

  1. Market
  2. Capital
  3. Services
  4. Linkages
The bakery relies on another company's product: linkages.

Sheffield's steel factories stayed after the coal mines closed. This is…

  1. Industrial inertia
  2. Footloose industry
  3. Linkages
  4. Foreign direct investment
Inertia: the industry doesn't relocate, due to historical ties.

What is skilled labour?

  1. Workers trained on the job

  4. Unpaid workers

What does Aughinish Alumina produce?

  1. Aluminium
  2. Bauxite
  3. Alumina powder

It turns bauxite into alumina, a semi-finished powder; smelters in the UK make the aluminium.

Which is NOT a reason MNCs such as Google locate in Ireland?

  2. A highly educated workforce
  3. Low corporation tax (12.5%)

E-M-E-E-T: EU, markets & labour, educated workforce, English, low tax.

Which is a negative of incinerators such as Poolbeg?

  1. They save space on landfills
  2. Fumes may be carcinogenic
  3. They provide electricity
  4. They create jobs
The other three are positives.

Rocks in the river load hit each other and break down. This is…

  1. Abrasion
  2. Attrition
  3. Solution
  4. Hydraulic action
Attrition = rock against rock. Abrasion = load scraping the bed and banks.

Which feature belongs to the youthful stage?

  1. Waterfall
  2. Delta
  3. Levee
  4. Meander
Youthful: V-shaped valley, interlocking spurs, waterfalls. Meanders = mature; deltas and levees = elderly.

Over time, a waterfall…

  1. Moves downstream
  2. Stays in the same place
  3. Retreats upstream
  4. Turns into a delta
Undercut rock collapses into the plunge pool, so the waterfall erodes its way upstream.
