🏞️ The Course and the Three Stages of a River
The journey of a river from its source to its mouth is called the course of the river. It has three stages: youthful (upper course), mature (middle course) and elderly (lower course).
💡 A river ages like a person: young and energetic near the mountains, mature in the middle, slow and elderly as it reaches the sea.
StageAlso calledFeatures include
1 YouthfulUpper courseV-shaped valley · interlocking spurs · waterfalls
2 MatureMiddle courseMeanders · wide valleys · ox-bow lakes · flood plains
3 ElderlyLower courseOx-bow lakes · flood plains · deltas · levees
🧩 Remember it: Y-M-E = Youthful, Mature, Elderly = Upper, Middle, Lower. Waterfalls come near the start; deltas come at the end.
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up the stage names and the course names. Youthful = upper, mature = middle, elderly = lower. Ox-bow lakes and flood plains appear in both the mature and elderly stages in your notes.
🌊 River Erosion
Rivers wear away (erode) their bed and banks in four ways: hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution.
ProcessWhat happens
1Hydraulic actionThe force of water hitting against the surface
2AbrasionScraping or wearing away by the river load
3AttritionThe impact of the force against the rocks hitting each other breaks them down
4SolutionMinerals are dissolved in the water and carried along
🧩 Remember it: H-A-A-S = Hydraulic action, Abrasion, Attrition, Solution.
Abrasion = the load Attacks the bed and banks (river against rock). Attrition = the load Argues with itself (rock against rock).
⚠️ Common mistake: mixing up abrasion and attrition. Abrasion = load scrapes the river’s bed and banks. Attrition = rocks in the load hit each other and break down.
💦 Waterfalls: A Feature of River Erosion
A waterfall occurs where a river flows over a vertical slope. It is a feature of erosion. Example: Powerscourt, Co. Wicklow.
💡 It’s like a stair step in the river: the soft rock underneath wears away faster, leaving the hard rock sticking out above it.

How a waterfall forms:

  1. The river flows over an area where hard rock (e.g. granite) lies on top of soft rock (e.g. limestone).
  2. The river erodes the soft rock faster, so a vertical drop develops.
  3. At the base, a plunge pool develops due to hydraulic action, along with the river’s load swirling around at the base, which wears away the base by abrasion.
  4. The rock above the plunge pool is undercut. It eventually collapses into the plunge pool below.
  5. The waterfall slowly erodes its way upstream (it “retreats backwards”).
🧩 Remember it: H-S-P-U-C: Hard over Soft → Plunge pool → Undercut → Collapse → retreat upstream.
⚠️ Common mistake: forgetting that the waterfall moves upstream (backwards), or leaving out the hard rock over soft rock that starts it. In a diagram, label hard rock, soft rock, plunge pool and the retreat.

✏️ Practice Questions

Written from your class notes. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

1. Name the three stages of a river and give two features of each.

Youthful (upper course): V-shaped valley, interlocking spurs, waterfalls.

Mature (middle course): meanders, wide valleys, ox-bow lakes, flood plains.

Elderly (lower course): ox-bow lakes, flood plains, deltas, levees.

2. Name and explain the four processes of river erosion.

Hydraulic action: the force of water hitting against the surface. Abrasion: scraping or wearing away by the river load. Attrition: rocks hit each other and break each other down. Solution: minerals are dissolved in the water and carried along.

3. Explain, with the aid of a labelled diagram, how a waterfall is formed.

Diagram: label hard rock over soft rock, the vertical drop, the plunge pool, and an arrow showing the waterfall retreating upstream.

(1) The river flows over hard rock (e.g. granite) on top of soft rock (e.g. limestone). (2) The soft rock erodes faster, developing a vertical drop. (3) A plunge pool develops at the base by hydraulic action; the swirling load wears the base by abrasion. (4) The rock above is undercut and eventually collapses into the plunge pool. (5) The waterfall slowly erodes its way upstream.

4. Name an example of a waterfall in Ireland and say what type of feature it is.

Powerscourt, Co. Wicklow: a feature of river erosion, where a river flows over a vertical slope.

5. What is the difference between abrasion and attrition?

Abrasion is the scraping or wearing away of the bed and banks by the river load. Attrition is the rocks of the load hitting each other and breaking each other down.

6. What is meant by the “course” of a river?

The journey of a river from its source to its mouth, divided into the youthful (upper), mature (middle) and elderly (lower) stages.
