🏛️ Ch 1 · The Iliad: Background, Characters & Gods ⏱ 12 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
EpicA long poem about heroes and great events; the Iliad is an ancient Greek epic.
Dactylic hexameterThe verse form the Iliad is written in.
In medias resLatin for “in the middle of things”: the Iliad starts in the middle of the story.
EpithetA descriptive phrase attached to a name, often a compound adjective, e.g. “brilliant Achilles”.
Formal speechA lengthy, formal speech: a typical element of the Homeric epic form.
Iliad“A poem about Ilium” (Ilium = Troy).
AchilleidAnother possible title: the poem's main focus is Achilles and his rage.
Trojan WarGreeks v Trojans over Troy; the Iliad covers only the 10th and final year.
AchaeansAnother name for the Greeks.
CassandraPriam's daughter with the gift of prophecy (given by Apollo).
ErisGoddess of discord; the one god not invited to the wedding of Peleus and Thetis.
ProtesilausSuitor of Helen; first casualty of the war, killed by Hector (“first to be slain”).

2 · Must remember

Cause of the war

Eris not invited to Peleus & Thetis' wedding→Golden apple “For the fairest”→Paris picks Aphrodite→She gives him Helen→Paris takes Helen from Sparta
🧩 The three offers: Hera = power (king of Europe and Asia) · Athena = victory (win the war) · Aphrodite = love (Helen). Paris picked love.

Hecuba's dream: she gives birth to a flaming torch → Cassandra: kill the baby (Paris) or Troy will be destroyed.

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Epithet: say compound adjective + example + why (oral poetry; quick; repetition helps the audience).
  • The Iliad covers only the 10th year, mainly the quarrel of Agamemnon and Achilles.
  • Chryseis = Apollo's priest's daughter; Briseis = Achilles' prize. Zeus is neutral.

🏹 Ch 2 · The Iliad Books 1–2: Plague, Quarrel & Hardship ⏱ 12 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
PlagueDeadly sickness sent on the Greek camp by Apollo's arrows after his priest's prayer.
ChrysesApollo's priest; his daughter Chryseis was taken by the Greeks.
ChryseisThe priest's daughter, Agamemnon's prize; returned to end the plague.
BriseisAchilles' prize, which Agamemnon takes in exchange.
MyrmidonsAchilles' troops, the best warriors, whom he recalls to camp.
ThetisAchilles' mother; she asks Zeus to help the Trojans.
Unbreakable vowZeus bows his head to agree to Thetis' request.
Deceptive dreamSent by Zeus to Agamemnon in the form of Nestor: attack at once.
Divine interventionGods directly interfere with human affairs.
Testing the armyAgamemnon tests his troops' morale by telling them to sail home.
ThersitesCommon soldier who criticises Agamemnon's greed, pride and treatment of Achilles.
Epic simileA long comparison, e.g. soldiers compared to bees, birds and fire.
Catalogue of ShipsA long list of all the Greek forces in Book 2.

2 · Must remember

Book 1: the quarrel

Chryses prays to Apollo→Plague arrows→Chryseis returned→Agamemnon takes Briseis→Achilles dishonoured, withdraws with Myrmidons→Thetis asks Zeus→Zeus bows head (vow)

Hera argues with Zeus; Hephaestus calms her.

Book 2: hardship of war

Deceptive dream (as Nestor)→Agamemnon tests army: “sail home”→Troops run to ships→Hera → Athena → Odysseus restore order→Thersites criticises→Nestor's advice
  • Social hierarchy: gods → kings → nobles → common warriors.
  • Nestor's advice (3 points): the oath was sworn; keep fighting; organise for battle.
Simile (“B-B-F”)Shows
BeesNumbers and organisation
BirdsScale, noise, energy
FirePower, destruction, fear

Catalogue of Ships: shows scale, national pride, historical significance; Agamemnon's force is the largest.

⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • Achilles withdraws because he is dishonoured (Briseis taken), not because of the plague or fear.
  • The dream only looks like Nestor: it is deceptive and sent by Zeus.
  • “Sail home” was a test of morale, not a real order; it backfired.

3 · Quick check say the answer, then tap the card to check

Why did Apollo send the plague?
His priest Chryses prayed to him after the Greeks took his daughter Chryseis
Why does Achilles withdraw from the fighting?
He is dishonoured when Agamemnon takes his prize, Briseis
What does Thetis ask Zeus, and how does he agree?
To help the Trojans; he bows his head (unbreakable vow)
Who restores order when the troops run for the ships?
Odysseus (Hera sends Athena to him)
What do the bees, birds and fire similes show?
Bees: numbers/organisation. Birds: scale/noise/energy. Fire: power/destruction/fear
⚔️ Ch 3 · The Iliad Books 4–11: The War Resumes ⏱ 12 min

1 · Key terms read each out loud, then cover the right column

TermExam definition
TrucePeace agreement between both sides after the duel of Paris and Menelaus.
KleosGreek word for glory.
PandarusTrojan archer tricked by Athena into shooting Menelaus, breaking the truce.
DiomedesGreek hero of Book 5; given courage, strength and skill by Athena; wounds Aphrodite and Ares.
AeneasAphrodite's son; attacked by Diomedes, rescued by Apollo.
HecubaHector's mother; offers him wine, which he refuses.
AndromacheHector's wife; begs him not to return to battle.
AstyanaxHector's baby son, frightened by his father's helmet.
DuelOne-to-one fight: Hector v Ajax in Book 7.
Defensive wallBuilt by the Greeks around their ships for protection (Book 7).
EmbassyOdysseus, Ajax and Phoenix try to persuade Achilles to return (Book 9).
DolonTrojan spy captured, questioned and killed by Odysseus and Diomedes.
RhesusLeader of Trojan allies whose famous horses are stolen (Book 10).

2 · Must remember

BookWhat happens
4Athena tricks Pandarus (promise of kleos) → he shoots Menelaus → truce broken
5Diomedes, inspired by Athena, wounds Aphrodite and Ares
6Hector: asks the women to pray to Athena, refuses wine, scolds Paris, kind to Helen, farewell to Andromache & Astyanax (duty v family)
7Hector v Ajax: equal, ends at night, gifts exchanged; Greeks build a wall round the ships
8Zeus forbids the gods to fight → Greeks lose; Trojans camp outside
9Embassy (Odysseus, Ajax, Phoenix) offers wealth, honour, rewards → Achilles refuses
10Odysseus + Diomedes kill Dolon, steal Rhesus' horses
11Agamemnon attacks; Agamemnon, Diomedes, Odysseus wounded; Achilles sends Patroclus: the tide turns
🧩 Remember it: envoys = O-A-P (Odysseus, Ajax, Phoenix): wealth, honour, rewards. Answer: no, “no gift can repair the insult”.
⚠️ Don't lose marks
  • The truce is broken by one arrow (Pandarus, tricked by Athena), not a battle.
  • Nobody wins the Book 7 duel: Hector and Ajax are equal and exchange gifts.
  • Achilles refuses because no gift can repair the insult, not because the gifts are small. Dolon is a Trojan spy.
