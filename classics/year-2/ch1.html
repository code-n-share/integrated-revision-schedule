✍️ Epic Form & Homeric Style
The Iliad starts in medias res: “in the middle of things”. The poem does not begin at the start of the war.
💡 Like pressing play on a film halfway through: you meet the story already in progress.
Homeric epithets are often compound adjectives. They came about as a result of composing and listening to oral poetry.
  • Examples: “brilliant Achilles”, “Hector breaker of horses”.
  • They let the poet describe a character or object quickly and economically, in terms the audience would recognise.
  • The repetition of familiar expressions also helped the audience follow the narrative.
💡 Like a nickname that sticks: say “Hector breaker of horses” and everyone instantly knows who and what he is.
The lengthy, formal speech is another typical element of the Homeric epic form.
⚠️ Common mistake: explaining an epithet as just “a describing word”. For full marks say it is often a compound adjective, give an example and say why it was used (oral poetry; quick and economical; repetition helps the audience follow).
📜 Introduction & Background
The Iliad is an ancient Greek epic poem written in dactylic hexameter. It narrates an episode of the Trojan War, fought between the Greeks and the Trojans. It centres on the battles and events during the weeks of quarrel between King Agamemnon and the warrior Achilles.
  • “Iliad” is a word that means “a poem about Ilium” (another name for Troy).
  • The poem could be called the Achilleid: its primary focus is Achilles and his rage.

The Trojan War

  • The Iliad takes place in the 10th and final year of the Trojan War, the greatest conflict in Greek mythology.
  • The war was fought between the Greeks and Trojans, upon the city of Troy on Asia Minor (Turkey).

Background in the Trojan War: Hecuba's dream

Troy ruled by King Priam & Queen Hecuba→ Hecuba dreams while pregnant: she gives birth to a flaming torch→ Cassandra (gift of prophecy) interprets it→ The new-born son (Paris) must be killed or he will lead to the destruction of Troy
💡 The flaming torch is a warning in picture form: fire = Troy burning.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying the Iliad tells the whole Trojan War. It covers only the 10th (final) year, and mainly the quarrel of Agamemnon and Achilles.

🍎 Prologue: Cause of the War
The prologue explains how the war began: a golden apple thrown by Eris, and a choice made by Paris.
  1. A wedding banquet for Peleus and Thetis. All Olympian gods are invited except Eris, goddess of discord.
  2. Eris vows revenge and strife for the insult. She throws a golden apple inscribed “For the fairest”.
  3. The apple lands in Paris's hands. Three goddesses make him an offer:
    • Hera: “I promise to make you king of Europe and Asia.”
    • Athena: “You will lead the Trojans against the Greeks and win.”
    • Aphrodite: “You will get the prettiest woman on Earth.”
  4. Paris chooses Aphrodite, who gives him Helen. Paris leaves his wife.

Cause of the war

Paris goes to Sparta as an ally→ Menelaus and Helen welcome him→ Paris breaks his trust and snatches Helen with the help of Aphrodite

Landing at Troy

  • Protesilaus was a suitor of Helen, fulfilling his vow, and was the first casualty of the war, killed by Hector. His name means “first to be slain”.
🧩 Remember it: the three offers = Hera Power (king), Athena Victory (war), Aphrodite Love (Helen). Paris picked love, and that started a ten-year war.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Paris won Helen in a contest. He chose Aphrodite as fairest; she gave him Helen. And he broke Menelaus' trust (a guest who repays hospitality by taking the host's wife).

  2. Homeric epithet

✏️ Practice Questions

Written from your class notes. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

1. Explain what is meant by “in medias res” and say how it applies to the Iliad. (3 marks)

In medias res means “in the middle of things”. The Iliad does not begin at the start of the Trojan War: it starts in the 10th and final year, during the weeks of quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles.

2. What is a Homeric epithet? Give two examples and explain why Homer used them. (6 marks)

An epithet is a descriptive phrase attached to a name, often a compound adjective. It came about from composing and listening to oral poetry.

Examples: “brilliant Achilles”; “Hector breaker of horses”.

Why: they let the poet describe a character or object quickly and economically in terms the audience would recognise, and the repetition of familiar expressions helped the audience follow the narrative.

3. Outline the background of the Iliad: what the poem is, which war it is about and which part of the war it covers. (5 marks)

The Iliad is an ancient Greek epic poem written in dactylic hexameter. “Iliad” means “a poem about Ilium” (Troy). It narrates an episode of the Trojan War between the Greeks and the Trojans, the greatest conflict in Greek mythology, on the city of Troy in Asia Minor (Turkey). It takes place in the 10th and final year and centres on the weeks of quarrel between King Agamemnon and the warrior Achilles. It could be called the Achilleid because its focus is Achilles and his rage.

4. Describe how the Trojan War began. (Prologue and cause of the war) (8 marks)
  • At the wedding banquet of Peleus and Thetis all the Olympian gods were invited except Eris, goddess of discord.
  • She vowed revenge and strife for the insult and threw a golden apple inscribed “For the fairest”.
  • It landed in Paris's hands. Hera promised to make him king of Europe and Asia; Athena promised he would lead the Trojans against the Greeks and win; Aphrodite promised the prettiest woman on Earth.
  • Paris chose Aphrodite, who gave him Helen, and he left his wife.
  • Paris went to Sparta as an ally; Menelaus and Helen welcomed him; he broke his trust and snatched Helen with the help of Aphrodite.
5. Name the gods who support the Greeks, the gods who support the Trojans and three neutral gods. (4 marks)

Greeks (Achaeans): Hera, Athena, Iris. Trojans: Artemis, Apollo, Aphrodite, Ares. Neutral (any three): Zeus, Hades, Hermes, Poseidon, Hephaestus.

6. Choose any four characters from the Greek side and any four from the Trojan side. For each say who they are. (8 marks)

Greek (examples): Agamemnon: king of Mycenae and leader of the Greeks. Achilles: leader of the Myrmidons, half-divine hero. Odysseus: king of Ithaca, the wiliest commander. Nestor: king of Pylos, Agamemnon's trusted adviser.

Trojan (examples): Hector: son of Priam and foremost Trojan warrior. Priam: aged king of Troy. Paris: Helen's lover-abductor. Pandarus: famous archer, son of Lycaon.
