🏹 Book 4: The Truce is Broken
  • After the duel between Paris and Menelaus, the war should have ended. Menelaus won fairly and should have got Helen back for the Greeks.
  • The gods disagree: Hera and Athena want Troy destroyed; Zeus considers ending the war.
Athena travels down and tricks Pandarus→ Says he will gain glory by shooting Menelaus→ Pandarus wounds Menelaus: truce broken
  • The Greeks are furious. Agamemnon fears his brother will die and worries the Greeks will be shamed if they go home without victory.
  • Menelaus' wound is not serious. The truce is now over and both armies prepare for war again.
  • Agamemnon walks among the troops, praising brave soldiers and criticising weak soldiers.
  • At last the two armies advance and fighting begins again. The book ends with Greeks and Trojans clashing in a fierce battle.
KLEOS = GLORY. Heroes fight for glory; Athena uses Pandarus' desire for glory to trick him.
💡 Kleos is the fame that makes a warrior's name live on after he dies.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying the Trojans or the Greeks broke the truce by a battle. It was one arrow: Pandarus (tricked by Athena) wounded Menelaus.
🛡️ Book 5: Diomedes, Hero of the Greeks
  1. Athena helps Diomedes: she gives him courage, strength and skill in battle, and the ability to recognise gods in battle.
  2. Inspired by the goddess, he becomes the most dangerous warrior on the battlefield. He charges into the Trojan army and defeats many famous Trojan warriors. His bravery inspires the Greeks and terrifies the Trojans.
  3. Diomedes attacks Aeneas. Aphrodite tries to protect her son and Diomedes wounds her hand with a spear. She flees to Olympus and the other gods mock her for entering the battlefield.
  4. Apollo rescues Aeneas and protects him from further harm.
  5. Ares joins the Trojans. Athena helps Diomedes guide his spear towards Ares.
  6. Diomedes wounds the god of war. Ares retreats to Olympus, complaining to Zeus about his injury.
  7. Diomedes has won great glory and proven himself one of the greatest Greek warriors in the war.
🧩 Remember it: Diomedes wounds two gods: Aphrodite (hand pierced by his spear) then Ares (god of war). Athena is his helper both times.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Diomedes fights alone. He is inspired and guided by Athena, and she gives him the ability to recognise the gods.
💔 Book 6: Hector & Andromache
Trojans struggling → Hector returns to Troy to ask the women to pray to Athena→ Hecuba offers wine; Hector refuses (must stay focused on duty)→ Hector criticises Paris for avoiding his responsibilities; Paris agrees to return
  • Helen blames herself for the war and the suffering it has caused. Hector treats her kindly and doesn't place all the blame on her.
The most important scene of the book: Hector meets his wife Andromache and baby son Astyanax on the walls of Troy.
  • Andromache begs him not to return to battle: she fears he'll be killed. She has already lost her father and brothers in the war.
  • Hector admits he fears for his family and believes Troy will one day fall, but says his duty as a warrior and defender of Troy is more important than his own safety.
  • When he tries to hold his son, the baby cries, frightened by Hector's helmet. Hector removes the helmet, laughs and lovingly holds his child.
  • He prays that Astyanax will grow up to be an even greater warrior than himself.
  • After saying goodbye, Hector returns to the battlefield, knowing he may never see them again.
💡 This scene shows Hector as a loving family man as well as a warrior: duty versus family is the conflict.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Hector goes back to Troy to rest. He goes to ask the women to pray to Athena, and he refuses wine to stay focused on his duty.
⚔️ Books 7 & 8

Book 7

  • Hector challenges the Greeks to a duel. Ajax accepts.
  • The two heroes fight bravely and equally; neither can defeat the other.
  • When night falls, the duel ends and the warriors exchange gifts as a sign of respect.
  • Both sides collect their dead and hold funerals. The Greeks build a defensive wall around their ships for protection.

Book 8

  • Zeus forbids the other gods from furthering the war.
  • Without help from the gods, the Greeks begin to lose.
  • By the end of the day the Trojans camp outside the Greek position and believe they are close to winning the war.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying someone wins the duel in Book 7. Hector and Ajax fight equally, it ends at night and they exchange gifts.
🎁 Book 9: The Embassy to Achilles
  1. The Greeks are in serious trouble as the Trojans push them back towards their ships.
  2. Agamemnon admits he was wrong to insult Achilles and offers him valuable gifts if he returns to battle.
  3. Odysseus, Ajax and Phoenix are sent to persuade Achilles. They promise him wealth, honour and rewards.
  4. Achilles refuses. He is still angry with Agamemnon and believes no gift can repair the insult he has suffered.
🧩 Remember it: the three envoys = O-A-P (Odysseus, Ajax, Phoenix): offers: wealth, honour, rewards. Answer: no.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Achilles refuses because the gifts are too small. He refuses because no gift can repair the insult.
🌙 Books 10 & 11

Book 10: the night mission

  • During the night both armies are nervous and unable to sleep. The Greeks decide to send spies into the Trojan camp.
  • Odysseus and Diomedes volunteer for the dangerous mission.
  • On their journey they capture a Trojan spy, Dolon, who reveals important information about the Trojan army. After questioning him, they kill him.
  • They then attack a group of Trojan allies led by Rhesus and steal his famous horses before safely returning to the Greek camp.

Book 11: the tide turns

  • The Greeks fight back strongly. Agamemnon leads a fierce attack and kills many Trojan warriors, showing why he is the commander of the Greek army.
  • For a time it looks as if the Greeks might regain control, but the tide soon turns: one by one, important Greek warriors are wounded, including Agamemnon, Diomedes and Odysseus.
  • As the leaders are forced to leave the battlefield, the Trojans gain confidence and push the Greeks back again.
  • Meanwhile Achilles is still refusing to fight. From his camp he watches the Greeks suffer. He sends his close companion Patroclus.

✏️ Practice Questions

Written from your class notes. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

1. Explain how the truce is broken in Book 4. (6 marks)

After the duel between Paris and Menelaus the war should have ended, as Menelaus won fairly and should have got Helen back. But the gods disagree: Hera and Athena want Troy destroyed while Zeus considers ending the war. Athena travels down and tricks Pandarus, convincing him he will gain glory (kleos) if he shoots Menelaus. Pandarus wounds Menelaus, breaking the truce on both sides. The Greeks are furious, but Menelaus' wound is not serious, and both armies prepare for war again.

2. Why is Diomedes “the hero of the Greeks” in Book 5? (8 marks)
  • Athena gives him courage, strength and skill, and the ability to recognise gods.
  • He becomes the most dangerous warrior on the battlefield and defeats many famous Trojan warriors.
  • His bravery inspires the Greeks and terrifies the Trojans.
  • He wounds Aphrodite's hand with a spear while she protects Aeneas; Apollo rescues Aeneas.
  • Guided by Athena he wounds Ares, who retreats to Olympus.
  • He has won great glory and proven himself one of the greatest Greek warriors.
3. Describe the meeting of Hector, Andromache and Astyanax in Book 6. What does it show about Hector? (10 marks)

They meet on the walls of Troy. Andromache begs him not to return to battle as she has already lost her father and brothers. Hector admits he fears for his family and believes Troy will one day fall, but says his duty as a warrior and defender is more important than his safety. His baby son cries, frightened by the helmet; Hector removes it, laughs and lovingly holds him, then prays that Astyanax will grow to be an even greater warrior. He returns to battle knowing he may never see them again.

Shows: Hector is both a loving husband and father and a dutiful warrior; he puts duty first.

4. How does Hector treat Paris and Helen in Book 6? Say what this shows about Hector. (5 marks)

Hector criticises Paris for staying at home and avoiding his responsibilities while Trojan soldiers die; Paris agrees to return to battle. Helen blames herself for the war; Hector treats her kindly and does not put all the blame on her. This shows Hector's sense of duty and his fairness and kindness.

5. Describe the duel between Hector and Ajax in Book 7 and what follows it. (6 marks)

Hector challenges the Greeks to a duel and Ajax accepts. They fight bravely and equally and neither can win. When night falls the duel ends and they exchange gifts as a sign of respect. Both sides collect their dead and hold funerals, and the Greeks build a defensive wall around their ships.

6. Why do the Greeks begin to lose in Book 8, and what does this lead to in Book 9? (6 marks)

Zeus forbids the other gods from furthering the war. Without help from the gods the Greeks begin to lose and the Trojans camp outside the Greek position, believing they are close to winning. In Book 9 the Greeks are in serious trouble, pushed back to their ships. Agamemnon admits he was wrong to insult Achilles and offers gifts; Odysseus, Ajax and Phoenix promise Achilles wealth, honour and rewards, but he refuses because no gift can repair the insult.

7. Outline what happens in Books 10 and 11. (8 marks)

Book 10: both armies are nervous at night. Odysseus and Diomedes volunteer to spy on the Trojans. They capture the Trojan spy Dolon, who reveals information, then kill him. They attack Trojan allies led by Rhesus, steal his famous horses and return safely.

Book 11: the Greeks fight back and Agamemnon leads a fierce attack. Then the tide turns: Agamemnon, Diomedes and Odysseus are wounded, and the Trojans push the Greeks back. Achilles is still refusing to fight, watches the Greeks suffer and sends his close companion Patroclus.
