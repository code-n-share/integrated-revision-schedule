🏹 Book 1: The Plague
Greeks have stolen women from Troy and nearby. Agamemnon takes Chryseis→ Chryses, priest of Apollo, prays for revenge→ Apollo shoots deadly arrows of plague into the Greek camp
  • The Greeks and Trojans had been fighting for almost 9 years.
  • The Greeks stole all the women from strongholds in and near Troy.
  • Agamemnon took Chryseis and Achilles took Briseis.
  • Chryseis is the daughter of Apollo's priest. The priest prayed to Apollo, and Apollo sent the plague to the Greek camp.
💡 Insult a priest and you insult his god: Apollo punishes the whole army for one man's greed.
⚔️ Book 1: Achilles v Agamemnon
  1. The people beg Agamemnon to give Chryseis back. He agrees only if he gets Achilles' prize (Briseis).
  2. Achilles feels incredibly dishonoured and stops fighting for the Greeks. (Athena calms him down.)
  3. Agamemnon gives Chryseis back and takes Briseis.
  4. Achilles recalls his troops, the Myrmidons (the best warriors), back to camp.
  5. Achilles asks his mother Thetis to ask Zeus to take the Trojans' side and make the Greeks feel they will never win without him.
  6. Thetis listens and does so when Zeus returns. Zeus agrees and bows his head (an unbreakable vow).
  7. Hera sees this and gets very mad at Zeus. They argue. Hephaestus calms them down and asks them to take a drink.
The quarrel is the heart of the poem: Agamemnon takes Achilles' prize, Achilles feels dishonoured, withdraws himself and the Myrmidons, and asks the gods to make the Greeks lose without him.
💡 Like the best player taking his ball and going home: the whole team suffers.
🧩 Remember it: Swap: Agamemnon gives back Chryseis (priest's daughter) and takes Briseis (Achilles' prize). Chryseis goes back, Briseis is Borrowed (taken).
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Achilles left because of the plague or because he was afraid. He withdrew because he felt dishonoured when Agamemnon took his prize, Briseis.
💭 Book 2: The Deceptive Dream
Theme of Book 2: hardship of the war. Zeus sends a deceptive dream to Agamemnon.
  • The dream takes the form of Nestor and tells Agamemnon that the gods support the Greeks and he should launch an immediate attack on Troy.
  • Agamemnon believes it and thinks victory is near.

Why is this important?

PointMeaning
Zeus fulfils his promise to ThetisHe is making the Greeks suffer, as Achilles asked.
Gods directly interfere with human affairsDivine intervention drives the plot.
Humans can't tell if divine messages are truthfulA message from the gods may be a trick.
⚠️ Common mistake: saying the dream was really from Nestor or told the truth. It only looks like Nestor; it is deceptive and sent by Zeus.
🧪 Book 2: Testing the Army

Agamemnon tests the army

  • Agamemnon decides to test the army's morale before they leave.
  • He tells them they should give up and sail home (“Troy can't be conquered”).
  • He expects them to retaliate and tell him he's wrong, but instead they run to the ships happily.

Athena intervenes

  • Seeing the Greeks rush for their ships, Hera worries that Troy will win and sends Athena to stop them.
  • Athena finds Odysseus and tells him to restore order. He immediately obeys.

Thersites challenges authority

  • A common soldier named Thersites stands up and criticises Agamemnon.
  • He accuses Agamemnon of greed, pride, taking prizes from others and mistreating Achilles.
Agamemnon tests morale→ Troops run to the ships→ Hera → Athena → Odysseus restores order→ Thersites criticises Agamemnon
⚠️ Common mistake: saying Agamemnon really wanted to go home. It was a test of morale; it backfired when the soldiers ran for the ships.
👑 Social Hierarchy & Nestor's Advice
Theme: social hierarchy. Society in the Iliad is ranked, from top to bottom:
  1. Gods
  2. Kings
  3. Nobles
  4. Common warriors
💡 Thersites is a common soldier speaking against a king: he is challenging the hierarchy.

Nestor's advice

Nestor reminds the Greeks that:

  • They have sworn an oath.
  • They must continue fighting.
  • They should organise themselves properly for battle.
🔥 Preparing for Battle: Similes & the Catalogue

The Greeks prepare for war. Homer uses a series of famous epic similes:

ComparisonQuotation / imageEffect
Bees“As warriors gather together, they are like swarms of bees leaving their hive.”Emphasises numbers and organisation
BirdsSoldiers compared to flocks of birdsHighlights scale, noise and energy
Fire“The army shines like a forest fire spreading across a mountainside.”Creates an image of power, destruction and fear
🧩 Remember it: B-B-F = Bees (numbers and organisation), Birds (scale, noise, energy), Fire (power, destruction, fear).

Catalogue of Ships

Part of Book 2 contains the famous Catalogue of Ships: a long list of all the Greek forces. Agamemnon provides the largest and most powerful force.

Why Homer included it:

  • To show the enormous scale of the expedition.
  • To celebrate a sense of national pride.
  • It gives the poem historical significance.

✏️ Practice Questions

Written from your class notes. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

1. Explain how the plague began and how it led to the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles. (8 marks)
  • Agamemnon took Chryseis, daughter of Chryses, priest of Apollo. The priest prayed to Apollo for revenge.
  • Apollo shot deadly arrows of plague into the Greek camp.
  • The people begged Agamemnon to give Chryseis back; he agreed only if he got Achilles' prize, Briseis.
  • Achilles felt incredibly dishonoured and stopped fighting. Agamemnon returned Chryseis and took Briseis.
2. What did Achilles do after Agamemnon took Briseis? Explain what he asked Thetis and what Zeus did. (6 marks)

Achilles recalled his troops, the Myrmidons, to camp and stopped fighting. He asked his mother Thetis to ask Zeus to take the Trojans' side and make the Greeks feel they would never win without him. Thetis did so when Zeus returned. Zeus agreed and bowed his head (an unbreakable vow). Hera saw this, got very mad and argued with Zeus; Hephaestus calmed them down and asked them to take a drink.

3. Describe the deceptive dream in Book 2 and explain why it is important. (6 marks)

Zeus sends a deceptive dream to Agamemnon. It takes the form of Nestor and tells him the gods support the Greeks and he should launch an immediate attack on Troy.

Importance: (1) Zeus is fulfilling his promise to Thetis; (2) it shows gods directly interfering in human affairs; (3) humans cannot tell whether divine messages are truthful.

4. How does Agamemnon test the army, what goes wrong and how is order restored? (6 marks)

Agamemnon tests the army's morale by saying they should give up and sail home (“Troy can't be conquered”). He expects them to protest, but instead they run happily to the ships. Hera is worried that Troy will win and sends Athena, who finds Odysseus and tells him to restore order. He obeys immediately.

5. Who is Thersites and what does his speech show about the Greek army and its social hierarchy? (6 marks)

Thersites is a common soldier who stands up and criticises Agamemnon, accusing him of greed, pride, taking prizes from others and mistreating Achilles.

The hierarchy is gods, kings, nobles, common warriors. A common warrior speaking out against a king challenges that hierarchy and shows there is resentment of Agamemnon in the army.

6. Choose one epic simile from Book 2. Quote or describe it and say what effect it creates. (4 marks)

Bees: “As warriors gather together, they are like swarms of bees leaving their hive.” It emphasises numbers and organisation.

Fire: “The army shines like a forest fire spreading across a mountainside.” It creates an image of power, destruction and fear. (Birds highlight scale, noise and energy.)

7. What is the Catalogue of Ships and why did Homer include it? (4 marks)

It is a long list of all the Greek forces in Book 2; Agamemnon provides the largest and most powerful. Homer included it to show the enormous scale of the expedition, to celebrate a sense of national pride and to give the poem historical significance.
