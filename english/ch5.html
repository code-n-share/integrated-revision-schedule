📌 Punctuation Marks

Each punctuation mark has a specific job. Use them correctly to make your writing clear and professional.

.
Full Stop
Ends a sentence. Always follow with a capital letter for the next sentence.
I love cats. She plays soccer.
?
Question Mark
Used at the end of a direct question.
What's your name? Do you like pizza?
!
Exclamation Mark
Shows excitement, surprise, or strong feelings.
Wow! That was amazing!
,
Comma
Separates ideas in a list, or marks a pause in a sentence. Use the Oxford comma before 'and' in a list.
I bought apples, bananas, and oranges.  |  After school, I went home.
" "
Quotation Marks
Go around spoken words (dialogue) — like little taking marks! When a new person speaks, start a new line.
Emma said, "I love reading books!"  |  "Can we go to the park?" asked Jake.
;
Semicolon
Separates two independent clauses (two complete sentences that are closely related).
We could go shopping tomorrow; you could borrow my brother's car to drive us there.
:
Colon
Introduces a list or an explanation.
English list: textbooks, pens, and a copybook.
-
Hyphen
Joins two adjectives before a noun, or adds extra info in the middle of a sentence.
The hike was organised for May 12th–15th.  |  People — both young and old — are advised to be careful.
( )
Brackets
Used to add extra or additional information that isn't essential to the sentence.
I read that novel (The Great Gatsby) in school.
…
Ellipsis
Signifies missing text, a pause, or a trailing off in thought.
Jack of all trades… better than a master of one.  |  I don't know… it sounds like a bad idea.
Apostrophes — Two Rules
Possession Ownership
Use an apostrophe + s to show that something belongs to someone.
Jane's parents work in the school's office.
The children's bags are in my way.
Contraction Shortening
Use an apostrophe to replace a missing letter when two words are joined together.
I am → I'm  |  do not → don't  |  it is → it's
⚠️
Common Apostrophe Traps it's = it is (contraction)  |  its = belonging to it (possession — NO apostrophe). Never use an apostrophe to make a word plural: "the dog's" means one dog owns something, "the dogs" is just the plural.
💬 Punctuating Dialogue

Dialogue in stories has its own special punctuation rules. Follow these carefully in your creative writing.

Dialogue Punctuation Rules
  • Use quotation marks (" ") around what's being said.
  • A new line every time someone new speaks.
  • Use dialogue tags → 'said', 'asked', 'whispered', 'yelled', 'answered'.
  • Use a comma to separate dialogue from dialogue tags.
  • Always start dialogue with a capital letter.
  • Use punctuation at the end of dialogue: " , ! ? . … "
"Hello," said Mary, with a grin.
"Ah hiya Mary," said John. "Where are you off to?"
"I'm going to get ice cream, it's roasting out!" she answered, mopping her brow exaggeratedly.
"Sounds great! Where are you getting it?"
"The van in the village," she answered, pointing excitedly.

Class Rules for Writing Always title and date your work. Use blue or black pens only. Do NOT use numbers when writing — spell them out. Exception: dates, statistics, numbers over 10, addresses. Do NOT use symbols (%, &, #) in formal writing.
⏱️ Tenses

Watch your tenses! You must stay consistent throughout your writing. Don't switch randomly between past and present.

TenseWhen to UseExample
Past Events that already happened I walked to the shop. She was nervous.
Present Events happening now I walk to the shop. She is nervous.
Future Events that will happen I will walk to the shop. She will be nervous.
🔁
Tense Consistency Pick ONE tense for your story and stick to it. Most creative writing uses the past tense. Only switch tense intentionally (e.g. for dialogue or a flashback).
📐 Sentence Structure Rules
Keep Sentences Short
Long, rambling sentences are hard to follow. Aim for clear, punchy sentences. End with a full stop before starting fresh.
Capital Letters
Always capitalise: the start of a sentence, names of people and places, the word "I", titles.
ireland → Ireland   house = lowercase (common noun)
Never Start with "Because"
"Because" introduces a dependent clause — it can't stand alone as a sentence. Attach it to an independent clause.
Because the bombing could start.
They ran because the bombing could start.
No Symbols in Writing
Don't use &, %, @, # in formal or creative writing. Spell words out in full.
100% → one hundred percent
No Numbers as Digits
Spell out numbers in writing. Exceptions: dates, statistics, numbers over 10, and addresses.
There were 3 cats.
There were three cats.
Spelling Correction (SP)
When you get an SP mark, write the correct word three times to help it stick in your memory.
togeter → together → together → together
🧠 Quick Quiz — Test Yourself
What punctuation mark ends a sentence?
Basics
A full stop (.) ends a sentence. It must always be followed by a capital letter for the next sentence.
What is the difference between a semicolon (;) and a colon (:)?
Punctuation
A semicolon (;) separates two independent clauses — two complete sentences that are closely related. A colon (:) introduces a list or an explanation. e.g. "We could go tomorrow; bring your coat." vs "I need three things: a pen, a notebook, and patience."
Name the 5 key dialogue punctuation rules.
Dialogue
1. Quotation marks around speech. 2. New line every time someone new speaks. 3. Dialogue tags (said, asked, whispered). 4. Comma before the dialogue tag. 5. Capital letter to start dialogue. 6. End punctuation inside the quotation marks.
What are the TWO uses of an apostrophe?
Apostrophes
1. Possession — to show something belongs to someone (Jane's book, the children's bags). 2. Contraction — to replace a missing letter when two words join (I'm, don't, it's). Remember: "its" (belonging) never has an apostrophe — only "it's" (it is) does.
What's wrong with this sentence: "Their are too many people here."?
Spelling
"Their" is wrong — their means belonging to them. The correct word here is there (referring to existence). Correct sentence: "There are too many people here."
Correct this sentence: "Me and my friends went to the shop."
Grammar
Correct: "My friends and I went to the shop." A quick trick: remove the others. "Me went to the shop" doesn't work — so it should be "I". Also, put yourself last as a matter of politeness.
Name three things an ellipsis (...) can be used for.
Punctuation
1. To signify missing text in a quotation. 2. To show a pause or hesitation in speech. 3. To create a trailing off — leaving something unsaid for dramatic effect. e.g. "I don't know… it sounds like a bad idea."
