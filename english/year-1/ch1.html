5
Theme
The big idea the poem explores
🌼 Poem 1 — Daffodils
"I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud"
(also known as "Daffodils")
by William Wordsworth
🌼
Rhyme Scheme
A B A B C C — same for all stanzas (ends in a rhyming couplet)
Mood / Tone
Happy · Joyful · Peaceful · Appreciation of nature · Calm and relaxed · Signs of admiration
Theme
The healing power of nature · Memory · Joy
Message
The poet reflects on the joy of nature, which lifts him from loneliness and sadness. Even in memory, nature brings happiness.
Development
Sadness and loneliness → happiness and joy — reflected through nature (= Pathetic Fallacy)
Main Technique
IMAGERY (visual) — used throughout the poem
Techniques & Quotes
  • Simile
    "I wandered lonely as a cloud"

    Compares the poet to a cloud floating alone — shows his isolation and freedom.
  • Simile
    "Continuous as the stars that shine"

    Shows the vast number of daffodils, stretching endlessly.
  • Personification
    "When all at once I saw a crowd"
     · 
    "Fluttering and dancing in the breeze"
     · 
    "Tossing their heads in sprightly dance"

    The daffodils are given human actions — they become alive, joyful companions for the poet.
  • Personification
    "And dances with the daffodils"

    In the final stanza, the poet's heart joins the dance — the memory brings joy.
  • Hyperbole
    "Ten thousand saw I at a glance"

    Exaggeration to emphasise the enormous number of daffodils.
  • Hyperbole
    "They stretched in never-ending line"

    Further exaggerates the scale of the scene.
  • Metaphor
    "What wealth the show to me had brought"

    The experience is metaphorically compared to wealth — precious and valuable.
  • Metaphor
    "They flash upon that inward eye"

    The memory flashes in his mind like a light — vivid and sudden.
  • Metaphor
    "And then my heart with pleasure fills"

    The heart is a container that fills up with joy.
  • Pathetic Fallacy
    The poem develops from sadness and loneliness to happiness and joy — all reflected through nature (the daffodils, the waves, the breeze).
  • Repetition
    "I gazed — and gazed — but little thought"

    Repetition of "gazed" shows he kept staring, almost in a trance.
  • Imagery (visual)
    Stanzas 2–4 are full of visual imagery. Especially:
    "The waves beside them danced; but they out-did the sparkling waves in glee"
  • Sibilance
    "Continuous as the stars that shine"

    The repeated 's' sound creates a soft, flowing, peaceful effect.
  • Assonance
    Repeated vowel sounds throughout (e.g. "host of golden daffodils", "beside the lake, beneath the trees").
  • Alliteration
    "That floats on high o'er vales and hills"
    — 'f' and 'h' sounds create a light, airy feeling.
🛤️ Poem 2 — The Road Not Taken
"The Road Not Taken"
by Robert Frost
🛤️
Mood / Tone
Mysterious · Thoughtful · Lonely · Calm · Sad / Overwhelming
Theme
Choices in life · Individuality · Not following the crowd
Message
Life is full of decisions; you can only choose one path. Decisions shape your life (butterfly effect). Be different — don't do what's expected.
Context
Written by Frost — who, what, where, when and why of the poem. A personal reflection on choice and regret.
Techniques & Quotes
  • Extended Metaphor
    "Two roads diverged in a yellow wood … Two roads diverged in a wood"

    The two roads = the choices we face in life. You can only take one. This metaphor runs through the entire poem. When Frost removes "yellow" at the end, it could mean: the colour of happiness is gone; time has passed; he is no longer afraid of his decision; autumn (things coming to an end) has turned to something new.
  • Pathetic Fallacy
    "… diverged in a yellow wood"

    "Yellow" represents autumn — change, things coming to an end. Used throughout the poem to reflect the poet's uncertainty.
  • Anaphora
    "And sorry I … And be one … And looked down"

    Repetition of "And" at the beginning of sentences in a sequence. Shows the poet feels overwhelmed and hesitant.
  • Repetition
    "… sorry I … long I stood … as I could"

    Repetition of the personal pronoun "I" — emphasises this is entirely his personal decision.
  • Enjambment
    "And sorry I could not travel both. And be one traveller, long I stood. And looked down as far as I could"

    Lines run into one another — shows distress, panic and urgency. He is trying to prove his point so people don't judge him for the decision he's about to make.
  • Contrast
    "And both that morning equally lay / In leaves no step had trodden black."

    "Morning" = new beginnings; "leaves" = autumn, something coming to an end. Moving between choices symbolises 2 choices: one story coming to an end, another beginning.
  • Imagery (visual)
    "I shall be telling this with a sigh"

    Aural imagery too — the sigh conveys relief, doubt, worry or regret (open to interpretation).
💡
Key insight about "yellow": The extended metaphor changes when Frost removes "yellow" at the end of the poem. By just doing that, it had a huge effect — it could mean he removed the colour of happiness, showing how times passed and his perspective of his decision has changed. Or it could mean yellow = symbol of fear, and the poet is no longer afraid.
🏝️ Poem 3 — The Lake Isle of Innisfree
"The Lake Isle of Innisfree"
by William Butler Yeats
🏝️
Mood / Tone
Peaceful · Quiet · Tranquil · Calmness · Silence
Theme
Belonging · Home · Longing
Rhyme Scheme
Same rhyming scheme for the whole poem
Message
How he longs to go back to the place where he can be himself. Even standing on a busy roadway, he can still hear the sounds of home calling to him. The power of being somewhere where you truly belong.
Techniques & Quotes
  • Repetition
    "… and go now, and go to Innisfree"
    — all of last stanza:
    "I will arise … I hear lake water … While I stand … I hear it …"

    The repetition of "I" and "go" shows his deep personal longing to return.
  • Assonance
    "I will arise …"
    ·
    "And a small cabin build there, of clay and wattles made."

    Repeated vowel sounds create a musical, flowing rhythm — like the sounds of nature he is longing for.
  • Alliteration
    "… hear lake water lapping with low sounds …"

    The repeated 'l' sounds create a slow, gentle, calming effect — sound of pleasure and peace.
  • Metaphor
    "I hear it in the deep heart's core."

    He can hear Innisfree calling to him even in the city. It's in the core of his heart — showing how deeply he feels connected to that place.
  • Metaphor
    "… veils of the morning …"

    Metaphor for fog. Also spiritual — connection to nature.
  • Pathetic Fallacy
    "… veils of the morning … midnights all a glimmer … noon a purple glow …"

    "Morning" = calmness, almost a nurturing quality. "Midnight" = magical, tranquil. "Noon" = warm, mystical, emotional richness. The times of day reflect different emotional states.
  • Imagery (aural)
    "I hear lake water lapping with low sounds by the shore"

    He can hear the sounds of home with such precision — how it's calling him to go back. Shows the intensity of his longing.
📿 Poem 4 — Midterm Break
"Midterm Break"
by Seamus Heaney
📿
Mood / Tone
Grief · Shock · Numbness · Restrained sadness
Theme
Death · Loss · Grief · Childhood innocence cut short
Message
A personal poem about the death of the poet's younger brother. Explores how grief affects a family differently and the painful experience of confronting death for the first time.
Key Fact
Studied poem — know the poet's name, the title, key techniques, message, mood and at least 2 PQE quotes.
📌
For the exam: Know this poem's poet, title, theme (death/grief), mood (restrained sadness/shock), and be able to identify and quote at least 2 techniques using PQE format. The final line — the length of the coffin equalling the age of the child — is one of the most powerful moments.
🕊️ Poem 5 — À Quoi Bon Dire
"À Quoi Bon Dire"
by Charlotte Mew
🕊️
Mood / Tone
Sad · Mysterious · Gloomy
Theme
Loss of a loved one · Grief · Enduring love
Rhyme Scheme
Stanza 1 = ABAB · Stanza 2 = ABAB · Stanza 3 = ABABB (ends in a rhyming couplet)
Message / Meaning
She's lost someone she used to love and is not ready to accept that they're gone. She's linking her memories with what's happening around her. Also about how she still loves him, even though he died seventeen years ago. Hints at reunion as her own life nears its end.
Techniques & Quotes
  • Repetition
    "But"
    ·
    "Good-bye"
    ·
    "everybody"

    The word "But" is repeated — shows she is silently arguing against the world's reality. "Good-bye" and "everybody" repeat to emphasise the finality others accept but she refuses to.
  • Contrast
    "kiss and swear"

    "Kiss" represents affection and love; "swear" represents commitment and promises in the relationship. These contrasting words together show the full depth of what she shared with her loved one.
  • Repetition (deeper effect)
    "everybody thinks … But I"
    and
    "everybody sees … But you"

    Shows the clear difference between what the world thought and what she and her loved one knew. It is almost like a silent argument she is trying to have with what the world's reality is — versus what she believes is still true. Like her love for her loved one who passed.
📘 PQE Example — "À Quoi Bon Dire"

Question: From your study of the poem "À Quoi Bon Dire" by Charlotte Mew, what do you think the message of the poem is?


Introduction: From studying the poem, "À Quoi Bon Dire", by Charlotte Mew, I think the message of the poem is the loss of someone you loved, and the grief of losing them.


Quote: You can see this when she says, "Seventeen years ago … sounded like Good-bye / everybody thinks that you are dead, But I". This shows that even though the person she loved died seventeen years ago, she's still grieving. This quote also shows that she's somewhat still in denial — she still isn't ready to accept that someone she loved has passed away.


Second PQE: Another place you can see this is when she says, "… as I grow stiff and cold … say Good-bye too … everybody sees that I am old… But you." This shows how even though she understands that she's getting older and her time to say "Good-bye" might be near. But, that only makes her closer to being reunited with the one she loves.


Conclusion: In conclusion, I think the message of the poem, "À Quoi Bon Dire", is the loss of someone you loved, and the grief that she's carried with her seventeen years after her loved one died. But also hints at the reunion of her and her loved one, as her journey comes to a near end.

🛠️ All Poetic Techniques — Definitions & Why They Are Used

Learn each technique: its definition, why the poet uses it, and at least one example from your studied poems.

Simile
A comparison using 'like', 'as', or 'than'.
Why used: allows the reader to create a vivid image in their mind.
e.g. "I wandered lonely as a cloud" (Daffodils)
Metaphor
A comparison NOT using 'like', 'as', or 'than'. Says one thing IS another.
Why used: creates an image that is easier for the reader to understand.
e.g. "What wealth the show to me had brought" (Daffodils); "I hear it in the deep heart's core" (Innisfree)
Extended Metaphor
A metaphor that is developed and sustained across an entire poem or large section of it.
Why used: gives the poem deeper symbolic meaning that builds as the reader progresses.
e.g. "Two roads diverged in a yellow wood" — roads = life choices (The Road Not Taken)
Personification
When an inanimate (non-living) object is given human characteristics.
Why used: connects the reader to the objects being personified and makes them feel emotions towards them.
e.g. "Fluttering and dancing in the breeze" (Daffodils)
Alliteration
When two or more words close together begin with the same consonant sound.
Why used: makes the piece sound pleasing to the reader; creates a musical effect.
e.g. "hear lake water lapping with low sounds" (Innisfree); "floats on high o'er vales and hills" (Daffodils)
Assonance
When the same vowel sound is repeated in nearby words.
Why used: creates rhythm within the piece.
e.g. "A host, of golden daffodils;" · "Beside the lake, beneath the trees"
Sibilance
The repetition of 's' sounds in nearby words.
Why used: slows down the reading process; can hint at danger, serenity or mystery.
e.g. "Continuous as the stars that shine" (Daffodils)
Imagery
A vivid and vibrant description that appeals to the reader's senses and imagination.
Why used: allows the reader to see, touch, taste, smell and hear what is happening.
Types: Aural (hear) · Visual (see) · Tactile (touch) · Olfactory (smell) · Gustatory (taste)
Hyperbole
An exaggerated statement that is not meant to be taken literally or seriously.
Why used: adds emphasis or humour to a piece.
e.g. "Ten thousand saw I at a glance" · "They stretched in never-ending line" (Daffodils)
Pathetic Fallacy
When human emotions are reflected in nature; the weather, seasons or time of day.
Why used: makes the environment reflect the inner experience of the author — allows the reader to understand on a deeper level.
e.g. "diverged in a yellow wood" (Frost) — autumn = change/endings
Enjambment
Continuing a sentence across a line break without pause (also called run-on lines).
Why used: speeds up the pace of the poem; creates a sense of urgency, tension or rising emotion in the poet.
e.g. "And sorry I could not travel both. And be one traveller, long I stood." (The Road Not Taken)
Repetition
Repeating a word or phrase to emphasise its importance.
Why used: helps draw the reader's attention to an idea, thought or feeling.
e.g. "I gazed — and gazed — but little thought" (Daffodils); "and go now, and go to Innisfree" (Innisfree)
Anaphora
Repetition of a word or phrase at the beginning of multiple consecutive lines.
Why used: creates a sense of being overwhelmed or hesitant; builds emotional intensity.
e.g. "And sorry I … And be one … And looked down" (The Road Not Taken)
Contrast
Placing two very different things side by side.
Why used: makes arguments stronger by giving emphasis; makes differences clearer.
e.g. "kiss and swear" (À Quoi Bon Dire) — affection vs. commitment
Onomatopoeia
When the word sounds like the noise it is describing.
Why used: creates a sensory effect to help create a vivid image.
e.g. "lapping" (Innisfree) — the sound of gentle water
Motif
A dominant, recurring idea through repeated use of images, ideas and symbols.
Why used: emphasises what is most important about the poem.
e.g. Roads and choices in The Road Not Taken; daffodils and nature in Daffodils
Mood / Tone
The state of mind of the poet being reflected in their work.
Why used: builds setting, creates atmosphere and projects the poet's emotions onto the reader.
e.g. Daffodils = joyful; Innisfree = peaceful; À Quoi Bon Dire = sad/mysterious
Rhyme
When words have the same sound pattern. Shown as ABAB, ABCB etc.
Why used: makes the poem sound more musical; creates a satisfying pattern.
Daffodils = ABABCC; Innisfree = same scheme throughout; À Quoi Bon Dire = ABAB / ABAB / ABABB
Stanza
A group of lines in a poem, arranged together — similar to a paragraph.
Why used: structure affects how the poem is meant to be read; stanzas organise the poem's ideas.
All stanzas in Daffodils have the same ABABCC rhyming pattern.
🔍 5 Things to Consider When Analysing Any Poem
💡
1. Theme
The central idea and the subject matter of the poem. The subject matter can reveal the underlying theme.
Ask: What is this poem really about beneath the surface?
🗣️
2. Language
Creates the mood and tone of the poem. Also impacts the rhythm of the poem. What word choices stand out?
Look for unusual or powerful word choices — why did the poet pick that word?
🎵
3. Sound and Rhythm
The syllabic patterns and stresses create the metrical pattern of a poem. Does it rhyme? What is the effect?
Read the poem aloud — what do you notice about how it sounds?
📐
4. Structure
Affects how the poem is meant to be read. How many stanzas? How many lines per stanza? Are there run-on lines?
Structure = the framework of the poem.
🌍
5. Context
The who, what, where, when and why of a poem. Knowing the poet's life or historical setting can help explain its purpose.
Context = background information that helps you understand the poem better.
✍️ How to Analyse a Poem Using PQE
PQE Format
P = Point (1 mark) · Q = Quote (1 mark) · E = Explanation (3 marks) = 5 marks total
💡 20 marks = 5 PQEs · 15 marks = 3 PQEs · 10 marks = 2 PQEs
Introduction Formula (for studied poetry)
  1. Introduce the written work first (name of poem in quotation marks)
  2. Introduce the author / poet
  3. Reference the question being asked
  4. State your point clearly
📘 Introduction Example

"From my study of the poem, 'The Road Not Taken', by Robert Frost, a memorable metaphor or simile used by Frost would be the extended metaphor he uses through his poem."

Conclusion Phrase
Always finish your PQE answer with a conclusion
  • "In conclusion / To conclude, it is clear to see that…"
  • Re-state your overall point without repeating the same words.
  • For unseen poems: identify the message/mood, then explain the techniques used to create it.
🃏 Technique Flashcards — Test Yourself

Click Reveal Answer to check your definition. Try to answer before clicking!

What is the rhyme scheme of "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" and what is the mood?
Daffodils
Rhyme scheme: ABABCC — same for all stanzas (ends in a rhyming couplet). Mood/Tone: happy, joyful, peaceful, appreciation of nature's beauty, calm and relaxed, signs of admiration. The poem develops from sadness and loneliness → happiness and joy — all reflected through nature (= Pathetic Fallacy).
Name 3 examples of personification in "Daffodils" and explain their effect.
Daffodils
1. "When all at once I saw a crowd" — the daffodils are compared to a crowd of people. 2. "Fluttering and dancing in the breeze" — they dance like humans. 3. "Tossing their heads in sprightly dance" — they toss their heads in joy. 4. "And dances with the daffodils" — in memory, the poet's heart dances WITH them. Effect: gives the daffodils life and personality, making them joyful companions.
What is the extended metaphor in "The Road Not Taken" and what does it represent?
Road Not Taken
"Two roads diverged in a yellow wood" — the two roads are a metaphor for the choices we face in life. You can only choose one and decisions shape your life (butterfly effect). Frost later removes "yellow" at the end of the poem. This could mean: the colour of happiness is gone, time has passed, his perspective has changed, yellow = symbol of fear and he's no longer afraid.
What is enjambment? Give an example and explain why Frost uses it in "The Road Not Taken".
Road Not Taken
Enjambment = continuing a sentence across a line break (also called run-on lines). Example: "And sorry I could not travel both. And be one traveller, long I stood. And looked down as far as I could." Frost uses this to show distress, panic and urgency — he is trying to justify his decision so people don't judge him. It also speeds up the pace, creating a sense of rising tension.
What is the message of "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" and what is the mood?
Innisfree
Message: He longs to go back to the place where he can be himself and where he feels most connected. Even standing on a busy roadway where things aren't peaceful, he can still hear the sounds of home calling to him. The power of being somewhere where you truly belong. Mood: peaceful, quiet, tranquil, calmness, silence. Theme: belonging and home.
What does the metaphor "I hear it in the deep heart's core" mean in Innisfree?
Innisfree
Even though he's in the city, he can hear Innisfree calling to him — it's at the very core (deepest part) of his heart. This shows the intensity of his longing to go back and how deeply he feels connected to that place. It is a metaphor because the "deep heart's core" is not a real physical place but represents his innermost feelings.
In "À Quoi Bon Dire", what are the two key techniques and what do they show?
À Quoi Bon Dire
1. Repetition — "everybody thinks… But I" and "everybody sees… But you" — shows the clear difference between what the world thought and what she and her loved one knew; almost a silent argument with reality. Like her love for her loved one who passed. 2. Contrast — "kiss and swear" — 'kiss' represents affection and love; 'swear' represents commitment and promises; together they show the depth and seriousness of the relationship.
What is pathetic fallacy? Give an example from a studied poem.
Techniques
Pathetic Fallacy = when human emotions are reflected in nature; the weather, seasons, or time of day. Why used: to make the environment reflect the inner experience of the author. Example 1: In Daffodils — "Continuous as the stars that shine" — the endless stars reflect the poet's joy. Example 2: In The Road Not Taken — "diverged in a yellow wood" — the yellowing autumn wood represents change and things coming to an end.
What is anaphora? How does Frost use it in "The Road Not Taken"?
Techniques
Anaphora = repetition of a word at the beginning of multiple lines, usually in sequence. Frost uses it with "And": "And sorry I … And be one … And looked down". The repetition of "And" at the beginning of sentences in a sequence shows the poet is feeling overwhelmed and hesitant — he is piling up reasons and thoughts one after another.
Name the 5 types of imagery and give an example of each.
Techniques
1. Visual (see) — "Ten thousand saw I at a glance" (Daffodils). 2. Aural (hear) — "I hear lake water lapping with low sounds" (Innisfree). 3. Tactile (touch) — "I grow stiff and cold" (À Quoi Bon Dire). 4. Olfactory (smell) — any description involving scent. 5. Gustatory (taste) — any description involving taste. Imagery allows the reader to see, touch, taste, smell and hear what is happening.
What is the PQE introduction formula for a studied poem? Give an example.
Exam Skill
Order: Written work → Author → Question → Point (WAQP). Example: "From my study of the poem, 'The Road Not Taken', by Robert Frost, a memorable metaphor or simile used by Frost would be the extended metaphor he uses through his poem." Then state your point, give your quote, explain the effect on the reader.
