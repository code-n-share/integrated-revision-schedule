📌 What is PQE?

PQE (also called PIE or PQA — all the same thing!) is the structure you use to answer questions in the exam. It applies to reading comprehension, poetry analysis, film studies, and functional writing questions.

P
Point
1 mark
Your main claim or argument in response to the question.
Q
Quote
1 mark
Evidence from the text — a direct quote using "quotation marks".
E
Explanation
3 marks
Explain the effect of the quote — why it matters, what it shows, how it makes the reader feel.
💡
1 PQE = 5 Marks P (1 mark) + Q (1 mark) + E (3 marks) = 5 marks. The Explanation is worth the most — always expand on it. Never leave your Explanation at one sentence.
🎯 How Many PQEs Do I Need?

Work out how many PQEs to write based on the marks available:

Marks AvailablePQEs RequiredNotes
20 marks4 PQEs (or 2 PQEs × 2 questions)Each PQE = 5 marks
15 marks3 PQEsEach PQE = 5 marks
10 marks2 PQEsEach PQE = 5 marks
5 marks1 PQEOne complete P, Q, and E
🖊️ The Introduction Formula

Every PQE response needs an introduction sentence first. The introduction follows this specific order:

1
Written Work
Name of the text/poem in "quotes"
2
Author/Poet
Full name of the writer/director
3
Question
Refer back to what the question asked
4
Point
State your answer/point clearly
⭐
Memory Tip: WAQP Written work → Author → Question → Point. The written work is ALWAYS introduced first. The second is the author or etc. The third is the question. And last is the point.
✍️ Example Introduction (PQE for a Poem)
Question: Select one word to describe Zlata's experience during the war. Explain your choice.

Introduction sentence:
"From my reading of 'Zlata's Diary: A Child's Life in Sarajevo', by Zlata Filipovic, a word I would use to describe her experience is 'traumatising'."

↑ This one sentence hits: (1) written work, (2) author, (3) answers the question, (4) gives the point.
✍️ Example Introduction (PQE for a Poem — Poetry Style)
"From my study of the poem, 'The Road Not Taken', by Robert Frost, a memorable metaphor or simile used by Frost would be the extended metaphor he uses through his poem."
📄 Full PQE Response — Example
✍️ Full PQE Example (5 marks)
Question: Would you be afraid to live in Sarajevo during the war? Why/Why not? Explain your answer with reference to the text. (5 marks)

Introduction + Point:
I would be afraid to live in Sarajevo during the war. I can see, from Zlata's experience, she witnessed "an ugly, disgusting war".

Quote:
Another reason why I would not like to live in Sarajevo during the war is, at the start, Zlata mentions "BOREDOM!!! SHOOTING!!! SHELLING!!! PEOPLE BEING KILLED!!! DESPAIR!!! HUNGER!!! MISERY!!! FEAR!!!"

Explanation:
I couldn't begin to imagine what that would feel like, and the constant fear of being bombed and making sure your family is safe, is just too much for me. So, to go back to the point, I would not like to live in Sarajevo during the war.
✅ Conclusion Phrases

Always end your PQE response with a conclusion sentence. Use one of these phrases:

Conclusion Starters
  • In conclusion, …
  • To conclude, …
  • It is clear to see that…
  • Therefore, it is evident that…
  • For these reasons, I believe that…
  • So, to go back to the point, …
✍️ Example Conclusion
"In conclusion, it is clear to see that Zlata's experience during the war was traumatising, due to the constant threat to her, and her family's lives."
📖 Reading Comprehension Strategy

Section A of the exam is Reading Comprehension (20 marks). Here is the step-by-step strategy from your exam notes:

  1. Read the questions FIRST — before you read the text. Know what you're looking for.
  2. Check how many marks each question is worth — this tells you how many PQEs to write.
  3. Read the comprehension carefully — don't rush. Read the whole passage.
  4. Underline the answer in the reading comprehension text as you go.
  5. Introduction first — name the author and the text at the start of your answer.
⏱️
Exam Timing You have 1 hour 30 minutes total. Section A (Reading Comprehension) = 20 marks. Section B (Poetry) = 20 marks. Section C (Film Studies) = 20 marks. Section D (Functional Writing) = 40 marks.
💡 Comprehension Tips & Tricks
TipDetail
Use the text's own wordsQuote directly from the passage in your Q (quote) section — it shows you can identify key evidence.
Don't repeat your pointYour Explanation should ADD new information — not just repeat the Point or the Quote in different words.
"In your own words"If the question says "explain in your own words" — you still use a quote but paraphrase around it.
Expand your EThe Explanation is worth 3 marks. Give at least 2–3 sentences explaining WHY the quote is significant.

Watch your tensesStay consistent — past, present, or future. Don't switch mid-answer.

🃏 Flashcard Drill
What does PQE stand for and how many marks is each part worth?
PQE Basics
P = Point (1 mark), Q = Quote (1 mark), E = Explanation (3 marks). Total = 5 marks per PQE. PQE is also known as PIE or PQA — all the same structure.
What is the WAQP introduction formula? Give an example sentence.
Introduction
Written work → Author → Question → Point.

Example: "From my study of the poem, 'The Road Not Taken', by Robert Frost, a memorable metaphor used by Frost would be the extended metaphor he uses throughout his poem."
If a question is worth 20 marks, how many PQEs do you need?
Marks
4 PQEs (since 1 PQE = 5 marks, and 4 × 5 = 20). For 15 marks = 3 PQEs. For 10 marks = 2 PQEs. For 5 marks = 1 PQE.
List the 5 steps for tackling a reading comprehension in the exam.
Comprehension
1. Read the questions FIRST. 2. Check how many marks each question is worth. 3. Read the comprehension carefully. 4. Underline the answer in the text. 5. Introduction first (author + name of text).
Give 3 conclusion phrases you can use to end a PQE answer.
Conclusion
Any of: "In conclusion, …" / "To conclude, …" / "It is clear to see that…" / "Therefore, it is evident that…" / "So, to go back to the point, …"
