📝 Overview — Section D: Functional Writing

In the exam, Section D is Functional Writing (40 marks). You will be given three options and must choose ONE. Possible text types include: diary entry, blog, letter, autobiography, biography, review, or speech. Each has its own distinct format and language style.

💡
Exam Strategy Always state your text type at the top of your answer (e.g. "Question Chosen: Q2 — Letter"). Use the correct format features from the start to earn marks before you even write your first sentence.
📔 Diary Entry
📔
Diary Entry
Private · Personal · First Person · Chronological
The 3 Fs of a Diary Entry
📅
Format
Date in the top left corner. Begin with "Dear Diary," then skip to next line. Close with "I'll write again soon!" or "Until tomorrow," followed by your name.
📋
Facts
Summarise the key events of the day in chronological order. If writing as a character, make sure you know their facts well.
💭
Feelings
Explain how events made you feel. Open up to your diary and show your emotional side. Focus on thoughts, feelings and intentions.
Format Checklist
  • Date at the top
  • "Dear Diary," as the opening
  • Reference to time and place
  • Written in first person (I, me, my)
  • Detailed account of events in chronological order
  • Informal, intimate language — describes thoughts, feelings, fears and intentions
  • Write as if events just happened (sense of immediacy)
  • Making a statement then changing your mind creates immediacy
  • Witty observations, sarcastic remarks and self-mockery entertain the reader
  • Sign off: "I'll write again soon, [Name]"
Language Style
Diary Language Features
  • Informal & conversational — relaxed tone, contractions (I'm, I've, can't)
  • Intimate — sharing your innermost thoughts, fears, doubts and intrusive thoughts
  • First person — I, me, my, mine throughout
  • Emotive language — express how you truly feel
✍️ Example Opening
Wednesday, 3rd September
Dear Diary,

I haven't fully survived the first week of Secondary School, but I have made it half way through! The first day was pretty nerve-wracking, but I somehow managed! I also had no idea where I was going!
💻 Blog
💻
Blog Post
Public · Opinions · First Person · Titled
Key Features
  • URL address at top (e.g. https://amazingzoos.ie/blog)
  • Blog title (name of the blog)
  • Entry title — catchy, short, snappy (puns work well!)
  • Date + time, blogger's name, category
  • Opinions expressed clearly
  • Written in first person
  • Details & information
  • Images* (mention them, can't draw)
  • Comments section* & links*
  • Introduction / Main Body / Conclusion
  • Sign in / Sign off
  • Language appropriate to audience (formal/informal/simple/complex)
Structure
Blog Structure
  • Introduction: Welcome readers, state the topic
  • Main Body: Details, opinions, information — each paragraph one idea
  • Conclusion: Summarise, call-to-action (ask readers to comment, share)
  • Sign off: Informal goodbye, your name, promise to return
✍️ Example Entry
🌐 https://amazingzoos.ie/blog
Amazing Zoos — Talking about zoos and animals!
📅 2nd October 2025 · Zoos · Aadya Singh

Hello, and welcome back to Amazing Zoos! Today we are going to talk about zoos and the amazing animals in them…
✉️ Letter Writing
✉️
Letter
Formal or Informal · Structured · Addressed
Formal Letter
  • Written officially with respectful greetings
  • Structured format, professional purposes
  • e.g. letter to your boss, to a company
  • Sign off: Yours Sincerely / Yours Faithfully / Kind Regards
Informal Letter
  • Relaxed and friendly tone
  • Casual greetings, friendly language
  • e.g. letter to a friend, family member
  • Sign off: Love from / See you soon / Best wishes
Letter Format (always use this layout)
  • Your name and address in the top right
  • Date below your address (on the right)
  • Recipient's name and address on the left
  • Greeting: "Dear [Name]," — start a new line
  • First paragraph (short): state why you are writing
  • Body paragraphs: each focusses on one idea
  • Final paragraph: conclusion / plans
  • Sign off (followed by a comma), then new line, then signature
Useful Sentence Starters
Letter Sentence Starters
  • I am writing to…
  • I am writing because…
  • I hope that…
  • I'm excited for…
  • I recall when…
  • The other day I…
📖 Autobiography
📖
Autobiography
Written by the person about themselves · First person
Definition

An account of someone's life written by that person themselves.

Features
  • Personal detail
  • Facts and information
  • Written in first person (I, me, my)
  • Narrative style
  • Events told in sequence or chronological order
  • Revelations of secrets or private matters
  • Thoughts, feelings and intentions
Language
Autobiography Language
  • Honest — truthful about experiences and feelings
  • Descriptive — paint a picture of events
  • Informal or formal — depends on the preference of the writer
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Biography
🧑‍🤝‍🧑
Biography
Written by someone else · Third person
Definition

An account of someone's life written by another person.

Features
  • Personal details
  • Facts and information
  • Written in third person (he, she, they)
  • Narrative style (storytelling)
  • Often recounts events in sequence (in order)
  • Often reveals secrets and private events
Language
Biography Language
  • Honest and factual
  • Appropriate to the topic — a celebrity biography may be informal and conversational; a political figure's biography may be more formal
⚠️
Auto vs Bio — Key Difference Autobiography = written BY the person (1st person). Biography = written BY SOMEONE ELSE about that person (3rd person).
⭐ Review
⭐
Review
Persuasive · Opinion-Based · First Person
Purpose

A review tries to convince the audience to agree with your thoughts, feelings and opinions. Create a connection with the audience to make them feel you are the same. Try not to spoil the plot/ending. Focus on key moments and use quotes to strengthen your opinion.

Structure
  • Introduction: Name the product/object, give a brief description. Hold off showing your opinion in the intro.
  • Description: More detailed background — when it was made, by who, what it's for/about.
  • Judgement: Pros/cons, your opinion and explanation for why you feel/think that way.
  • Details: Be descriptive — about your feelings, thoughts and the product/object.
  • Engaging: Persuasive — think about your audience and appropriate language.
Key Features
  • Written in first person (me, my, mine, I)
  • Information + facts (background info)
  • Engaging and persuasive tone
  • Audience-appropriate language
🎤 Speech Writing
🎤
Speech
Informative · Persuasive · Special Occasion
Types of Speech
  • Informative: Present facts in a concise, engaging manner
  • Persuasive: Use facts and opinions to convince audience
  • Special Occasion: Entertain or pay tribute (weddings, funerals)
Structure
  • Beginning: introduce yourself + topic
  • Middle: main body of argument
  • Conclusion: restate main points (don't repeat)
  • Short, simple sentence structures

🃏 Flashcard Drill
What are the 3 Fs of a diary entry?
Diary
Format (date, "Dear Diary," sign-off), Facts (key events in chronological order), Feelings (how events made you feel — thoughts, fears, intentions).
Name 5 features of a blog post that a diary does NOT have.
Blog
1. URL address, 2. Blog title, 3. Entry title (catchy/punny), 4. Images, 5. Comments section & links. Also: typed onto a web page; public audience; time stamp.
What is the main difference between an autobiography and a biography?
Auto/Bio
Autobiography is written BY the person themselves (first person: I, me, my). Biography is written by SOMEONE ELSE about the person (third person: he, she, they).
Name all 8 speech techniques and give one famous example speech.
Speech
1. Engage audience (MLK Jr.), 2. Structure (Malala), 3. Personal pronouns (Amanda Gorman), 4. Anecdote (Churchill), 5. Rhetorical question (Michelle Obama), 6. Triadic structure (JFK), 7. Call to action (Greta Thunberg), 8. Repetition (Lincoln — Gettysburg Address).
What is the correct letter layout? (describe step by step)
Letter
Top right: your name + address + date. Left: recipient's name + address. Then: greeting ("Dear [Name],"). Short intro paragraph. Body paragraphs (one idea each). Conclusion. Sign off ("Yours Sincerely," / "Love from,") → new line → signature.
What are the 4 parts of a review's structure?
Review
1. Introduction — name the product, brief description, hold off opinion. 2. Description — background, when made, by who, what it's about. 3. Judgement — pros/cons, your opinion. 4. Details — descriptive, feelings, thoughts. Goal: convince the reader to agree with your opinion.
