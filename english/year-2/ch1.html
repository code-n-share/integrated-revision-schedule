📖 Key Terms at a Glance
TermMeaning (plain English)
Personal essayAn essay in the first person about your own memories or events, reflecting on your thoughts, feelings and emotions.
AnecdoteA short story about something that happened. It is used to open the essay.
ObservationSomething you noticed. This is the other way to open the essay.
ReflectThink about what an event means and its implications, not just what happened.
First personWriting as "I".
Narrative styleTelling events as a story, in sequence.
ConclusionWhat has changed and what you learned. It refers back to the opening.
🏗️ Basic Structure of a Personal Essay
Personal essay structure: open with an anecdote or observation, reflect on it, keep reflecting (or add new anecdotes), draw conclusions, then refer back to the opening.
💡 It is like telling a friend a story, then explaining why it stuck with you, and ending by going back to where you started.
  1. Open with an anecdote or observation (1–2 paragraphs).
  2. Reflect on the anecdote and its implications. Depending on the topic, get a bit broader (1–2 paragraphs).
  3. Continue reflecting or bring in new anecdotes to reflect on.
  4. Draw conclusions: what has changed, what you learned.
  5. Refer back to the opening. Address it now that you have reflected.
🧩 Remember it: O-R-C-C-R – Open, Reflect, Continue, Conclude, Return (to the start).
⚠️ Common mistake: only telling the story. The reflection (what it means, what you learned) is the heart of the essay, and the ending must go back to the opening.

❓ Quick Quiz

Which is the best way to open a personal essay?

  1. A summary of the whole essay
  2. A list of your hobbies
  3. An anecdote or observation
  4. A quotation from a famous author
The notes say to open with an anecdote or observation (1–2 paragraphs). A summary gives away the essay and a quote isn't personal.

What should you do straight after the opening anecdote?

  1. Draw your conclusions
  2. Reflect on it and its implications
  3. Start a new essay topic
  4. Retell the same story
Reflection comes straight after the opening. Conclusions come later.

Which is NOT a feature of a personal essay?

  1. Written in first person
  2. Events told in sequence
  3. Reflective
  4. Written in third person, with no feelings
A personal essay is in first person and describes thoughts, feelings and emotions.

How should the essay end?

  1. By referring back to the opening
  2. By introducing a brand new anecdote
  3. By repeating the title
  4. By asking the reader a question
The last step is to refer back to the opening now that you have reflected.

What belongs in the conclusion?

  1. The plot of the story again
  2. Only descriptions of places
  3. What has changed and what you learned
  4. A list of features
Conclusions answer "what's changed, what did I learn?".

Which set describes the language of a personal essay?

  1. Formal, technical, distant
  2. Honest, descriptive, personal
  3. Persuasive, aggressive, short
  4. Humorous, vague, impersonal
Honest, descriptive and personal are the three qualities in the notes.
✏️ Practice Questions
Outline the basic structure of a personal essay. (5 marks)
  1. Open with an anecdote or observation (1–2 paragraphs).
  2. Reflect on the anecdote and its implications; get a bit broader depending on the topic (1–2 paragraphs).
  3. Continue reflecting or bring in new anecdotes to reflect on.
  4. Draw conclusions: what has changed and what you learned.
  5. Refer back to the opening and address it now that you have reflected.
List the features of a personal essay. (4 marks)
Any four or more: first person; deals with memories or events; describes thoughts, feelings and emotions; narrative style; events told in sequence; informative and detailed; reflective.
Describe the language you should use in a personal essay and say why. (3 marks)
Honest, descriptive and personal. The essay is about your own memories, thoughts and feelings, so honest and personal language makes it believable, and descriptive language lets the reader see and feel what you did.
Plan a personal essay (headings only) on a time something changed how you think. (5 marks)
Open: one anecdote of the moment. Reflect: what it meant and its implications, then a little broader. Continue: a second anecdote or further reflection. Conclude: what changed and what you learned. Refer back: return to the opening anecdote and see it differently.
