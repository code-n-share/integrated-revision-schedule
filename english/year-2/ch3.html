🧢 Ponyboy
1. Beginning of the novel: Ponyboy is very innocent and doesn't fully understand why the boys do the things they do. He just follows along and doesn't question it. He's very good at school and has a huge imagination. His parents only recently died, but it hasn't fully hit him. When Darry steps up to become a "father figure", Ponyboy is confused why he acts that way and wants his "old brother" back. He still believes Socs and Greasers can never be the same.
💡 Quote: "We get jumped by the Socs… abbreviation for Socials, the jet set, the West-side rich kids… like the term 'greaser', which is used to class all us boys on the East Side."
2. Key events
  • Chat with Cherry at the drive-in changes his view of the Socs: "Things are rough all over."
  • Darry slaps him out of frustration and he runs away: shows how broken their relationship is at the start.
  • Johnny kills Bob to save him; this changes the whole story. "I had to. They were drowning you, Pony." + "I killed him… I killed that boy."
  • Hair cut and bleached: he feels a part of his identity has gone.
  • Saves the children from the burning church: shows bravery and courage.
  • Talk with Randy: changes his opinion of Socs "just being stuck-up with no feelings".
  • Talk with Cherry: he now realises things are rough all over.
  • Johnny's death: he's confused, doesn't fully digest what happened and what Johnny said: "Stay gold, Ponyboy. Stay gold…"
  • Socs confront him but he feels nothing and responds by threatening them: a massive change in personality.
3. End of the novel: Ponyboy has changed a lot but still has bits of the beginning Ponyboy in him. Due to his head injury he's clumsy, forgetful and his grades are tanking. He's no longer afraid of what might happen and has lost a lot of his innocence. He might be going through depression because he's lost both his friends in the span of one day. He's learnt that things might not always be as they seem and has a deeper understanding of what's going on around him.
4. Relationships
WithWhat it shows
DarryPonyboy thinks he's too strict or doesn't understand him; shows how sensitive Ponyboy is and how he misunderstands people who care. Later he realises how much Darry loves him.
SodapopVery close: Soda listens and understands his feelings. Ponyboy values emotional support and needs people he can trust.
JohnnyBoth very close because they understand each other's difficult lives. Johnny's death affects him deeply: caring, loyal and emotionally sensitive.
DallySlowly realises Dally also has a sensitive side: Ponyboy is thoughtful and can look past how people act on the outside.
CherryTheir conversations help him realise Socs have problems too: he's open-minded and learns not to judge people on social class.
5. Themes
  • Identity & labels – Ponyboy learns that being a Greaser doesn't define who he is.
  • Family – he learns to understand Darry and realises how much his brothers love him.
  • Social class – he realises Socs and Greasers aren't as different as he first thought.
🧩 Remember it: B-K-E-R-T – Beginning, Key events, End, Relationships, Themes: the five headings to use for any character essay.
🔪 Johnny
1. Beginning of the novel: Johnny is shy, quiet and nervous because of the abuse he deals with at home and the trauma he has from the Socs. He lacks confidence and is quite frightened, but he's also kind, loyal and cares deeply about his friends. He sees the Greasers as his family because they make him feel safe and accepted.
2. Key events
  • Johnny gets jumped by Socs. This leaves him traumatised, which explains why he's so nervous at the start of the novel.
  • When the Socs attack Ponyboy and Johnny, Johnny uses his switchblade to kill Bob so he could protect Ponyboy: "I killed him… I killed that boy." It shows Johnny is braver than we think he is.
  • Johnny and Ponyboy rescue the children from the burning church. Johnny gets badly injured.
  • Johnny dies at the hospital after being severely injured. He dies telling Ponyboy, "Stay gold, Ponyboy. Stay gold…"
3. End of the novel: By the end, Johnny has shown how brave, selfless and caring he is. Even though he was shy and frightened at the beginning, saving the children proves he is willing to risk his life to help others. His final words, "Stay gold, Ponyboy. Stay gold…", show he is still kind and hopeful despite everything he has been through.
4. Relationships
WithWhat it shows
PonyboyBest friends who understand each other because they both have problems at home. Johnny trusts Ponyboy and feels comfortable around him: caring and loyal.
DallyJohnny looks up to him because he sees him as brave and tough. Dally deeply cares about Johnny, though he doesn't often show it.
The GreasersHis real family, because he doesn't feel loved or safe at home. Shows how much he values and is loyal to them.
His parentsA very harsh relationship: they neglect and mistreat him.
5. Themes
  • Friendship & loyalty – sees the Greasers as his real family because they care for and protect him.
  • Love & sacrifice – risks his life to save the children from the burning church: brave and selfless.
  • Violence – suffers violence at home and from the Socs, which affects his confidence and makes him afraid.
⚠️ Common mistake: describing a character only at the start or only at the end. Examiners want change: Johnny goes from shy and frightened to brave and selfless; Ponyboy from innocent and following along to changed, with a deeper understanding.
🎬 The 5 Main Key Scenes
  1. Cherry and Ponyboy talk – changes Ponyboy's view of Socs ("Things are rough all over").
  2. Bob's death – Johnny kills Bob to save Ponyboy; changes the whole story.
  3. The church fire (starred in notes *) – Ponyboy and Johnny save the children; bravery and courage.
  4. Johnny's death and Dally's death – link to themes (loyalty, love, loss).
  5. The rumble – fighting fixes nothing; Randy's talk; the Greasers win but lose Johnny and Dally; Randy quote.
🧩 Remember it: Cherry, Bob, Church, Deaths, Rumble: the order they happen in the book.
⚠️ Common mistake: picking scenes without explaining their importance. For each scene say: what happens, what it shows, a quote, and the theme.

✏️ Practice Questions
How does Ponyboy change from the beginning to the end of the novel? (8 marks)
Beginning: innocent, follows along, good at school, still believes Socs and Greasers can never be the same. Key events: Cherry ("Things are rough all over"), Darry slapping him, Bob's death, losing his hair, the church fire, Randy, Johnny's death, confronting the Socs. End: clumsy, forgetful, lost innocence, possibly depressed, but with a deeper understanding that things aren't always as they seem. Themes: identity and labels, family, social class.
Explain what Ponyboy's relationships with Darry and Cherry show about him. (6 marks)
Darry: Ponyboy thinks he's too strict or doesn't understand him, showing how sensitive he is and that he misunderstands people who care; later he realises how much Darry loves him. Cherry: her conversations help him see Socs have problems too, showing he's open-minded and learns not to judge people on social class.
Write about Johnny's character at the beginning and end of the novel. (6 marks)
Beginning: shy, quiet, nervous, lacks confidence because of abuse at home and the Socs' attack, but kind, loyal and sees the Greasers as family. End: shown to be brave, selfless and caring; saving the children proves he'll risk his life for others; his final words "Stay gold, Ponyboy. Stay gold…" show he is still kind and hopeful.
Discuss two relationships that are important to Johnny. (4 marks)
Ponyboy: best friends who understand each other because they both have problems at home; Johnny trusts him. Greasers: his real family because he doesn't feel loved or safe at home. (Also: Dally, who he looks up to; his parents, who neglect him.)
Choose a key scene and explain its importance, linking it to a theme. (6 marks)
Example – Bob's death: Johnny kills Bob with his switchblade to protect Ponyboy ("I had to. They were drowning you, Pony.") It changes the whole story and shows violence, loyalty and Johnny's courage.
Name and briefly describe the five main key scenes in order. (5 marks)
1. Cherry and Ponyboy talk (Socs have problems too). 2. Bob's death (self-defence). 3. The church fire (bravery). 4. Johnny's and Dally's deaths (themes). 5. The rumble (fighting fixes nothing; Greasers win but lose Johnny and Dally).
