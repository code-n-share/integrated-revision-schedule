🎞️ Shot Types

There are 6 basic types of shots. Know the name, what it shows, and why directors use it.

① Extreme Close-Up (ECU)
A very close shot used to draw focus to a specific detail — eyes, hands, objects.
WHY: To show a specific detail; to reveal emotion up close.
② Close-Up Shot
An object or an actor's head takes up most of the screen. Used to reveal emotion through facial expression. Best choice for shooting dialogue.
WHY: To show emotion, fear, joy, shock through facial expression.
③ Medium / Mid Shot
An actor seen from the waist up. The most common type of shot. Shows emotion through facial expression AND body language.
WHY: Most versatile — shows both face and body language.
④ Full Shot
The actor's entire body is visible, with some of the setting. Shows character emotion through posture and gesture.
WHY: To show posture, movement, character's relationship to setting.
⑤ Long Shot
Actor's entire body seen as well as some of the setting. Shows a character's emotion through posture and gesture against their environment.
WHY: To show character in context of their environment.
⑥ Extreme Long Shot (ELS)
Shows landscape — character barely visible. Makes person look small or even vulnerable. Establishes setting.
WHY: To show the vast scale of a setting; to make a character feel small/isolated.

💡 Lighting
High-Key Lighting
Bright, even illumination — creates a well-lit, upbeat, and cheerful scene.
WHY: To show happiness, safety, positivity, a welcoming environment.
Low-Key Lighting
Minimal illumination — creates a shadowy, moody, dramatic, or mysterious atmosphere.
WHY: To create tension, fear, mystery, danger.
🎨
Coco Lighting Tip In Coco, warm/colourful lighting = family and music (happiness). Lighting gets darker and cooler when there is grief, loss or separation. Music = bright.

🎨 Setting, Colour, Costume & Props
Setting
A place or environment — gives us information about the time, location, and atmosphere.
WHY: To establish context; to reflect the mood of the scene.
Costume
Carefully chosen to represent a range of things about characters — their occupation, age, status.
WHY: To show character identity; colours in costumes carry symbolic meaning.
Colour
Colours shape emotions, reinforce meaning, and often communicate things the script never explicitly says.
WHY: Orange = happiness/celebration; Blue = authority/sadness; White = innocence; Cold tones = ambiguity/grief.
Props
Objects that reveal information about character, setting and themes. Think about symbolism.
WHY: A guitar = music/freedom; letters = love/memory; a photo = connection/family.
🎬 Key Moment 1 — Opening Scene
🌅
Key Moment 1: Opening Scene
Introducing Miguel, Coco, and the family's ban on music
Shots
  • Full Body Shot → setting (poor, run-down, warm, happy, low lighting), characters: Miguel (young boy, chaotic, goofy, energetic) + Coco (old, fragile woman)
  • Zoom Shot → shows the family, warmth (setting), traditional/religious, gives hints (e.g. spirit animal)
Camera Angles
  • Low angle → music = powerful, important, meaningful, very influential
  • Eye level → Miguel + Coco — no bias, both on equal ground
Props
  • Shoes → brought family together; replacement to music; shows endurance; creates culture within the family; grounded + structure = contrast to music
Setting
  • Town → run-down, happy (colours, flowers, music), lively
  • Mexico, in the past
  • Poverty (of the times)
Lighting
  • Colourful + bright when Miguel is with family + music → warm lighting
  • When husband leaves → grief, loss, mourning
  • Lighting gets bright again → shows how their situation improves. Music = bright
Colour
  • Colours warm + happy with family
  • Colour + undertone became cold when with Ernesto (negative foreshadowing)
  • Orange → happiness, celebration, joy (positive emotions linked with orange)
  • Music makes colours brighter
Costume
  • Miguel = white tank (innocence), blue jeans (hard working)
  • Nanna = blue dress + apron (authority, working woman)
  • Imelda = flowy dress (freedom, beauty, money) → simple dress + apron (working woman, practical)
Sound / Music
  • Music = topics of love (Hector wrote them about wife/daughter), upbeat, vibrant, happy, traditional Mexican music
  • Music gets sad + slow when husband leaves
  • Stops after Imelda shuts the door (only sound is door closing) → shows they're shutting music out of their life
  • Dialogue: Miguel (narrator) — colloquial language (everyday language) to give further insight into his emotions
🎬 Key Moment 2 — The Ending / Climax
🎵
Key Moment 2: The Ending
Miguel sings "Remember Me" to Mama Coco — Hector is saved
Shots
  • Close-Up Shot → shows emotion on Miguel + Mama Coco's faces (happy tears, love, happiness, relief)
  • Mid Shot → Miguel + Mama Coco show emotion + body language (Miguel plays guitar)
Camera Angles
  • High angle on M. + MC hugging + family → shows setting, as if Hector is looking down on them
  • Low angle on MC → power to save Hector if she remembers
  • High angle on M. → powerless → needs MC to remember Hector, he can't save him
Costume
  • MC → scarf (comfort, orange = happiness + remembrance)
  • Grandmother → blue dress (authority)
  • Miguel → white tank (innocence)
Colours
  • Before he gets to MC → cold, shadows, blue/dark (ambiguity), shadows when there's no music
  • Orange, warm, yellow → when he is with MC (happiness, bravery, joy)
Setting
  • Hints of poverty, decorations, Mexico
  • Empty (early morning) → new beginning, something is about to begin
Props
  • Guitar → music, freedom, innocence (white)
  • Hector's letters → love, adoration
  • Hector's photo → love, adoration, unity of family (physical photo, after life)
Sound / Music
  • Music fast, tense, hectic (shows tension, urgency)
  • Music stops when he reaches MC (reached destination)
  • Music soft + sad, then happy (reflects the emotions of Miguel)
  • Music throughout movie reflects how Miguel feels
✍️ Example PQE Answer (20 marks — exam style)
Intro: "From studying Lee Unkrich's musical movie 'Coco', the climax of the movie, where Miguel tries to help his great-grandma Coco remember her father, Hector, is a powerful moment that shows the importance of family love."

Point: Music contributes to making this scene powerful.
Quote: We see this when Miguel sings "Remember Me" to his great-grandma Coco, to try to help her remember her father, Hector, before he disappears forever.
Explanation: This quote shows how much music contributes to the scene, as it reflects the important message of remembering people you've lost, and the power of family love. It creates a heart-warming atmosphere that evokes strong emotions from the viewer.

Point 2: Two powerful angles that help create a strong atmosphere in this scene are the low angle and the high angle.
Quote/Detail: The low angle is used on Mama Coco to show that she has the power in this moment, because it's up to her to remember Hector and save him. In contrast, the high angle on Miguel shows how powerless he feels, as all he can do is hope that she remembers her papa.
Explanation: These angles make the atmosphere tense and full of emotions, showing how important this moment is and how Hector's fate depends on Mama Coco's memory.
🃏 Flashcard Drill
Name the 6 shot types in order from closest to furthest.
Shots
1. Extreme Close-Up → 2. Close-Up → 3. Medium Shot → 4. Full Shot → 5. Long Shot → 6. Extreme Long Shot.
What does a LOW ANGLE shot show? What does a HIGH ANGLE shot show?
Angles
Low angle = camera below subject, looking up → subject appears LARGE, POWERFUL, IMPOSING.
High angle = camera above subject, looking down → subject appears SMALL, WEAK, POWERLESS.
In Key Moment 2 (the ending), how are angles used on Miguel vs Mama Coco?
Coco KM2
Mama Coco: LOW ANGLE → she has the power (it's up to her to remember Hector and save him).
Miguel: HIGH ANGLE → he is powerless, all he can do is hope she remembers. The contrast creates a tense, emotional atmosphere.
What does the colour ORANGE symbolise in Coco? What does cold/dark lighting indicate?
Colour
Orange = happiness, celebration, joy, remembrance, bravery. Present when Miguel is with Mama Coco or when there is music.
Cold/dark colours = ambiguity, grief, loss, mourning. Present when there is no music or when separation occurs.
What is the name of the film and director you studied? Why is music significant in the film?
Coco Overview
Film: "Coco" (2017) — Director: Lee Unkrich.
Music is central to the film's message — it represents love, family, memory and freedom. When music plays, lighting becomes brighter and colours warmer. When it is silenced (Imelda shuts the door), it symbolises the family shutting music out of their lives. Music throughout the movie reflects how Miguel feels.
